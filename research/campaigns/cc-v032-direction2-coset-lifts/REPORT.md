# cc-v032-direction2-coset-lifts: coset lifted products over small nonabelian groups, distance 2 at one-row bases and at most 4 at two-row bases

## What was asked

Issue #7377, direction 2: enumerate `lift_{G;H}(A, B)` (code-calculus v0.3.2,
`L-LIFTED` with a subgroup) with nontrivial `H <= G` of index 2 to 8, and ask
whether the coset lifts reach codes the regular lifts over groups of the same
block count do not. Definition and runner: `research/campaigns/cc-v032-direction2-coset-lifts/run.py`.

## What was spent

Everything ran single-threaded on one laptop core. The committed phase A pass
(nauty-certificate class keys, 1x2 and 1x3 bases) took 3,014 s; the three
superseded passes it replaced (display-keyed, 4,049 s; full-row WL keyed,
3,804 s; the 2x2 display-keyed pass, 128 s) took 7,981 s. The gate over the
295 committed classes took 8,282 s (28 s per class on average, 39 s at most),
and the gate runs of the superseded passes 12,160 s (155 + 240 classes at 1x2
and 1x3, 240 at 2x2). About 8.7 CPU-hours in all. Comparators: 518,397 regular
lifts over the abelian groups of order 2 to 8 and over S3, D4, and Q8, in
3,214 connected classes.

## What ran

`run.py` against the code-calculus v0.3.2 release (github.com/unitaryfoundation/code-calculus @
474104a257f79019a61bb3e8d9c7ae25e43047a6) and this repository's verifier at
e43a53888, on one laptop (18 cores). Seeds: `--seed 7377` for every sampled
sweep and the witness searches. Gate: `research/kit/coordination.gate_and_record`
on each staged representative (the trusted `verify/validate_candidate.py`).

## What was tried

The base matrices are 1xn group-algebra protographs (single elements and sums
of two, the first entry of A and of B normalized to contain the identity), not
board codes as outer and inner factors as the direction's text proposes; that
substitution is listed under what the next searcher should know. For every
group, every subgroup generated by one or two elements, one per conjugacy
class, of index 2 to 8. The 1x2 sweep is exhaustive where the pair count is at
most 100,000 (22 of 47 subgroups) and a 100,000-pair sample otherwise; the
1x3 sweep is a 2,000-pair sample per subgroup. A class is "new" when no
regular lift of the same block count (over any abelian group of that order,
or S3, D4, Q8) has its nauty certificate. The 1x2 comparator is exhaustive;
the 1x3 comparator is a sample, so its "new" counts are upper bounds (the 75
normal-H classes it calls new are the proof: a normal H gives the quotient
lift, which the comparator holds).

## Survivors

All 295 classes pass the gate; every one has distance 2 (RIS at 20,000
trials per side; nothing was refuted). The gate labels five of them
board-advancing in the weight-8 unrestricted cell, because no entry there has
n no larger, k no smaller, and w no larger:

- `[[40,24,2]]` w=8, D4/H of order 2 (index 4, non-normal), base 1x3, class `3be74612be706b1c`
- `[[40,24,2]]` w=8, D4/H of order 2 (index 4, non-normal), base 1x3, class `46b13d629ee163c6`
- `[[80,46,2]]` w=8, D8/H of order 2 (index 8, normal), base 1x3, class `37a7a676e49b5cbd`
- `[[80,46,2]]` w=8, D8/H of order 2 (index 8, normal), base 1x3, class `4f7780e9d769a3b0`
- `[[80,46,2]]` w=8, D8/H of order 2 (index 8, normal), base 1x3, class `b7951529c31a4bc0`

They are d = 2 codes at k/n of 0.58 to 0.60 and are staged, not filed. Whether
a d = 2 record belongs on the board is a maintainer's decision; the gate's
rule says they advance it. The other 290 classes are dominated, mostly by
entries at the same n with larger d.

The 2x2 bases (240 classes, an earlier display-keyed pass whose class counts
are superseded but whose gated codes stand): 219 at d = 2, 15 at d = 3, 6 at
d = 4 ([[64,4,4]] w=6 twice, [[64,2,4]] w=6 and w=7, [[32,2,4]] w=6,
[[64,8,4]] w=6), none board-advancing (`result-gated-2x2.json`).

## Dead ends

- A normal subgroup H gives nothing: the coset lift over G/H with the left
  action of A and the right action of B restricted to N_G(H) = G is the
  regular lift over the quotient group, row for row (checked at the matrix
  level on S3/Z3 and D4/Z2 and confirmed by the exhaustive 1x2 comparison: 0
  new connected classes for every normal H in every group).
- The first phase A pass keyed classes on the library's retained display
  (`lifted_product` keeps the first independent rows), and the WL signature
  of that display depends on which rows were kept, so the quotient lifts of
  normal subgroups came out "new" 3,380 times. The fingerprint (a row-space
  invariant) does not have this problem; the WL signature does. Keying on the
  WL hash of the full generated rows then merged distinct codes (16 classes
  where nauty finds 107 on 300 random Z8 lifts), so the committed pass keys on
  the nauty certificate of the typed Tanner graph of the full generated rows.
- Every one-row class has d = 2. With entries of at most two group elements a
  1xn base gives checks of weight at most 2n + 2 and a weight-2 logical on
  every class tried; the 2x2 bases reach d = 4 at best, below the board.

## What the next searcher should know

- The direction's own proposal, board codes as the outer and inner factors of
  a coset lift, was not run; this campaign answers the fingerprint question at
  the protograph level only. A rerun with board entries as A and B is the
  natural next step and will cost more per class (n grows as [G:H] times the
  factor sizes).
- Reaching d >= 3 at one-row bases needs entries of three or more group
  elements; the 2x2 results say two-row bases get to d = 4 and no further at
  these orders.
- Non-normal H at index above 8 was excluded by the index cap; S4 has
  subgroups of index 12 and 24 that the cap removed.
- For the library: a consumer that compares coset lifts across quotients
  should key on the full generated rows or on a row-space invariant, not on
  the retained display's WL signature; `machine.ingestion.ingest` gives a
  canonical display that avoids the order dependence but still drops rows.
