Direction & hypothesis

arXiv:2610.13880 (Gideon Lee, "Bicycle Race: small two-block codes on the Pareto frontier", 9 October 2026) enumerates about 10^8 two-block group-algebra codes C(a,b) of check weight five to nine over groups of order at most 160 (n <= 320), with exact distances, and publishes a database of 12,448 of them (github.com/gideonlsx/bicycle-race @ aed705a, file data/codes.jsonl, CC BY 4.0), each with a witness logical. The question here is narrow: which of the codes the paper tabulates (Tables 2, 3, 9 and 11, and the ones named in Sec. 3) advance the board's four-axis frontier (n, k, d, check weight) and are not already on it? This entry is one of the 28 that do.

What was searched

  • Screen: the 45 distinct parameter triples in those tables were compared

    • with every CSS entry on main under the site's Pareto rule (an entry dominates when n <=, k >=, d >=, w <= with at least one strict), at the paper's check weight. Thirteen are already on the board at the same or a lower weight ([[60,4,8]], [[64,18,8]], [[84,14,10]], [[90,8,10]], [[90,12,9]], [[102,20,10]], [[144,12,12]], [[180,8,16]], [[182,6,18]], [[186,10,14]], [[192,8,16]], [[288,12,18]] and [[288,16,16]]); four are dominated by board entries ([[168,12,12]] by [[144,12,12]], [[198,8,16]] by [[180,8,16]], [[200,16,12]] by [[192,16,12]], and [[216,4,16]] by [[192,4,16]]); Table 9's [[192,12,14]] and [[192,16,12]] are Lin-Pryadko archive codes, not in the paper's database, and already on the board. The remaining 28 are new nondominated points and are filed one per PR.

  • This code: record 23f7085fc401fb11 of the database (Table 2 of the paper;

    • database labels standout, front, front-2D), over the abelian group C45 x C3 (GAP SmallGroup(135,2)), check weight 9 (block weights 6 + 3): a = 1 + x^6 y^2 + x^10 + x^25 y^2 + x^27 + x^40 y, b = 1 + x^3 y + x^36 in generators x (order 45) and y (order 3), i.e. the bivariate bicycle code with A = a(x,y), B = b(x,y). The stored matrices use the qubit ordering of the paper's database (record 23f7085fc401fb11, github.com/gideonlsx/bicycle-race @ aed705a, data/codes.jsonl, CC BY 4.0): qubits 0..134 are the left block and 135..269 the right block, group elements numbered as GAP's Elements(SmallGroup(135,2)) with e0 the identity, x = e7, y = e2, so a = e0 + e1 + e12 + e20 + e65 + e74, b = e0 + e3 + e76.

  • Rebuild: the generators' right-multiplication permutations in the

    • database's data/groups.jsonl give the multiplication table; then H_X = (L(a) | R(b)), H_Z = (R(b)^T | L(a)^T) with qubits 0..|G|-1 the left block. A short standalone NumPy script did this and checked CSS commutation, k = n - rank H_X - rank H_Z = 72, max check weight 9, and both of the paper's witnesses (weight 6 X, weight 6 Z, in the kernel of the other side's checks and outside its own stabilizer row space) before anything was submitted.

  • Distance: the paper states d = 6 exactly. Its record: d_X "ladder in the screen (frontier mode, exact_on_accept; cluster engine, symmetry roots)";

    • d_Z "ladder in the screen (frontier mode, exact_on_accept; cluster engine, symmetry roots)"; verification "one engine". Here, qldpc submit's own search (20,000 NumPy RIS trials per side, then a 2,000,000-trial accelerator pass) read d_X <= 6 and d_Z <= 6, and the verifier's refutation search found nothing lighter than 6.
