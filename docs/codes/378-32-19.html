Direction & hypothesis

Target cell: weight-8 x unrestricted (CSS), which is where the verifier places a check weight of 8. The source is arXiv:2608.08996 (multi-agent discovery of practical qLDPC codes), whose Table 1 lists 20 parameter-frontier discoveries and Table 2 a further 7 structurally distinct ones. Eight of those codes are already entries on this board, all citing the paper, but this one was missed: no entry shares (n, k) = (378, 32), nothing dominates it at n <= 378, k >= 32, d >= 19, w <= 8, and kd^2/n = 30.56. The paper also reports the distance as exact rather than an upper bound, which is unusual in its table and worth having on the board.

What was searched

No search. This is a reproduction of a published instance, and every invariant the paper states reproduces exactly: n = 378, k = 32 by GF(2) rank, check weight w = 8, H_X H_Z^T = 0 over GF(2), and d = 19 on both Pauli sides. The construction comes from the paper's Supplementary Information, which sits at the end of main.tex after the References and so is invisible to a plain text search of the article body: G = He(Z_3) x Z_7 with K = {e} (free action) and 1x1/1x1 boundary maps, so the coset-orbit balanced product reduces to the ordinary lifted product over G, i.e. the two-block group-algebra form already in the kit. A first attempt at the Heisenberg Cayley table put the product's index in the column slot instead of the right operand's index; the symptom was k = 15 against the paper's 32 together with a CSS failure, caught by an identity assertion added to the builder.
