[[514,32,62]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 257

At row weight 12 or lighter, the best distance on the board with n <= 514 and k >= 32 is 30, at [[390,32,30]] and row weight 10. This entry reaches 62. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same n and k range is 64, at [[514,64,64]] and row weight 20.

Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on x^m - 1, k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1), so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor g of chosen degree and draw a and b as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor t of m gives a quotient-fold ceiling of (m/t) d_t, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension deg g carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 16 sits where both are weak here.

What was searched

m = 257 with ord_2(257) = 16, so the cyclotomic cosets of x^257 - 1 over GF(2) number 17 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 1 coset of total degree 16, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. a and b are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

Draws with two even weights are rejected: (x + 1) divides a binary polynomial exactly when its weight is even, so two even weights would add the root-1 coset to the common gcd and land the draw one degree above the target. Here a has weight 5 and b has weight 7.

Evidence trail

deg gcd(a, x^257 - 1) = 16, deg gcd(b, x^257 - 1) = 16 and deg gcd(a, b, x^257 - 1) = 16, so the leak between the single-block kernels and k is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 62. The per-seed spread: 2 seeds found 62, 1 seed found 64, 5 seeds found 66, 7 seeds found 68, 7 seeds found 70, 2 seeds found 72. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 16 whose zeros are the 241 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 89, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 90 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 80. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, weigh 62, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space.
