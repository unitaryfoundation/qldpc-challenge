[[480,16,26]]: weight-6 two-block group algebra code over C12 x Dic5

Direction & hypothesis

Cell: check weight at most 6, unrestricted locality (no layout submitted). The leaders of this cell are two-block group algebra (2BGA) codes over non-abelian groups with a large centre, among them 960-24-38 and 720-16-36 over C_m x Dic5. Hypothesis: k of C(G;a,b) can be read off from the blocks of F_2[G], and choosing a and b so that k is carried by a single block, in which the two norms meet in a curvilinear point, gives connected codes with large distance. For G = C_m x Dic5 with m = 3 x 2^e the subgroup A = <z, x, y^2> is abelian of index 2, its 2-part A_2 is central, and its odd part <t> is cyclic of order 15 (t = z^4 x for m = 12). A Galois orbit of characters of order 15 of <t> has the block M_2(F_4[A_2]); there a and b act by 2 x 2 Clifford symbols over the local ring O = F_4[A_2], and if both residues have rank at least 1 the orbit contributes 4 dim O/(N(a), N(b)) to k, where N(a), N(b) are the determinants (norms in F_2[A]).

What was searched

Normal forms a = 1 + u + y v, b = 1 + u' + v' y with u, v, u', v' in A (every weight-3 element with two terms in one coset of A has this form up to two-sided translations, which give equivalent codes). At an order-15 character the residue of N(a) vanishes for exactly one value of the C3-part of v per value of the odd part of u != 1, so the pairs that carry k at a given orbit can be listed exactly. We enumerated all normal-form pairs on C_m x D5 and C_m x Dic5, m = 6, 12, 24, by the ideal (N(a), N(b)) at each orbit. On C12 x Dic5 the class with contact length 4 at one fixed order-15 orbit and nothing elsewhere has 78848 pairs (as many again for the other orbit), all with k = 16. We drew 320 of them uniformly and screened each (upper bounds only) by an information-set search with 1e3 trials per side plus searches restricted to words invariant under central subgroups; for comparison 80 uniform weight-3 pairs with k = 16 on the same group and 60 pairs carried by the order-3 characters. In all, 1420 screened codes on six groups, 47 CPU-minutes.

Related work: the board contributor @MicheldeMessieres reports publicly, in notes/720-16-36.md, an exhaustive census, up to equivalence, of the weight-6 (3+3) 2BGA codes over C_m x H for H among small non-abelian groups including Dic5, for group orders up to 480, in the same convention and indexing; only its list of codes is not published. C12 x Dic5 has order 240, so that census very likely contains this code; it was not submitted or described there. We found the code independently, by the design above (a single carrying orbit of order 15, the curvilinear point Spec F_4[eps]/(eps^4)); our contribution is its identification and the exact distance.

Evidence trail

  • Screening: 10 of the 320 designed pairs reached 26 or more (one of them 28, lowered to 26 by a deeper search), and all ten are this code (canonical forms of the Tanner graphs); the best uniform pair with k = 16 reached 20, the best order-3 pair 16.
  • Construction: provenance.construction; rank H_X = rank H_Z = 232, k = 16, every column of weight 3, Tanner graph connected; rebuilt independently with a Python group law and with Magma from the presentation (SmallGroup(240,40)).
  • Representation theory (computed, Propositions 3 to 5 of the README of the package below): all 16 logical qubits lie in the block of the factor t^4 + t + 1 of t^15 + 1; there the residues of both Clifford symbols have rank 1 and O/(N(a), N(b)) has length 4 and Hilbert function (1,1,1,1), so it is F_4[eps]/(eps^4) over the residue field, and k = 2 x 2 x 4. The Brauer sandwich gives k_ss/k/k_C = 4/16/32: two simple modules of dimension 2 with kappa(a) = kappa(b) = 1 and Cartan entries 8 = |A_2|; the semisimple part carries 4 logical qubits and the central defect group the other 12. Along the 2-tower, the same supports over C_m x Dic5 give k = 4, 8, 16, 16 for m = 3, 6, 12, 24.
  • Upper bound: an information-set search with 2e4 trials per side found words of weight 26 on both sides (31 and 16 distinct ones) and nothing lighter; the entry carries one per side.
  • Lower bound: all rows of H_X sum to the all-one vector, and so do those of H_Z, so every logical has even weight and it suffices to exclude weight <= 24. Two independently written exhaustive programs (exactA, exactB), anchored at the orbits of the Tanner-graph automorphism group (order 240, two qubit orbits), find no nontrivial logical of weight <= 24 on either side, in two sets of runs on two instances whose groups were computed independently (nauty, Magma); nodes and CPU seconds for the sides Z / X:
  • first set (W = 25, searched 24), one anchor per orbit, earlier orbits excluded: exactA 1.06e8 / 1.05e8, 314 / 366 s; exactB 1.10e8 / 1.09e8, 349 / 402 s
  • second set (W = 24) on a relabelled instance: exactA with pattern classes of the single check orbit, 3.00e7 / 2.82e7, 50 / 47 s; exactB with the orbits in the other order, 1.22e8 / 1.19e8, 269 / 261 s
  • tropidist b03ec2d4077f (the board's encoding, 16 threads per side, on a qubit relabelling of this entry) certified d_X = d_Z = 26 exactly, in 5185 s and 5474 s wall time, with a returned vector of weight 26 on each side; an earlier single-threaded attempt (1200 s per side) had reached only d >= 22. Records, inputs, the permutation and a checker that it maps the rows of H_X and H_Z of this entry onto the inputs: github.com/TimoKellerMath/quantum-error-correction @ 77f905b6e8dd, two-block-and-amplified-codes/logs/trop480/.
  • Final claim: d_X = d_Z = 26 exactly, by three independent programs (exactA and exactB: Theorem 2 of the README, completeness proofs of both programs in their notes there; tropidist). certs/480-16-26.json is the tropidist certificate.

Dead ends

  • At equal k the design is not markedly better than uniform sampling: on C24 x Dic5 (n = 960, k = 24) 15 of 60 designed and 10 of 30 uniform pairs reached kd^2/n >= 24 in screening, all of them the board's 960-24-38 or one dominated second code. For pairs with two terms in one coset of A, k >= 16 already forces a single carrying orbit, so uniform pairs at equal k mostly are designed pairs. The design saves rank computations (a factor of about 70 at k = 16), not distance.
  • Contact schemes that are not curvilinear (Hilbert function (1,2,1)) gave at most 12 at n = 480 (18 codes); contact length 8 (k = 32) at most 12 at n = 480 and 20 at n = 960; carrying k at both order-15 orbits forces a and b into a subgroup of index 5, hence disconnected codes; the cyclic 2-part of C24 x D5 (n = 480, k = 16) gave at most 20 in 160 codes.
  • A double cover over C24 x Dic5 found in the screening (n = 960, k = 16): the lightest words found are the pulled-back words of weight 52; its distance is open.
  • Larger residue fields by uniform sampling: on C12 x Dic7 (n = 672) the connected codes with k = 16 reached at most 14, on C4 x Dic15 (n = 480) at most 12, and on C20 x Dic5 (n = 800) only 0.6 % of 5000 pairs had k > 0.
  • Literature checked without a [[480,16,d >= 26]] code or a code over C12 x Dic5: Lin and Pryadko (arXiv:2306.16400), G. Lee (arXiv:2610.13880), Aydin, Tamo and Barg (arXiv:2606.17268), Rajpurohit, Lalitha and Garani (arXiv:2610.12782), arXiv:2606.24808, arXiv:2608.08996, arXiv:2608.12509, the Error Correction Zoo; QECDB was not checked.

Tools

Claude Opus 5.5 (Claude Code); Python and Magma for the group algebra, the blocks, the census, the Brauer sandwich and the equivalence tests (with nauty); C for the screening and for the two exact programs, exactA and exactB, which generalize the two programs of weight6-exact-distances (github.com/TimoKellerMath/quantum-error-correction @ 2be467a7bd36) to any check weight and any group of qubit permutations, with completeness proofs in their notes; tropidist (Cheng, Guo, Pan, arXiv:2611.08247). About 2.7 CPU-hours in total: design, census and screening 1.4, exact searches 0.6, tropidist 0.7.
