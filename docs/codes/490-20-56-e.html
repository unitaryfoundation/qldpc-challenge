Direction & hypothesis

The board already holds this exact point four times, at row weights 32, 24, 28 and 16 (codes/490-20-56.json, codes/490-20-56-b.json, codes/490-20-56-c.json, codes/490-20-56-d.json). Row weight is a board axis, so a lighter check matrix at the same n, k and d is an advance with nothing else attached, and the gap between 16 and the family's reach looked worth one sweep. The ring size is m = 245, which is divisible by 5 and 7 and so admits the residue construction that fixes k without constructing the gcd.

What was searched

m = 245 was picked by replacing a modulus-selection metric that had been driving this search wrongly. The earlier metric priced each modulus by a score ceiling k(2m/q)^2/n against the best kd^2/n in its blocklength bucket; that ceiling rests on the quotient-norm lift cap, which the symmetric fold described below refutes, and the comparison treats the board as a score table when it is a Pareto front over n, k, d and w. Repriced by how many undominated candidates a modulus still yields, m = 245 was untried, so four cores were pointed at it.

The sweep draws sparse polynomial pairs on x^245 - 1, computes k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1) by Euclid so most draws die before any matrix work, and prices survivors against the quotient-norm lift, the single-block ideal of (x^m - 1)/gcd(a, x^m - 1), and the symmetric fold. The first 2,367 distinct pairs from m = 245 and m = 175 gave 2,205 undominated at their screened distance, collapsing to 9 Pareto-distinct points.

The symmetric fold

For any partition of the n qubits into equal-size orbits, the vectors constant on orbits are u = O v, so H_Z u = 0 reduces to (H_Z O) v = 0 on n/r columns, and the lifted weight is r wt(v). Each partition therefore bounds the distance on a code a factor r smaller. Two conditions matter: the small code is handed to a distance search rather than having combinations of orbit basis vectors enumerated, since the interesting folded logicals are not sparse in that basis; and a lifted witness counts only if it both commutes and escapes the rowspace of the same-type checks, because a logical of the folded code can lift to a stabilizer of the original. Checking commutation alone reports weight-4 logicals on the toric codes codes/36-2-6.json and codes/64-2-8.json, whose distances are 6 and 8; with both conditions it returns 6 and 8 exactly.

This matters because random information-set decoding is close to blind to these logicals rather than merely slow at finding them: the invariant subspace is not a positive-measure slice of the information sets it samples.

Evidence trail

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound.

symmetric fold, every partition to 160 cols 56 (tight: matches the witness) random information set, 24 seeds x 2e6 66 (running minimum at 8 seeds) verify/validate_candidate.py passed, not refuted, no d-only peers

The fold is tight here, finding a weight-56 logical of its own rather than merely failing to beat the claim, and the trial-based search stays well above it.

Dead ends

Of the 9 Pareto-distinct points from these two moduli, 7 were refuted by the fold, including five at a claimed [[490,20,70]] scoring 200.0 whose true bound is 42 to 56. The refuted set is dominated by claims sitting on the quotient-norm lift cap 2m/q, which is a structural signature: a claim at the cap means the lift found its own best witness and stopped, and a fold at a divisor of m beats it.

The second survivor shows the fold is not sufficient alone. A candidate screened at [[350,20,40]] cleared the fold at exactly 40 and then yielded a weight-38 witness under the confirmation search, so it is [[350,20,38]] and is being prepared separately. Neither instrument subsumes the other: the fold reaches logicals that random information sets structurally cannot, and the confirmation search reaches logicals that are not constant on any equal-orbit partition.
