construction Z4-invariant (quasi-cyclic) non-CSS stabilizer code: 16 qubits in 4 orbits of Z4 (qubit 4*b+g, b = orbit, g in Z4), 12 generators in 3 orbits of Z4 (each orbit = the 4 translates g -> g+t of one representative Pauli), every generator of weight <= 5. Found by a lazy SAT search over such codes (commutation encoded exactly; every nontrivial logical of weight < 4 found along the way is excluded together with its Z4 translates, then re-solved), related to the group-based (coset / Cayley-graph) constructions of non-CSS stabilizer LDPC codes of Camara, Ollivier and Tillich (arXiv:quant-ph/0502086). Orbit representatives: X0 Z1 Z3 Y4 Z11; X7 Y10 Y12 X13 Z14; Y2 X7 X8 X9 Y12. k = 16 - 12 = 4 (independent generators). Not CSS under any single-qubit Clifford.

[[16,4,4]] — weight-5 Z4-invariant non-CSS stabilizer code

Direction & hypothesis

Among the general-stabilizer board's entries with generator weight <= 5, the best kd^2/n was 2.778 when this search started (codes/18-2-5-s.json, @FarLab). Weight <= 5 is our own filter, not a board track; in the board's weight-6 stabilizer cell this entry does not lead. Wei, Han, He, Li and Liu (arXiv:2601.19848) tabulate the lowest possible generator weight for small stabilizer codes (n <= 12). The hypothesis: small codes whose generators are the translates of a few Pauli strings under a small abelian group (we call them quasi-cyclic; the idea of building non-CSS stabilizer LDPC codes from finite groups goes back to the group-based (coset / Cayley-graph) constructions of non-CSS stabilizer LDPC codes of Camara, Ollivier and Tillich (arXiv:quant-ph/0502086)) leave a SAT solver few enough unknowns to decide directly whether weight 5 allows d = 4 at rate 1/4, which would give kd^2/n = 4.

Also builds on: the low-weight framing and the small-code tables of Wei, Han, He, Li and Liu (arXiv:2601.19848), and the group-based (coset / Cayley-graph) constructions of non-CSS stabilizer LDPC codes of Camara, Ollivier and Tillich (arXiv:quant-ph/0502086).

Board precedents: translate-of-a-Pauli (cyclic) stabilizer codes are already on the board from @FarLab (codes/18-2-5-s.json and siblings codes/20-2-6-s.json, codes/34-2-7-s.json); this entry uses several orbits under Z4 instead of one. SAT-based discovery of small codes is already on the board from @MathysRennela, including a CSS [[16,4,4]] of weight 6 (codes/16-4-4.json); this entry is a different, non-CSS code (hence the -s slug, as in @FarLab's codes/18-2-5-s.json).

Our contribution: the Z4-invariant lazy SAT search that found these generators, and the exact distance checks below.

Frontier: Dominates codes/48-4-4.json ([[48,4,4]], weight 5, @cbjuan) on n; not dominated by any entry in the cell. This is not a claim to lead the cell: many weight-6 stabilizer entries score higher than 4.0.

What was searched

  • Shape (b, c) = number of qubit orbits b and generator orbits c under the group G, so n = b|G| and there are c|G| generators. Here G = Z4, (b, c) = (4, 3): n = 16, 12 generators, k >= 4, generator weight <= 5. Unknowns are the Pauli letters of the 3 orbit representatives; commutation of every pair of orbits is encoded exactly (XOR constraints, CaDiCaL 1.9.5 through PySAT, seed 0).
  • Distance >= 4 is imposed lazily: solve, search the solution exactly for a nontrivial logical of weight < 4, exclude it and its Z4 translates from being undetectable, solve again. This run took 282 rounds and 64 s on one thread.
  • The same search in the same session: 0.6 CPU-hours up to the find; about 4.4 CPU-hours for the whole session including the dead ends listed below (23 SAT runs, from runs of 0 s to 1250 s). Found at weight 5: [[9,3,3]] (Z3), [[12,4,3]] (Z4), and after this find [[20,5,4]] (Z5, also kd^2/n = 4.0; not part of this PR). No code of the given shape: [[8,3,3]] (no symmetry), [[10,4,3]] (Z2), [[12,6,3]] (Z3, Z2), [[11,5,3]] (no symmetry). Undecided within 900-1250 s: [[12,3,4]] (Z3), [[12,4,4]] (Z4), [[16,4,5]] (Z4) and seven larger targets.
  • Earlier, our sweep of @FarLab's one-block cyclic family, widened to Z_a x Z_b (all translates of a single weight-5 Pauli, n <= 38; a few minutes of CPU), found nothing above 2.78 at weight 5; it rediscovered codes/18-2-5-s.json.

Evidence trail

Exact distance for these generators: an exhaustive enumeration of every Pauli of weight 1, 2 and 3 (16,248 operators) finds no nontrivial logical; at weight 4 there are 89 nontrivial logicals, among them the stored witness (X on 0, 1, 2, 5 and Z on 0, 1, 2: Y0 Y1 Y2 X5). The SAT search reached the same exact value, and two further separate exhaustive programs agree. Every nonidentity element of the stabilizer group has weight >= 5 (all 4,095 enumerated), so the code is nondegenerate.

| effort (CPU-hours) | lightest logical found | |---|---| | 0.01 (exhaustive over all Paulis of weight <= 3, then weight 4) | 4 (no logical of weight 1, 2 or 3) |

Claim: d = 4, filed as a witness-backed upper bound because the board's Pauli-weight certifier is not available yet; the exhaustive enumeration shows it is exact. No trial counts: the distance check is exhaustive, not random.

Non-CSS: the verifier finds no single-qubit Clifford frame that makes the generators CSS, and a separate linear-algebra test finds none for the whole stabilizer group. k = 4 from the rank of the 12 independent generators.

Literature: Grassl's codetables.de gives d = 5 as the optimum for [[16,4]] without a weight limit, so this code is not distance-optimal; its point is the generator weight. We did not find a weight-5 [[16,4,4]] stabilizer code in codetables.de, arXiv:2601.19848 (tables stop at n = 12), arXiv:2601.15446 (Wang, Liu, Li, Kubica and Gu; its [[16,4,4]] has weight 8) or the EC Zoo (errorcorrectionzoo.org); literature novelty is not claimed.

Dead ends

  • One-orbit codes (a single weight-5 Pauli and its translates): best 2.78 at n <= 38, the board's [[18,2,5]] again; next [[24,4,4]] at 2.67.
  • Raising d to 5 at n = 16 (Z4, same shape) and d to 4 at n = 12 (Z4, rate 1/3): undecided after 20 min each.
  • Rate 1/2 at d = 3 under small groups ([[12,6,3]] at weight 5, Z3 and Z2): no code of this shape.

Reproduction

Qubit 4*b + g (b = 0..3 orbit, g in Z4); translation by t sends 4*b + g to 4*b + ((g + t) mod 4). The 12 generators are the 4 translates of each representative:

  • X0 Z1 Z3 Y4 Z11
  • X7 Y10 Y12 X13 Z14
  • Y2 X7 X8 X9 Y12

These are exactly the entry's checks.S rows 0-3, 4-7 and 8-11.
