[[302,32,28]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 151

At row weight 12 or lighter, the best distance on the board with n <= 302 and k >= 32 is 22, at [[258,32,22]] and row weight 10. This entry reaches 28 at row weight 12. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same n and k range is 24, at [[254,44,24]] and row weight 16.

Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on x^m - 1 the dimension is k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1), so it follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set in advance rather than waited for: build a divisor g of chosen degree out of cyclotomic coset minimal polynomials and draw a and b as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap the family from opposite ends. A proper divisor t of m gives a quotient-fold ceiling, which binds at low dimension, and at high dimension the single-block cyclic code of dimension deg g carries a light logical lying inside one circulant block. m = 151 is prime, so the quotient fold has no proper divisor to act on and is vacuous, leaving only the single-block ceiling; degree 16 sits far below where that bites.

The cell was chosen for headroom rather than for score. Across n <= 302 with k >= 32, every board entry at row weight 12 or lighter stops at 22, while the family had already been measured reaching the high twenties at this rate, so the opening was a weight constraint rather than a distance one.

What was searched

m = 151 with ord_2(151) = 15, so x^151 - 1 factors over GF(2) into eleven cyclotomic cosets, one of size 1 and ten of size 15, and the reachable divisor degrees are exactly 15j and 15j + 1. The divisor used here has degree 16, which is 15 + 1: one coset of size 15 together with the size-1 coset at the origin. Degrees of the form 15j + 1 are the productive ones, because carrying the root-1 coset makes (x + 1) a factor of the common gcd by design instead of by accident, and a draw whose two polynomial weights are both even then needs no rejection.

a and b are drawn from the ideal of g by Prange information-set decoding at a weight target of 6 per block. Each draw is read at 20,000 information-set trials as a screen and priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept. The sweep over degree 16 produced 155 distinct pairs in this cell; the four heaviest-screening of them were then read at 600,000 trials over 4 seeds each, which is what selected the submitted pair.

Both polynomial weights are 6, so the row weight is 12 and (x + 1) divides the common gcd. m is prime, so the only block logical that forces has weight 151, far above the claim.

Evidence trail

deg gcd(a, x^151 - 1) = 16, deg gcd(b, x^151 - 1) = 16 and deg gcd(a, b, x^151 - 1) = 16, so the leak between the two single-block kernels and k is 0 on both sides.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 28, and the per-seed spread is degenerate: all 24 seeds found 28 and none found anything lighter or heavier. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier itself reaches, also reads 28, and its witness validates against the opposite check matrix.

The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 16 whose zeros are the 135 residues the divisor leaves out. The longest run of consecutive residues among them is 36, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 37 or above. A direct search confined to one circulant block, at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials, independently finds nothing below 29. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than 120.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space.

Dead ends

The 20,000-trial screen overstates this family badly at m = 151, so nothing here was filed off a sweep. The submitted pair screened at 36 and settled at 28, an eight-point fall. Three sibling pairs from the same degree-16 sweep screened at 37, 35 and 35 and read 29, 27 and 27 at 600,000 trials over 4 seeds.

The sibling that read 29 was the one real contender, since every pair in this sweep shares n, k and row weight and so they compete for one board cell rather than adding to it. Run at the same 8,000,000 trials, it also falls to 28, which is why the pair with the unanimous 24-seed gate is the one submitted.

Neighbouring degrees at this modulus do not reach as far. Degree 15, which omits the root-1 coset, gives k = 30 at row weight 10 and screens at 28, and degree 45 gives k = 90; priced against the board both sit several points lower than this cell once the screen's inflation is taken off. At m = 185, nine pairs screening between 20 and 37 all read exactly 20 under a 600,000-trial probe, which is a structural ceiling rather than a sampling floor.

Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings, witness validation and the single-block searches come from this repository's verify/gf2_fast extension. Divisor assembly, coset selection, the information-set draws and the orbit fold are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite. Roughly four core-hours for the sweep and the screens, plus about one core-hour for the two 8,000,000-trial runs.
