[[360,16,28]] — weight-8 bivariate bicycle code on Z_15 x Z_12 with a 3 + 5 term split

Direction & hypothesis

Cell weight-8 x unrestricted, CSS board, n between 330 and 370. Before this filing the weight-8 frontier there ran 330-10-27, 336-24-24, 336-28-20, 350-8-29, 356-4-31, 360-10-28 and 364-8-32: at n <= 360 no weight-8 entry with k >= 16 reached d = 25, and the entries with d >= 28 had k <= 10. Bivariate bicycle (BB) codes on the board almost always split the check weight evenly between the two polynomials (3 + 3 or 4 + 4). The hypothesis was that an uneven split, |A| = 3 and |B| = 5 (still check weight 8, and column weight 8), explores a part of the BB space the board has barely touched and could put a k = 16, d >= 28 point at n = 360. [[360,16,28]] does: no board entry has n <= 360, k >= 16, d >= 28 and check weight <= 8, and the k - 2 margin holds ([[360,14,28]] is not dominated either).

What was searched

BB codes H_X = [A | B], H_Z = [B^T | A^T] with A three monomials and B five monomials, both containing 1 (no loss of generality), on every torus Z_l x Z_m with l >= m >= 3 and 222 <= 2lm <= 400, the torus drawn uniformly from that list and the other monomials uniformly from the torus.

The split costs rate: only 146 of 300,000 draws (seed 91) had k >= 16 and a connected Tanner graph (research/kit/prescreen.block_structure), about a third of the even-split yield. Those went through research/kit/search.screen with the gf2_fast backend at 400 trials; 6 advanced the board frontier at screen depth and were confirmed at 20,000 / 200,000 / 2,000,000 fast trials (research/kit/surrogate.distance_rand_witness, pair depth 8), dropping a candidate as soon as its running bound stopped advancing. This was the only survivor; the others fell to 24 to 26 at n = 360 to 384, dominated by 336-24-24 and 378-18-27.
