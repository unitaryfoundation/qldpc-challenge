[[600,24,26]]: weight-6 two-block group algebra code over D5 x C30

Direction & hypothesis

Cell: check weight at most 6, unrestricted locality (no layout submitted). The leaders of this cell are two-block group algebra codes over non-abelian groups, and every entry there with d >= 20 was a witnessed upper bound. Hypothesis: an odd-index voltage lift of a good base code along a central subgroup K keeps the distance, since for |K| odd and equal k one has d(G/K) <= d(G) <= |K| d(G/K) (Hirasaki and Lee, arXiv:2607.28621, Cor. 6; for central K also Guémard and Zémor, arXiv:2502.20297 v2, Prop. 3.5 and Rem. 3.6), while the decomposition of F_2[K] into characters decides which lifts gain logical qubits.

What was searched

Base: the [[200,16,12]] code over D5 x C10 = <x,y | x^10, y^10, y x y^-1 = x^9> with a = 1 + x^4 y^8 + x^3 y^9 and b = 1 + x^2 y^4 + x^4 y^7. It has the parameters of the weight-6 [[200,16,12]] code over C10 x D10 (GAP notation for D5 x C10) in the census of G. Lee (arXiv:2610.13880, Table 9), credited there to the code archive of Lin and Pryadko (arXiv:2306.16400); we did not check equivalence. We took all 3-fold voltage lifts along the central subgroup <x^10> of G = <x,y | x^30, y^10, y x y^-1 = x^19> that keep the images of a and b: 41 equivalence classes, with k in {16, 20, 24, 32, 48}. The eight lifts with k = 24 fall into two equivalence classes; one is this code, the other has d <= 12. The 16 logical qubits of the base sit at an orbit of 8 characters of order 5 of the abelian subgroup <x, y^2>, trivial on the central C3, so the quotient keeps them; the lift adds 8 at an orbit of 4 characters of order 15.

Evidence trail

  • k = 24 = 600 - 288 - 288 (ranks over GF(2)), computed three times independently.
  • Upper bound: 83 Z-type and 70 X-type logical operators of weight 26, found by random information-set search (1e5 trials per type); the entry carries one of each.
  • Lower bound. Every column of H_X and H_Z has weight 3, so every vector of ker H_X and of ker H_Z has even weight and it suffices to exclude weight at most 24. The automorphism group of the Tanner graph that preserves the blocks and the check types has order 150 and two orbits on each block (x^i y^j with j even, resp. odd). The anchored search with exclusions, one anchor per orbit (implementation A with the earlier orbits excluded, implementation B with nothing excluded for the two anchors in the first block and the first block excluded for the two in the second), finds no nontrivial logical operator of weight at most 24 on either side, in two independently written programs:
  • Z side, implementation A (vsearch_orb): 6.36e7 nodes, 345 s, nothing found
  • Z side, implementation B (cluster2): 4.2e8 generated states, 412 s, nothing found
  • X side, implementation A (vsearch_orb): 6.42e7 nodes, 392 s, nothing found
  • X side, implementation B (cluster2): 4.42e8 generated states, 501 s, nothing found
  • Result: d_X = d_Z = 26, exact. The completeness of the search is proved in a short note that comes with both programs, their inputs and logs: github.com/TimoKellerMath/quantum-error-correction @ 2be467a7bd36, weight6-exact-distances/exact_distances.pdf (Theorem 4.1). certs/600-24-26.json records the verdict at level solver.
  • Earlier runs of implementation B in another indexing of the group, at W = 25 without the parity reduction (4.0e8 nodes on the Z side, 3.6e8 on the X side; not part of the published package), agree.

Dead ends

  • The other class of lifts with k = 24 has d <= 12.
  • The board entry 600-16-30 of @FarLab lives on the same group, with the same images in the base and other voltages. For it, one implementation (cluster2, W = 23, automorphism anchors) found no nontrivial logical operator of weight at most 23 on either side, which would give 24 <= d <= 30 there; that run is not part of the published package.
  • Our best abelian weight-6 two-block codes at n <= 600 reach kd^2/n = 21.21, with a [[510,16,26]] code equivalent to the board entry 510-16-26 of V. Russo (by an automorphism of Z/255 and translations).

Tools

Claude Opus 5.5 (Claude Code); Magma and Sage for k, equivalences and automorphisms; two C programs for the exact search that share no code (implementation B implements Algorithm 1 of Rowshan and Devitt with the additions of Section 3 of exact_distances.pdf, implementation A was written later, from scratch, from a description of Algorithm 1 of exact_distances.pdf and its proof); Python for the instances. The search refines the complete searches of Dumer, Kovalev and Pryadko (arXiv:1611.07164) and Okada and Kasai (arXiv:2607.14091); further ingredients are in Lee et al. (arXiv:2609.30069), J. Y. Lee (arXiv:2610.06820), Cheng, Guo and Pan (arXiv:2611.08247), Rowshan and Devitt (arXiv:2609.39395) and the fieldnote fieldnotes/2026-10-13-bb360-12-exhaustive-d24.md of C. Crocker; Section 3 of exact_distances.pdf says what is taken from where.
