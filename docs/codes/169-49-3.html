[[169,49,3]] octahedron-graph incidence hypergraph product

Direction & hypothesis

Target the unrestricted / weight-6 frontier at d = 3 using research/kit/products.py (products.hypergraph_product(H1, H2)). For a connected Delta-regular graph G = (V, E) of girth g, dropping 1 redundant vertex row from the |V| x |E| vertex-edge incidence matrix yields a full-row-rank (|V|-1) x |E| parity-check matrix H with row weight Delta, column weight <= 2, cycle-space dimension k_1 = |E| - |V| + 1, and classical distance d_1 = g. Its symmetric hypergraph product has check weight w = Delta + 2, n = |E|^2 + (|V|-1)^2, k = (|E| - |V| + 1)^2, and distance d = g by Tillich-Zemor Theorem 1 (arXiv:0903.0566).

What was searched

Screened symmetric and asymmetric hypergraph products of full and row-reduced vertex-edge incidence matrices of regular graphs (K_4, K_5, octahedron K_{2,2,2}, K_{3,3}, K_{4,4}, Petersen, Heawood) with Delta <= 4 (`w <= 6). For the 4-regular octahedron graph K_{2,2,2}` (6 vertices, 12 edges, girth 3), the 5 x 12 full-row-rank incidence matrix gives [[169,49,3]] at check weight w = 6 (kd^2/n = 2.609), saving 11 physical qubits over the unreduced 6 x 12 incidence product [[180,50,3]].

Distance d = 3 follows from Tillich-Zemor Theorem 1 (cycle-space girth 3 of K_{2,2,2}) and was independently checked with verify/sat_certify.py (CryptoMiniSat 5.14 SAT, UNSAT below weight 3 on both sides in 0.05 s); claimed as a witness-backed upper bound.

edges = [(u, v) for u in range(6) for v in range(u+1, 6) if u + v != 5]
H = np.zeros((6, len(edges)), dtype=np.int8)
for j, (u, v) in enumerate(edges): H[u, j] = H[v, j] = 1
hx, hz = hypergraph_product(H[:-1], H[:-1])
