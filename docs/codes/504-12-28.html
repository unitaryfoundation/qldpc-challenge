[[504,12,28]]: weight-6 cyclic two-block code over Z_252

Direction & hypothesis

Cell: check weight at most 6, unrestricted locality (no layout submitted). A cyclic two-block code whose weight-3 polynomials a, b have a common zero of order N in a field of characteristic 2 captures k = 2 ord_N(2) logical qubits from that Frobenius orbit. Such a pair needs a relation 1 + u + v = 0 with u, v generating the N-th roots of unity, which for odd N <= 600 happens exactly for N in {3, 7, 15, 31, 63, 127, 255, 511, 73, 85, 273, 341, 585}. Hypothesis: on Z/(mN) with m > 1 (twisted covers of the codes on Z/N), the distance grows with m while k stays 2 ord_N(2). Covers of codes are studied by Guémard and Zémor (arXiv:2502.20297) and by Hirasaki and Lee (arXiv:2607.28621), and sequences of covers of bivariate bicycle codes by Symons, Rajput and Browne (arXiv:2511.13560).

What was searched

About 3e4 weight-6 cyclic and abelian two-block codes with one captured reduced point of order N and |G| = mN <= 300, in the families N = 31 (m <= 9), 63 (m <= 4), 73 (m <= 4), 85 (m <= 3), 127 (m <= 2), 255 and 273 (m = 1), 15 and split orders, reduced modulo the symmetries; distances bounded by Lee-Brickell information-set search (3e5 trials for the finalists). For N = 63 (ord_63(2) = 6, so k = 12) and m = 4 the best class is this code: found as a = 1 + x + x^195, b = 1 + x^134 + x^48 on Z/252 and submitted in the equivalent normal form a = 1 + x + x^58, b = 1 + x^48 + x^166 (an automorphism of Z/252 and translations). The same search reproduced the [[254,14,16]], [[340,16,18]] and [[248,10,18]] codes of Liang et al. (arXiv:2503.03827).

Evidence trail

  • k = 12 = 504 - 246 - 246 (ranks over GF(2)).
  • Upper bound: a Z-type logical operator of weight 28 found by the exact search at W = 28; the X-type witness is its image under the X/Z exchange below.
  • Lower bound. The qubit permutation (block 1, i) <-> (block 2, -i) maps the X-check i onto the Z-check -i and the Z-check i onto the X-check -i, so d_X = d_Z. Every column of H_X has weight 3, so every vector of ker H_X has even weight and it suffices to exclude weight at most 26. The anchored search with exclusions (implementation A: pattern anchoring under the cyclic translations; implementation B: one anchor per block) finds no nontrivial Z-logical operator of weight at most 26, in two independently written programs:
  • Z side, implementation A (vsearch_orb): 4.05e8 nodes, 1987 s, nothing found
  • Z side, implementation B (cluster2): 5.25e9 generated states, 4873 s, nothing found
  • Result: d_X = d_Z = 28, exact. The completeness of the search is proved in a short note that comes with both programs, their inputs and logs: github.com/TimoKellerMath/quantum-error-correction @ 2be467a7bd36, weight6-exact-distances/exact_distances.pdf (Theorem 4.1). certs/504-12-28.json records the verdict at level solver.

Dead ends

  • The best codes of the other families: [[510,16,26]] (N = 85, m = 3), equivalent to the earlier board entry 510-16-26 of V. Russo; [[510,16,24]] (N = 255, m = 1); [[508,14,26]] (N = 127, m = 2); [[558,14,28]] (N = 31, m = 9, with a further captured point of order 3, so k = 14 rather than 2 ord_31(2) = 10), with the parameters of the board entry 558-14-28 of V. Russo but not affinely equivalent to it; for N in {31, 73, 85} every code on Z/N itself has d <= 10, so only covers are useful.
  • The weight-6 bivariate bicycle code with n = 504, k = 12 of Symons, Rajput and Browne (arXiv:2511.13560, Table 1, d <= 30 from BP-OSD) has, by our computation, also d = 28; it is not part of this submission.

Tools

Claude Opus 5.5 (Claude Code); Magma for k and witnesses; a Lee-Brickell information-set search in C for the screening; two C programs for the exact search that share no code (implementation B implements Algorithm 1 of Rowshan and Devitt with the additions of Section 3 of exact_distances.pdf, implementation A was written later, from scratch, from a description of Algorithm 1 of exact_distances.pdf and its proof); Python for the instances. The search refines the complete searches of Dumer, Kovalev and Pryadko (arXiv:1611.07164) and Okada and Kasai (arXiv:2607.14091); further ingredients are in Lee et al. (arXiv:2609.30069), J. Y. Crocker; Section 3 of exact_distances.pdf says what is taken from where.
