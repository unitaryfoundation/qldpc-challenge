[[336,24,12]] — two-block group-algebra code over S4 x C7

Direction & hypothesis

Cell weight-6 x unrestricted, CSS board, n between 300 and 400. Before this filing the weight-6 frontier there held 310-10-22, 330-22-9, 330-68-6, 340-16-18, 360-12-24, 360-20-20, 360-38-8 and 382-36-10: every weight-6 entry with k >= 24 had d <= 10, and every one with d >= 12 had k <= 20. The hypothesis was that two-block group-algebra (2BGA) codes on non-abelian groups of order 150 to 200 could put a k >= 24, d >= 12 point into that gap at check weight 6. [[336,24,12]] does at the smaller end of the band: no board entry has n <= 336, k >= 24, d >= 12 and check weight <= 6, and the margins hold at k - 2 and d - 2 (neither [[336,22,12]] nor [[336,24,10]] is dominated). It is complementary to the [[378,24,16]] on C_63 ⋊ C_3 filed from the same campaign: that one has the larger distance, this one reaches k = 24, d = 12 with 42 fewer qubits.

What was searched

Random-support two-block codes with a, b three group elements each, both containing the identity. This draw came from the kit's coset-code builder (research/kit/coset.py, Aydin-Tamo-Barg) with the trivial subgroup H = {e}, for which build_coset(mul, [0], a, b) is identical to group_algebra.build_2bga(mul, a, b) (the module's own self-test asserts this), so the code is a plain 2BGA on S4 x C7 (order 168, n = 336), and it is filed as one. The coset sweep drew groups from S5, A6, S5 x C2, S6, A5 x C3, A5 x C2, S4 x C5, S4 x C6, S4 x C7 with H a cyclic subgroup (or trivial) chosen so that 222 <= 2 [G:H] <= 400, a from G and b from the normaliser of H.

Random supports almost never give usable k (a generic weight-3 element is invertible on the higher-dimensional irreducible blocks of F_2[G]), so the sweep ran a parallel generate-and-rank prefilter: 60,000 draws (seed 54), of which 1,035 had k >= 12 and were connected. Those went through research/kit/search.screen with the gf2_fast backend at 400 trials; 3 advanced the board frontier at screen depth and were confirmed at 20,000 / 200,000 / 2,000,000 fast trials (research/kit/surrogate.distance_rand_witness, pair depth 8). The other two were a [[288,24,8]] on S4 x C6 (twice), which the [[240,24,8]] filed from the same campaign dominates. A serial sweep of 3,000 coset draws without the prefilter (seed 32) had 2,869 with k < 12 and nothing advancing; a second prefiltered sweep of the same generator at check weight 8 found nothing advancing either.

Dead ends

  • Cyclic generalized-bicycle codes with a designed divisor g | x^m - 1 at

    • check weight 6 (weight-3 a and b): at odd m from 111 to 200 and deg g >= 8 there are essentially no weight-3 multiples of g; the few that exist are lifts from a subring Z_{m/s} and give direct sums. Weight-4 multiples exist only for deg g <= 22, and 4,000 of those at 300 <= n <= 400 gave nothing advancing the weight-8 frontier even at 400 trials.

  • Random-support 2BGA at check weight 8, 16,000 serial plus 150,000

    • prefiltered draws (2,881 with k >= 18): every screen-depth survivor collapsed by 20,000 trials (dominated by 248-24-18, 252-26-18, 292-22-23, 336-24-24, 378-18-27 and similar).

  • Non-abelian lifted products with 1 x 2 bases: weight-(3,2) entries

    • (check weight 8), 5,000 draws, nothing advancing; weight-(2,2) entries (check weight 6) give k = |G| + 6 at d = 6, already dominated by the board's 258-88-6.

  • Proper coset codes (H non-trivial and non-normal, b restricted to the

    • normaliser) in this sweep never passed the k prefilter at k >= 12 with a competitive distance; every survivor had H = {e}.

Reproduction

The group is direct_product(sym(4)[0], cyclic_product(7)[0])[0] from research/kit/group_algebra.py: S4 in the kit's BFS element order (generated by the transposition (0 1) and the 4-cycle) times C_7, element (s, c) at index 7 s + c, identity at 0. The supports are element indices in that table:

a = [0, 96, 143] (S4 elements 0, 13, 20 with C_7 parts 0, 5, 3), b = [0, 88, 166] (S4 elements 0, 12, 23 with C_7 parts 0, 4, 5).

import sys; sys.path[:0] = ["research/kit", "verify"]

Check sets match codes/336-24-12.json row for row in this order. Witness search: `surrogate.distance_rand_witness(HX, HZ, trials=2_000_000, seed=54000003 + 2_000_000, backend="fast", pair_depth=8)` returns a weight-12 logical; the numpy lightest_logical at 1,500 trials with seeds 54 (X) and 55 (Z) gives the two witnesses in the JSON.
