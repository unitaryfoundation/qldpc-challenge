Direction & hypothesis

arXiv:2610.13880 (Gideon Lee, "Bicycle Race: small two-block codes on the Pareto frontier", 9 October 2026) enumerates about 10^8 two-block group-algebra codes C(a,b) of check weight five to nine over groups of order at most 160 (n <= 320), with exact distances, and publishes a database of 12,448 of them (github.com/gideonlsx/bicycle-race @ aed705a, file data/codes.jsonl, CC BY 4.0), each with a witness logical. The question here is narrow: which of the codes the paper tabulates (Tables 2, 3, 9 and 11, and the ones named in Sec. 3) advance the board's four-axis frontier (n, k, d, check weight) and are not already on it? This entry is one of the 28 that do.

What was searched

  • Screen: the 45 distinct parameter triples in those tables were compared

    • with every CSS entry on main under the site's Pareto rule (an entry dominates when n <=, k >=, d >=, w <= with at least one strict), at the paper's check weight. Thirteen are already on the board at the same or a lower weight ([[60,4,8]], [[64,18,8]], [[84,14,10]], [[90,8,10]], [[90,12,9]], [[102,20,10]], [[144,12,12]], [[180,8,16]], [[182,6,18]], [[186,10,14]], [[192,8,16]], [[288,12,18]] and [[288,16,16]]); four are dominated by board entries ([[168,12,12]] by [[144,12,12]], [[198,8,16]] by [[180,8,16]], [[200,16,12]] by [[192,16,12]], and [[216,4,16]] by [[192,4,16]]); Table 9's [[192,12,14]] and [[192,16,12]] are Lin-Pryadko archive codes, not in the paper's database, and already on the board. The remaining 28 are new nondominated points and are filed one per PR.

  • This code: record 1d70ba96465b3ac1 of the database (Tables 2 and 11 of the paper;

    • database labels standout, anchor-beater, front), over the non-abelian group (C7 x C7) : C3 (GAP SmallGroup(147,4), order 147), check weight 7 (block weights 4 + 3): a = e0 + e1 + e2 + e11, b = e0 + e8 + e61 with elements numbered as GAP's Elements(SmallGroup(147,4)), e0 the identity. Table 2 of the paper writes the same code as a = 1 + c + v^2 c^2 + u, b = 1 + u v + u^2 v^4 over (C7 x C7) : C3 = <u, v, c | u^7 = v^7 = c^3 = 1, uv = vu, c u c^-1 = u^4, c v c^-1 = v^4> (lambda = 4). Taken from record 1d70ba96465b3ac1 of the paper's database (github.com/gideonlsx/bicycle-race @ aed705a, data/codes.jsonl, CC BY 4.0), whose data/groups.jsonl gives the generators' right-multiplication permutations in that numbering; qubits 0..146 are the left block and 147..293 the right block.

  • Rebuild: the generators' right-multiplication permutations in the

    • database's data/groups.jsonl give the multiplication table; then H_X = (L(a) | R(b)), H_Z = (R(b)^T | L(a)^T) with qubits 0..|G|-1 the left block. A short standalone NumPy script did this and checked CSS commutation, k = n - rank H_X - rank H_Z = 18, max check weight 7, and both of the paper's witnesses (weight 20 X, weight 20 Z, in the kernel of the other side's checks and outside its own stabilizer row space) before anything was submitted.

  • Distance: the paper states d = 20 exactly. Its record: d_X "equal to d_Z by a checked X/Z-exchange automorphism";

    • d_Z "ladder (cluster engine, symmetry roots), from the screen's lower bound 18, no target"; verification "checked: GAP rebuild, QDistRnd and QDistEvol find nothing below d". Here, qldpc submit's own search (20,000 NumPy RIS trials per side, then a 2,000,000-trial accelerator pass) read d_X <= 20 and d_Z <= 20, and the verifier's refutation search found nothing lighter than 20.
