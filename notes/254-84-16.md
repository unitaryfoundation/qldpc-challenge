# [[254,84,16]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 127

At row weight 26 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 254` and `k >= 84` is 10, at `[[228,86,10]]` and row weight 12. This entry reaches 16. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 10, at `[[228,84,10]]` and row weight 12.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Here m is prime, so the quotient fold has no proper divisor to act on and is vacuous; only the single-block ceiling binds, and degree 42 sits below where it bites.

## What was searched

`m = 127` with `ord_2(127) = 7`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^127 - 1` over GF(2) number 19 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 6 cosets of total degree 42, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

Draws with two even weights are rejected: `(x + 1)` divides a binary polynomial exactly when its weight is even, so two even weights would add the root-1 coset to the common gcd and land the draw one degree above the target. Here `a` has weight 13 and `b` has weight 13.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^127 - 1) = 42`, `deg gcd(b, x^127 - 1) = 42` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^127 - 1) = 42`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 16. The per-seed spread: 24 seeds found 16. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 42 whose zeros are the 85 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 29, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 30 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 22. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier itself reaches, reads 16.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; each weighs 16, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/254-84-16.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
