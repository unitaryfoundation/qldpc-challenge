# [[514,96,58]] — cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal

No board entry at `n <= 514` reaches `k >= 96` and `d >= 58` together. The
nearest entries with that dimension and distance sit at `n = 610` and above,
and the best entry at this blocklength is `[[514,128,52]]` at `kd^2/n = 673.4`
against 628.3 here, so this adds a distinct corner rather than a better score.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`,
so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be chosen. The target
was `m = 257`, which is prime: a proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold
ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, so a prime modulus admits no quotient fold at all and
the only structural ceilings left are the two-orbit block swap and the
single-block bound.

## What was searched

`m = 257` with `ord_2(257) = 16`. The cyclotomic cosets of `x^257 - 1` over
GF(2) are one of size 1 and sixteen of size 16, so the reachable divisor degrees
are multiples of 16 plus an optional 1. A sweep over degrees 32, 48, 80 and 96
drew `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of each divisor's ideal by Prange
information-set decoding, screening every draw against the quotient-lift,
single-block and orbit-fold bounds before any confirmation time was spent.

Degree 48 gives this code. Triage read 70 at 20,000 trials; the 24-seed
confirmation settled it at 58, which is the usual direction and size of that
correction in this family.

## Evidence trail

Divisor degree 48, so `k = 96`. Both single-block gcds equal 48, matching
`deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so neither circulant block carries a kernel wider than
`k` requires.

- General RIS, 24 random information-set seeds at 2,000,000 trials each
  (10,712 s): lightest logical weight 58 on Z and 60 on X, per-seed spread 58 to
  64 with a median of 62.
- Orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the 514 qubits
  (`research/foldbound.py`): lightest validated lift weight 146. A prime
  modulus leaves only the two-orbit block swap, and it is not binding here.
- Single-block circulant search, 12 seeds at 600,000 trials: nothing under 58.

The claim is `d = 58`, a witness-backed upper bound. Both witnesses are in the
submission and were re-checked against `H_X` and `H_Z` with plain integer
arithmetic.

## Dead ends

The information-set draw adds the factor `x + 1` on its own, and at this
modulus that costs real distance. `(x+1)` divides a polynomial exactly when its
weight is even, so it divides `gcd(a, b)` exactly when both weights are even,
and the draw returns even weights roughly half the time. A sweep aiming at
degree 48 then lands on `k = 98` rather than 96 with nothing to detect it: the
common gcd really is degree 49.

Landing two above is the worst case, because the root-1 coset collapses the BCH
bound on the single-block cyclic code. At `m = 257` that bound reads 90, 86 and
74 at `k` = 32, 64 and 96, and 64, 52 and 44 at `k` = 34, 66 and 98. Measured:
the `k = 98` draws gate at 54 while the first `k = 96` draws reach 70 at triage
and 58 under confirmation. Rejecting a draw whose two weights are both even
costs under half the draws and is what separates those two columns.

Degrees 80, 96 and 112 (`k` = 160, 192 and 224) produced nothing undominated
across 300 draws each on eight processes, despite domination thresholds of only
`d > 30` at `k = 160` and `d > 13` at `k = 192`. The general distance does not
reach even those, so a low threshold is not on its own a reason to search a
cell.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 under Claude Code. `gf2_fast` from `verify/` for the accelerated
RIS and single-block searches, `verify/gf2.py` for GF(2) rank, nullspace and
logical bases, `research/foldbound.py` for the orbit fold, and
`verify/validate_candidate.py` for the verdict. Divisor assembly and the
information-set draw are described above rather than committed. The 24-seed
confirmation ran 10,712 s on 6 cores, about 18 core-hours.

## Reproduction

`m = 257`, `n = 514`, `k = 96`.

```
a(x) = 1 + x^45 + x^74 + x^76 + x^82 + x^109 + x^120 + x^130 + x^176 + x^219
     + x^252
b(x) = x^8 + x^10 + x^48 + x^80 + x^104 + x^126 + x^186 + x^195 + x^200
     + x^230 + x^239
```

`H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]` and `H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]`, with
`circ(p)[i, j] = 1` exactly when `j - i mod 257` is an exponent of `p`. The
exact supports are in `codes/514-96-58.json`.
