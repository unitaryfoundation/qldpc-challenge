# [[674,86,93]] — cyclic generalized bicycle at prime length 337, no imposed symmetry

## Direction & hypothesis

Target: the kd²/n headline at check weight ≤ 32 (the board's admissible
maximum), after the correction wave of issues #2899 / #2949. Every top entry
at n = 674–690 — this author's `codes/682-172-52-b.json`, `codes/682-130-56.json`
and `codes/682-82-35.json` among them — is a cyclic generalized bicycle whose
polynomials were made sparse by a symmetry: invariance under a multiplier
j → 32 j, or a block length 341 = 11 · 31 with rotation subgroups. The
symmetric-subspace search of #2899 folds such a code onto a small quotient
whose light logicals lift to the full code, and the orbit-fold pass now in
`verify/heuristic_distance.py` finds them in seconds (`[[682,172,78]]` → 52,
`[[682,82,86]]` → 35).

This entry is the opposite construction: a **prime** block length, so Z_337
has no rotation subgroups, and a polynomial pair with **no multiplier
relation**, so the only automorphisms are the cyclic shifts and the fold
search has nothing to act on. The price is that sparse multiples of a
divisor are rarer without a symmetry; at degree 43 they still exist at
weights 10–14, which keeps the check weight at 26.

## What was searched

x^337 − 1 = (x + 1) · sixteen irreducible factors of degree 21. Divisors of
degree 43 = 1 + 2 · 21 (k = 86): 120 ideals, visited in random order (seed
324). Per ideal:

1. Sparse codewords of the ideal by the kit's Prange search
   (`research/cyclic_gb.py`, `prange_sparse_words`, 20,000 trials, weights
   8–14; 120 words kept).
2. The structural cap once per ideal: single-block logicals are words of the
   [337, 43] code generated by (x^337 − 1)/g and do not depend on the pair,
   so `gf2_fast.circulant_gb_witness` (60k, then 300k trials) decides whether
   any pair from the ideal can reach the target. Ideal 1: cap 85, below the
   89 needed for kd²/n > 1000, skipped. Ideal 2: cap 109.
3. Per pair (|a| + |b| ≤ 28): k = 2 deg g checked, Tanner-graph and
   stabilizer-group connectivity checked, then the verifier's own
   `orbit_fold_min_logical` (300 fold trials, 90 s cap) — **no symmetry to
   fold** (0 partitions) — then an RIS ladder of 2k → 50k → 500k → 2M trials
   per pair, discarding as soon as the bound fell below the target.

The first pair of ideal 2 survived the whole ladder and is this entry.

The board already holds two prime-length k = 86 codes that the correction
wave did not touch, `codes/674-86-89.json` (w = 24, 1010.7) and
`codes/674-86-90.json` (w = 30, 1033.5) — consistent with the hypothesis —
and one symmetric k = 86 code it did, `codes/674-86-56.json` (87 → 56).
This entry uses a different divisor and polynomials from all three.

The claim is a witness-backed **upper bound**; the exact distance is open.
Score at the bound: 86 · 93² / 674 = 1103.6. Without exploitable
structure, a lower bound can only come from more random search, which is
the same tool every refuter has; the family's symmetric entries lost 20–50
units to the fold search, and this construction is chosen so that it cannot.

## Dead ends

- Sparse multiples at prime length are the limiting resource: at degrees
  64 and 85 (k = 128, 170) the lightest ideal words weigh 24–30 or more, so
  pairs there exceed the board's check-weight cap of 32 and are not
  submittable, although a first campaign without the cap found
  [[674,128,≤88]] (w = 66) and [[662,182,≤74]] (w = 80). Weight-12–16 words
  do exist at degrees 61–64 (k = 122–128) with a 40,000-trial Prange search;
  those campaigns are running and will be reported separately if they
  survive.
- The first ideal visited (cap 85) shows that the constituent [337, 43]
  code, not the pair, is the first filter: the cap search costs seconds per
  ideal and rejected it before any RIS trial was spent.

## Reproduction

Let C(S) be the 337 × 337 binary circulant whose row i has ones at columns
(i + s) mod 337 for s ∈ S. H_X = [C(a) | C(b)], H_Z = [C(b)ᵀ | C(a)ᵀ] with a
and b above. The divisor is gcd(a, x^337 − 1) = gcd(b, x^337 − 1), degree 43.
