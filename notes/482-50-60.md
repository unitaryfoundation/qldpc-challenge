# [[482,50,60]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 241

At row weight 20 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 482` and `k >= 50` is 31, at `[[390,82,31]]` and row weight 16. This entry reaches 60. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 38, at `[[390,82,38]]` and row weight 32.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 25 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 241` with `ord_2(241) = 24`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^241 - 1` over GF(2) number 11 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 2 cosets of total degree 25, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

The divisor carries the root-1 coset, so both polynomial weights are even by construction and `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd by design rather than by accident. Here `a` has weight 10 and `b` has weight 10.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^241 - 1) = 25`, `deg gcd(b, x^241 - 1) = 25` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^241 - 1) = 25`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 60. The per-seed spread: 3 seeds found 60, 1 seed found 62, 4 seeds found 63, 4 seeds found 64, 7 seeds found 65, 4 seeds found 66, 1 seed found 67. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 25 whose zeros are the 216 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 48, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 49 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 71. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; each weighs 60, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends

- Nothing else from this front survived its gate.
- m = 241 came from ranking every reachable design point by the longest unpicked run of its coset pick, not from row yield. It is one of ten primes under the blocklength cap with a workable ord_2, and at a prime modulus every quotient fold is vacuous.
- Two checks were added to the pipeline after three codes filed earlier the same night were refuted or found duplicate, and this code passed both before filing. Its check-matrix signature matches no entry in codes/, which is how [[514,130,51]] was caught as an existing board code only after a 48-million-trial gate. And a single information-set run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the refutation gate uses, reads 63 against this claim of 60; twenty-four independent seeds of 2,000,000 each had bottomed at 60. One long run finds operators that many short ones miss, which is how [[674,84,94]] and [[514,98,57]] were refuted after passing their 24-seed gates.

The triage reading for this pair was 65, against a gated 60. Every member of this front moved the same way, which is why all of them were gated rather than filed off the sweep.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings and single-block searches come from the repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension; divisor assembly, coset selection and the information-set draws are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite.

## Reproduction

`m = 241`, `n = 2m = 482`. With `a = {16, 54, 60, 61, 64, 86, 135, 136, 161, 225}` and `b = {43, 45, 60, 66, 71, 104, 108, 120, 124, 179}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/482-50-60.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
