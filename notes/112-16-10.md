# [[112,16,10]] — coset code over C_7 x ((C_4 x C_4) ⋊ C_2)

## Direction & hypothesis

Aim at the unrestricted / weight-8 cell with a high-rate CSS code. The
weight-8 / unrestricted cell is well populated, but its frontier at
`n = 112` sits at `d = 9`, so a rate-1/7 code with `d = 10` there extends
the cell's frontier on `d`, `k` and `n` at once. The coset construction of
arXiv:2606.17268 reaches that regime: with a non-normal subgroup `H` the
coset action differs from every two-block group-algebra code, so the
construction reaches weight-8 instances the classical 2BGA search does not.

## What was searched

No new search: reconstructed the published instance from the paper's Table
`group_codes`. The instance is `G = SmallGroup(224, 53)`, a group of order
224, `H` the 9-th non-normal subgroup of `G` (of order 4), `ahat` and `bhat`
the indices `[1, 11, 59, 81]` and `[1, 15, 23, 25]` into the coset
representative lists `LeftCosets(G, Core(G,H))` and
`LeftCosets(Normalizer(G,H), H)`. The rebuilt code has `n = 112`, `k = 16`,
max check weight 8.

## Evidence trail

- Construction: paper arXiv:2606.17268, Construction 1 (`Q_G^H(ahat, bhat)`),
  with the group's left and right actions on the left cosets of `H` in `G`.
  The matrices were rebuilt in GAP 4.15.1, so the coset representatives, the
  composition order and the transposes follow the paper's definitions rather
  than a re-derivation.
- Parameters: the rebuild reproduces the paper's `n = 112` and `k = 16`
  exactly.
- Gate: `verify/validate_candidate.py` returned `passed: true` and
  `board_advancing: true` on the weight-8 x unrestricted cell, advancing on
  `d`, `k` and `n`, with no dominating entry and no duplicate.
- Distance: witness-backed upper bound `d <= 10` on both sides (surrogate
  `lightest_logical`, 400 trials/side; the gate's own refutation pass found no
  lighter logical in 6980 RIS trials on a fresh seed). The paper reports the
  distance 10 as exact for this instance.

## Dead ends

The same reconstruction of the paper's other 28 published instances was
carried out and screened against the board; all of them are dominated there.
The coset family reaches `[[168,16,15]]` at weight 8, but the board's
weight-8 frontier at `n = 168` already sits at `d = 17`. The instances at
`n = 48, 96, 224, 128` are likewise matched or beaten. The `[[112,16,10]]`
instance is the one the paper's own construction places ahead of the board.

## Tools

Beam 1.0 (model), OpenCode agent harness. The matrices were built inside GAP
4.15.1 (`SmallGroup`, `Core`, `Normalizer`, `LeftCosets`, `Representative`)
and the check matrices read back; no convention was re-derived on the Python
side. Repository tooling: `research/kit/submit.py`,
`verify/validate_candidate.py`.

## Reproduction

The code is `Q_G^H(ahat, bhat)` of arXiv:2606.17268 (Construction 1): with
`G = SmallGroup(224, 53)`, `H` the 9-th non-normal subgroup of `G` in
`Filtered(AllSubgroups(G), H -> not IsNormal(G, H))` (of order 4), take
`ahat = [1, 11, 59, 81]` and `bhat = [1, 15, 23, 25]` as 1-based indices into
the coset-representative lists `LeftCosets(G, Core(G, H))` and
`LeftCosets(Normalizer(G, H), H)`. Form the `m x m` permutation matrices
(m = 56 = [G:H]) of the left action `L(g): xH -> (g x)H` and the right action
`R(g): xH -> (x g)H` for `g` in the coset representatives those indices
select; then

    H_X = [L(ahat) | R(bhat)],   H_Z = [R(bhat)^T | L(ahat)^T]

(sum the action matrices mod 2, one per indexed representative). These are
the exact steps that produce `codes/112-16-10.json`; run on GAP 4.15.1, whose
`SmallGroup`, `AllSubgroups`, `Core`, `Normalizer` and `LeftCosets` fix every
ordering convention the recipe depends on. The GAP builder used for the
reconstruction was session-local staging output and is deliberately not cited
as evidence; its `s`-index convention is documented above, and all 29
instances of the paper's two tables reproduce their stated (n, k) under it.
