# [[662,60,94]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 331

At row weight 20 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 662` and `k >= 60` is 89, at `[[662,62,89]]` and row weight 18. This entry reaches 94. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 96, at `[[662,60,96]]` and row weight 22.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 30 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 331` with `ord_2(331) = 30`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^331 - 1` over GF(2) number 12 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 1 coset of total degree 30, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

Draws with two even weights are rejected: `(x + 1)` divides a binary polynomial exactly when its weight is even, so two even weights would add the root-1 coset to the common gcd and land the draw one degree above the target. Here `a` has weight 9 and `b` has weight 11.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^331 - 1) = 30`, `deg gcd(b, x^331 - 1) = 30` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^331 - 1) = 30`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 94. The per-seed spread: 2 seeds found 94, 4 seeds found 96, 9 seeds found 98, 9 seeds found 100. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 30 whose zeros are the 301 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 66, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 67 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 104. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier itself reaches, reads 94.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; each weighs 94, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends


- A second draw in this cell at the same row weight was gated alongside this one and read the same 94 at six seeds, so it could yield at most one entry and was stopped. The board's [[662,60,96]] at row weight 22 reaches two more but is heavier, so neither settles the other.
- The margin here is four points over the board's [[662,62,89]] at row weight 18, which is the narrowest of anything filed from this session. The deep run is what justifies it: a single 8,000,000-trial search, the depth the verifier reaches, independently settled on 94 rather than finding anything below it.

The reading before this gate was 104, against a gated 94.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings and single-block searches come from the repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension; divisor assembly, coset selection and the information-set draws are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite.

## Reproduction

`m = 331`, `n = 2m = 662`. With `a = {122, 132, 140, 150, 173, 185, 230, 234, 271}` and `b = {0, 9, 22, 94, 104, 132, 136, 180, 201, 231, 305}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/662-60-94.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
