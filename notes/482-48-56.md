# [[482,48,56]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 241

At row weight 14 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 482` and `k >= 48` is 26, at `[[390,68,26]]` and row weight 13. This entry reaches 56. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 60, at `[[482,50,60]]` and row weight 20.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 24 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 241` with `ord_2(241) = 24`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^241 - 1` over GF(2) number 11 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 1 coset of total degree 24, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

Draws with two even weights are rejected: `(x + 1)` divides a binary polynomial exactly when its weight is even, so two even weights would add the root-1 coset to the common gcd and land the draw one degree above the target. Here `a` has weight 7 and `b` has weight 7.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^241 - 1) = 24`, `deg gcd(b, x^241 - 1) = 24` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^241 - 1) = 24`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 56. The per-seed spread: 1 seed found 56, 1 seed found 58, 3 seeds found 60, 14 seeds found 62, 5 seeds found 64. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 24 whose zeros are the 217 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 69, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 70 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 72. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier itself reaches, reads 62.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; the X witness weighs 56 and the Z witness weighs 60, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends

- [[482,48,56]] from the same front reaches the same 56 but at row weight 16 against 14 here, so this entry dominates it and takes the same slug rather than standing beside it.
- A third draw at this design point, row weight 22, reached 62 over 16 of its 24 gate seeds and is dominated by the board's [[482,50,60]] at row weight 20, so it was dropped rather than carried further. Row weight 14, 16 and 22 therefore all land at 56 to 62 here, and only the weight the checks come in at separates them.

The reading before this gate was 72, against a gated 56. Every member of this front moved the same way, which is why all of them were gated rather than filed off the sweep.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings and single-block searches come from the repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension; divisor assembly, coset selection and the information-set draws are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite.

## Reproduction

`m = 241`, `n = 2m = 482`. With `a = {11, 45, 139, 186, 229, 234, 236}` and `b = {61, 70, 116, 126, 184, 200, 201}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/482-48-56.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
