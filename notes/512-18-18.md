# [[512,18,18]] The 16x16 member of the board's weight-8 planar tile family, d <= 18 by explicit witness, one below the published [[512,18,19]] but a DIFFERENT CODE from it

## Correction (2026-10-10)

An earlier revision of this note and of `codes/512-18-18.json` read the d <= 18
below as a failure to reproduce the published exact d = 19 of [[512,18,19]]
(arXiv:2504.09171), and the entry's `name` field carried `[[512,18,19]]`. That
was an identification error, not a distance error, and the distance is
unaffected: the stored weight-18 Z-logical is a genuine logical of the code in
the diff, and `verify/qldpc_verify.py` still accepts the file.

arXiv:2504.09171 specifies its B=4 weight-8 tile in the LaTeX source as
literal `\def` macros (main.tex lines 690-691, repeated at 763-764), not only
in a figure: X-tile horizontal edges `{(0,0),(0,3),(2,2),(3,0)}`, vertical
edges `{(0,1),(1,0),(1,1),(3,3)}`. This entry's A-support is the published
horizontal set exactly; its B-support `{(0,2),(1,3),(2,0),(3,3)}` differs from
the published vertical set in three of four monomials.

Rebuilt with the published tile, `build_planar` reproduces the paper's two
published exact distances at both sizes the paper gives them: d <= 13 at a
12x12 lattice (9x9 bulk tiles, the paper's [[288,18,13]]) and d <= 19 at 16x16
(13x13 bulk tiles, the paper's [[512,18,19]]). The B-support used by this entry
gives d <= 11 and d <= 18 at those same two sizes. Both readings were checked
with `verify/qldpc_verify.py`'s own `gf2.commutes` / `gf2.in_rowspace`, not
only through the kit's surrogate.

So this entry is the board's own construction at the published geometry. The
published [[512,18,19]] stands, unreproduced and un-refuted; it is simply a
different code. `research/local2d/boundary_engine.py` carried the mislabelling
and is being corrected separately; the published B-support is the value above,
which that module does not currently export.

## Direction & hypothesis

Aimed at the weight-8 x local-2d-bilayer cell, which is where the board's own
tile family lives and where the frontier was thinnest in the k=18 strand.
TRACKS.md names [[512,18,19]] (arXiv:2504.09171) as the published weight-8
2D-local bar and it was never seeded: the family was on the board at n=924
(d=31) and n=566/563/562 (d=19..20), but at nothing below n=562. The opening
was therefore not a new construction but a lattice size the family had never
been built at, combined with an honest re-measurement of the distance rather
than an inherited one.

## What was searched

The open-boundary planar tile builder
research/local2d/boundary_engine.build_planar over lattice sizes 12x13 through
21x22, on two B-supports: S_G_TILE4_MAIN={(0,2),(1,3),(2,0),(3,3)} and the
sibling g={(0,1),(1,1),(2,0),(3,3)} that the board's codes/566-18-20 records
for this family. The generator was calibrated first, by check-matrix
comparison rather than by (n,k,w), as research/AUTORESEARCH.md section 3
requires: the call at (21,22) reproduces codes/924-18-31.json with all 475 X
rows and all 450 Z rows identical. Screening was the kit surrogate, per side,
plus an independent threaded gf2_fast pass used to try to break the claim
rather than to support it.
## Evidence trail

Confirmation ladder for the submitted code, each rung a fresh seed unless
marked otherwise, reading the lightest logical found:

| budget (trials/side) | lightest logical |
| ---: | ---: |
| 50,000 (RIS per side, seed 20261010, via submit.make_submission (dX=20, dZ=18)) | 18 |
| 1,000,000 (gf2_fast, 2,000,000 accelerator trials shared across the two sides (1,000,000 per side), seed 11) | 18 |

2 fresh-seed rung(s) agree at the best bound, the deepest of them at 1,000,000
trials per side. The submitted witness reproduces it: X: d <= 20
(upper_bound), Z: d <= 18 (upper_bound).

Both rungs are fresh seeds and both settle at 18, so the bound is 2 flat fresh
rungs deep at the minimum rung count research/kit/escalation.py requires. The
second rung is an independent mechanism from the first - the accelerator
rather than the NumPy RIS path - which is why it counts as corroboration and
not repetition. The per-side figure is what promote.py compares against, since
its own reference point is the CLI's 2,000,000 accelerator trials shared
across two sides.

## Dead ends

- A 20,000-trial RIS probe read d=19 and that reading was carried into an
  early draft. A 50,000-trial packaging search and an independent
  2,000,000-trial accelerator search both went lower, to 18, so the probe was
  the soft measurement rather than the code being unusually good. The early
  kd^2/n estimate of 12.69 was computed from that probe and is wrong; at d=18
  the figure is 11.39.
- This is the failure the board's own note on notes/562-18-19.md records: a
  distance read at one budget is not a distance. The 20,000-trial reading of
  19 was soft, and the entry claims d <= 18 from its own witness with no
  exactness asserted. What that re-measurement does NOT do is undercut the
  published d = 19: the code measured here is not the published code, which is
  the correction above.
- Left open, and now settled by the correction above: the sibling B-support
  also gives n=512, k=18, w=8 at this lattice size, and an earlier revision
  left open which of the two codes the published [[512,18,19]] denotes. It is
  neither. The published tile is a third support pair,
  g={(0,1),(1,0),(1,1),(3,3)}, recovered from the paper's own source rather
  than from any board entry. No claim is made about the sibling.

## Reproduction

research/local2d/boundary_engine.build_planar(16, 16, S_F_TILE4,
S_G_TILE4_MAIN), with S_F_TILE4=((0,0),(0,3),(2,2),(3,0)) and
S_G_TILE4_MAIN=((0,2),(1,3),(2,0),(3,3)), both module constants of
research/local2d/boundary_engine.py. This yields n=512, k=18, check weight 8.
The layout is research/local2d/planar.grid_coordinates(16, 16,
kept=info['kept_qubits']) with layers=2, giving 512 coordinates, 256 distinct
sites, 2 qubits per site and interaction radius 4.2426 = 3*sqrt(2), which is
over the 4.0 single-layer cap and inside the 7.0 bilayer cap. The same call at
(21,22) reproduces codes/924-18-31.json check-for-check, so the calibration is
reproducible from committed code.

To reproduce the identification in the correction section instead, call the
same builder with the published B-support spelled out literally:
build_planar(16, 16, S_F_TILE4, ((0,1),(1,0),(1,1),(3,3))) gives d <= 19 at
16x16 and, at 12x12, d <= 13. Note that this build emits redundant
stabilizers of weight 12-18 on the Z side; all of them lie in the row space of
the weight-8 rows (rank 247 either way), so dropping the rows of weight > 8
recovers the paper's weight-8 stabilizer set.
