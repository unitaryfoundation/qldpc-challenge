# [[682,62,100]] — cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal

No board entry at any blocklength reaches `k >= 62` and `d >= 100` together. At
`n <= 682` with `d >= 100` and row weight at most 20 there are three entries and
every one of them has `k = 2`. This code supersedes my own `[[682,62,98]]` at
row weight 24, which it dominates on both axes.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`,
so the dimension is a property of a polynomial gcd and can be chosen rather than
waited for. Building a divisor `g` of known degree and drawing `a` and `b` from
its ideal fixes `k = 2 deg g` on demand. The target was the band at `n` near 700
where the board carries high distance only at `k = 2`.

## What was searched

`m = 341 = 11 * 31`, with `ord_2(341) = 10`. The cyclotomic cosets of
`x^341 - 1` over GF(2) number 38: one of size 1, thirty-one of size 10 and six
of size 5. The divisor is assembled from coset minimal polynomials over
GF(2^10) to reach degree 31, and `a` and `b` are drawn as low-weight codewords
of its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, giving weight 8 and weight 12.

Triage read 109 at 20,000 RIS trials. Confirmation settled it at 100, which is
the usual direction and size of that correction in this family.

## Evidence trail

Divisor degree 31, so `k = 62`. Both single-block gcds, `deg gcd(a, x^m - 1)`
and `deg gcd(b, x^m - 1)`, equal 31 as well, so neither circulant block carries
a kernel wider than `k` requires.

Three independent mechanisms, none of which reaches below the claim:

- General RIS, 24 random information-set seeds at 2,000,000 trials each
  (31,061 s): lightest logical weight 100 on both X and Z, with the per-seed
  spread running 100 to 104 and four seeds landing on 100.
- Orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the 682 qubits, with the folded
  code handed to a distance search (`research/foldbound.py`): lightest validated
  lift weight 110, at 31 orbits of size 11.
- Single-block circulant search, 10 seeds at 600,000 trials: nothing under 100.
  A logical supported on one block of `H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]` is constrained
  only by that block, so it lives in one 341-column kernel that the general
  search essentially never samples.

The claim is `d = 100`, a witness-backed upper bound. Both witnesses are in the
submission and were re-checked against `H_X` and `H_Z` with plain integer
arithmetic: each has weight 100, commutes with the opposite checks, and lies
outside the rowspace of its own.

## Dead ends

The fold is what decides this family, and the cheap bounds do not stand in for
it. `[[682,102,85]]` at row weight 16 from the same sweep passed the
quotient-lift and single-block bounds and then folded to weight 44, on the same
partition of 31 orbits of size 11 that clears this code at 110. Its sibling
`[[682,102,90]]` at row weight 22 shares its modulus and its divisor degree of
51, and folds at 110. So the fold outcome is a property of the particular `a`
and `b` rather than of `m` and `deg g`, and no design argument substitutes for
running the fold on each draw.

Low dimension in this family dies on the fold for the same reason. Twenty draws
at `k = 40` to `42` read 108 to 116 at triage and settled at 55 to 88, and the
best of them is dominated by `[[674,42,97]]` at row weight 13. High dimension
dies on the single block instead, where the BCH bound on the dimension-`deg g`
cyclic code falls from 120 at `k = 80` to 72 at `k = 180`. Degree 31 sits below
the first of those and well clear of the second.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 under Claude Code. `gf2_fast` from `verify/` for the accelerated
RIS and single-block searches, `verify/gf2.py` for GF(2) rank, nullspace and
logical bases, `research/foldbound.py` for the orbit fold, and
`verify/validate_candidate.py` for the verdict. Divisor assembly and the
information-set draw are described above rather than committed. The 24-seed
confirmation ran 31,061 s on 4 cores, about 35 core-hours.

## Reproduction

`m = 341`, `n = 682`, `k = 62`.

```
a(x) = x^35 + x^41 + x^152 + x^175 + x^176 + x^195 + x^227 + x^335
b(x) = x^24 + x^35 + x^45 + x^122 + x^136 + x^191 + x^192 + x^193 + x^197
     + x^218 + x^258 + x^274
```

`H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]` and `H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]`, with
`circ(p)[i, j] = 1` exactly when `j - i mod 341` is an exponent of `p`. The
exact supports are in `codes/682-62-100.json`.
