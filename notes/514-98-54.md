# [[514,98,54]] weight-20 cyclic generalized-bicycle from a designed divisor

## Direction & hypothesis

No board entry at n <= 514 reaches k >= 98 and d >= 54 at any row weight. A residue construction cannot aim there, since it fixes k between 20 and 34 whatever the ring. Because k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1), choosing a divisor of x^m - 1 and drawing a and b from its ideal selects the dimension instead, and here a degree-49 divisor gives k = 98.

Two facts govern the construction, both checked rather than assumed. x^m - 1 is squarefree over GF(2) only for odd m, which makes m = 257 usable and the even moduli of a residue sweep unusable. And the generators are low-weight codewords of the cyclic code the divisor generates, found by information-set decoding inside that ideal.

## What was searched

The divisor is a product of cyclotomic-coset minimal polynomials over GF(2^16), so its degree is chosen; sampling sparse polynomials and taking gcds with x^m - 1 lands on a target degree almost never and kept 0 of 12 draws in an earlier attempt. Sweeping deg g / m from 0.04 to 0.30 over many coset subsets at nine odd moduli produced 299 priced candidates; 245 were undominated after folding, which Pareto-thinning over n, k, d and w reduced to 32 genuinely distinct front points.

## Evidence trail

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound of 54, set by a weight-54 X witness; the Z side stands at 58.

    random information set, 24 seeds x 2e6      58   (median 61, maximum 63)
    single-block circulant, 8 seeds x 6e5       54   on X
    research/foldbound.py, uncapped             no folded logical found
    verify/validate_candidate.py                passed, not refuted

The first row and the second do not search the same space, and the gap between them is the whole correction here. This code was first submitted claiming 58 on the strength of the general search alone. A logical supported on one block of `H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]` is constrained only by that block, so it lives in a kernel of dimension `deg gcd(b, x^m - 1)`, which is 65 here against the 49 that `k` requires: only the common gcd sets the dimension, so `b` picked up one extra cyclotomic coset of size `ord_2(257) = 16` for free. A general information-set search draws across all 514 columns and essentially never lands inside that 65-dimensional subspace, which is why 24 seeds at 2,000,000 trials each plateaued 4 above the truth. Rejecting such a draw costs two polynomial gcds.

The symmetric fold finds nothing here, so it neither refutes the claim nor supports it. Both witnesses were re-verified against the committed matrices, in the kernel of the opposite checks and outside the row space of their own side. The gate reports no distance-only peers and no shared Weisfeiler-Leman signature.

## Dead ends

Row weight is this construction's price and the schema is its ceiling. `schema/code.schema.json` caps each check's support at 32 entries, so a heavier code cannot be submitted whatever it scores. Two candidates from this sweep passed full 24-seed gates at row weights 48 and 58, one scoring kd^2/n = 1493.1, and neither is submittable. Rebuilding the heavier one under the cap is impossible: the lightest codewords in its degree-101 divisor's ideal are weight 26 to 34, so any pair of generators sums to at least 52.

Every distance in this family falls as search depth rises, so triage figures are not findings. This code was screened at 69 by a 20,000-trial reading, gated at 58 and settled at 54, and siblings moved 62 to 52, 60 to 51, and 108 to 98 under the same treatment.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 under Claude Code. `verify/gf2_fast` for the random information-set search, `research/foldbound.py` for the symmetric fold, `verify/validate_candidate.py` for the gate. The divisor construction and its sweep are described above in enough detail to rewrite; they are not committed here. The 24-seed scan took 5,645s on eight cores.
