# [[514,66,63]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 257

At row weight 18 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 514` and `k >= 66` is 31, at `[[390,82,31]]` and row weight 16. This entry reaches 63. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 58, at `[[514,96,58]]` and row weight 22.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Here m is prime, so the quotient fold has no proper divisor to act on and is vacuous; only the single-block ceiling binds, and degree 33 sits below where it bites.

## What was searched

`m = 257` with `ord_2(257) = 16`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^257 - 1` over GF(2) number 17 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 3 cosets of total degree 33, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

The divisor carries the root-1 coset, so both polynomial weights are even by construction and `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd by design rather than by accident. Here `a` has weight 8 and `b` has weight 10.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^257 - 1) = 33`, `deg gcd(b, x^257 - 1) = 33` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^257 - 1) = 33`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 63. The per-seed spread: 2 seeds found 63, 2 seeds found 64, 4 seeds found 65, 3 seeds found 66, 5 seeds found 67, 8 seeds found 68. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 33 whose zeros are the 224 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 51, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 52 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 73. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier itself reaches, reads 66.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; each weighs 63, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Reproduction

`m = 257`, `n = 2m = 514`. With `a = {47, 50, 69, 78, 128, 149, 162, 223}` and `b = {24, 35, 41, 42, 48, 68, 100, 121, 122, 254}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/514-66-63.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
