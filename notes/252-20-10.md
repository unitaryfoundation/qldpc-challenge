# [[252,20,10]] — two-block group-algebra code over C21 ⋊ C6 (= C21 x S3)

## Direction & hypothesis

Cell `weight-6 x unrestricted`, CSS board, n between 221 and 300. The
weight-6 frontier there ran 224-18-12, 224-12-16, 234-26-7, 240-16-14,
254-14-16, 270-30-7, 288-16-16, 288-12-18, 294-10-20: at n <= 300 every
weight-6 entry with k >= 20 had d <= 7, and every one with d >= 10 had
k <= 18. The hypothesis was that two-block group-algebra (2BGA) codes on
non-abelian groups of order 111 to 150 could put a k = 20, d = 10 point into
that corner at check weight 6. [[252,20,10]] does: no board entry has
n <= 252, k >= 20, d >= 10 and check weight <= 6.

## What was searched

Random-support 2BGA codes H_X = [L(a) | R(b)], H_Z = [R(b)^T | L(a)^T] with
a, b three group elements each, both containing the identity (no loss of
generality: multiplying a or b by a group element permutes qubits and
checks). The group menu was every non-abelian group of order 111 to 200 the
kit builds from Cayley-table constructors: dihedral D_n, metacyclic
C_n ⋊_r C_k (up to two actions r per (n, k)), C_m x D_k, C_m x A4,
C_m x S4, C_m x A5, D_a x D_b, C_m x (C_n ⋊ C_k) for eight small metacyclic
factors, S5, and the split metacyclic presentations ZSZ(l1, l2, q) of
research/kit/nonabelian_lp.py.

Random supports almost never give usable k (a generic weight-3 element is
invertible on the higher-dimensional irreducible blocks of F_2[G]), so the
sweep ran a parallel k prefilter: 200,000 draws at 222 <= n <= 400
(seed 51), of which 198,731 had k < 14 and 888 were direct sums; the k
histogram of the 381 kept was 14:90, 16:159, 18:49, 20:38, 24:37, 28:3,
32:3, 44:2. Those 381 went through research/kit/search.screen with the
gf2_fast backend at 400 trials; 6 advanced the board frontier at screen
depth and were confirmed at 20,000 / 200,000 / 2,000,000 fast trials
(research/kit/surrogate.distance_rand_witness, pair depth 8). A second sweep
of 400,000 draws restricted to 222 <= n <= 300 with k >= 16 (seed 61) found
the same (n, k, d) point on two further presentations of the same order-126
group, which is what one expects when the code is a property of the group
rather than of the draw.

## Evidence trail

Witness-backed **upper bound** d <= 10, not an exact distance. The bound was
10 at 400 trials and never moved; both sides have a weight-10 logical.

| screen (gf2_fast, seed from fingerprint) | 400 | 10 |
| confirm | 20,000 | 10 |
| confirm | 200,000 | 10 |
| confirm | 2,000,000 | 10 |

| deep ladder, 3 fresh seeds (47789, 55708, 63627), gf2_fast | 10,000,000 each | 10, 10, 10 (X side) |

`verify/validate_candidate.py` (through research/kit/coordination.py's gate
wrapper) returned `passed: true`, `board_advancing: true`, `d_only_gain:
false`, label "advances the weight-6 x unrestricted board". Literature
novelty is unverified; board-relative novelty comes from
verify/validate_candidate.py (fingerprint and Weisfeiler-Leman dedup
against every board entry found nothing).

Two other draws on groups of the same order, MC(21,6,2) and C6 x (C7 ⋊ C3),
reached the same [[252,20,10]] through 2,000,000 trials and were not filed
separately (same parameters, same cell).

## Dead ends

- Cyclic generalized-bicycle codes with a designed divisor g | x^m - 1 at
  check weight 6 (weight-3 a and b): at odd m from 111 to 200 and deg g >= 8
  there are essentially no weight-3 multiples of g (the residue table
  x^i mod g gives none for almost every (m, g)); the few that exist are
  lifts from a subring Z_{m/s} and give direct sums. Weight-4 multiples
  exist in quantity only for deg g <= 22, and 4,000 of those at 300 <= n <=
  400 (k 20 to 100) produced nothing that advanced the weight-8 frontier
  even at 400 trials.
- Random-support 2BGA at check weight 8, 16,000 draws across 222 <= n <=
  400: 15,629 had k < 18; the 4 screen-depth survivors collapsed to
  d <= 17 by 20,000 trials (dominated by 252-20-21, 292-22-23, 336-24-24,
  378-32-19).
- Random-support bivariate bicycle codes at check weight 6 (8,000 draws at
  300 <= n <= 400) and 8 (5,000 draws): 7,971 and 4,981 had k < 12 / 16;
  nothing advanced.
- Non-abelian lifted products with 1 x 2 bases: weight-(3,2) entries (check
  weight 8), 5,000 draws on groups of order 45 to 80, nothing advancing;
  weight-(2,2) entries (check weight 6) give k = |G| + 6 at d = 6 (e.g.
  [[400,86,6]]), which the board's 258-88-6 already dominates.
- 2BGA coset codes (Aydin-Tamo-Barg) with weight-3 supports on S5, A6,
  S5 x C2, S6, A5 x C3, A5 x C2, S4 x C_m: 3,000 draws, 2,869 had k < 12,
  nothing advancing.

## Reproduction

The group is research/kit/nonabelian_lp.py's `zsz(21, 6, 8)`, i.e.
research/kit/group_algebra.py's `metacyclic(21, 6, 8)`: C_21 ⋊ C_6 with
y x y^-1 = x^8, element x^i y^j at index 6 i + j, identity at 0. Since
8 = -1 mod 3 and 8 = 1 mod 7, the action inverts the 3-part of C_21 and
fixes the 7-part, so the group is C_21 x S_3 (order 126). With x = (1, 0),
y = (0, 1):

a = 1 + x^8 + x^20 y^3   (indices 0, 48, 123),
b = 1 + x^11 y^2 + x^19 y^4  (indices 0, 68, 118).

```python
import sys; sys.path[:0] = ["research/kit", "verify"]
from nonabelian_lp import zsz
from group_algebra import build_2bga
from css import compute_k
mul = zsz(21, 6, 8)
HX, HZ = build_2bga(mul, [0, 48, 123], [0, 68, 118])   # n = 252, k = 20
assert compute_k(HX, HZ) == 20
```

Check sets match codes/252-20-10.json row for row in this order. Witness
search: `surrogate.distance_rand_witness(HX, HZ, trials=2_000_000,
seed=51000003 + 2_000_000, backend="fast", pair_depth=8)` returns a
weight-10 logical; the numpy `lightest_logical` at 1,500 trials with seeds
51 (X) and 52 (Z) gives the two witnesses in the JSON.
