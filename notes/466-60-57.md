# [[466,60,57]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 233

At row weight 20 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 466` and `k >= 60` is 31, at `[[390,82,31]]` and row weight 16. This entry reaches 57. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 46, at `[[466,118,46]]` and row weight 30.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 30 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 233` with `ord_2(233) = 29`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^233 - 1` over GF(2) number 9 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 2 cosets of total degree 30, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

The divisor carries the root-1 coset, so both polynomial weights are even by construction and `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd by design rather than by accident. Here `a` has weight 8 and `b` has weight 12.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^233 - 1) = 30`, `deg gcd(b, x^233 - 1) = 30` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^233 - 1) = 30`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 57. The per-seed spread: 4 seeds found 57, 5 seeds found 58, 7 seeds found 59, 7 seeds found 60, 1 seed found 61. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 30 whose zeros are the 203 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 28, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 29 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 68. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier itself reaches, reads 58.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; each weighs 57, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends

- [[466,60,56]] from the same front is lighter at row weight 18 and stops at 56, so neither settles the other.
- This design point was swept to convergence before anything was gated: 1,342 draws left the front at row weight 18 reaching 66 and row weight 20 reaching 68 at triage, with nothing lighter than weight 18 beating the board at all. Both were gated and both survive, because the lighter draw stops lower, so neither settles the other and the cell held no entry before them.

The reading before this gate was 68, against a gated 57. Every member of this front moved the same way, which is why all of them were gated rather than filed off the sweep.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings and single-block searches come from the repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension; divisor assembly, coset selection and the information-set draws are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite.

## Reproduction

`m = 233`, `n = 2m = 466`. With `a = {25, 63, 117, 125, 164, 179, 192, 227}` and `b = {10, 16, 19, 36, 73, 82, 88, 109, 124, 168, 196, 228}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/466-60-57.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
