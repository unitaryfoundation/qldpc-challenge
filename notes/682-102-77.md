# [[682,102,77]] — cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal

No board entry at any blocklength combines `k >= 102`, `d >= 77` and row weight
at most 16. At `n <= 682` with that dimension and weight the best distance on
the board is 44, so this code reaches 77 in the same cell. It does not compete
with `[[682,102,90]]` at row weight 22: that entry has more distance and this
one has lighter checks, and neither dominates the other.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`,
`k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd
and can be chosen rather than waited for. The aim here was the light-weight end
of the same design point that produced `[[682,102,90]]`: a degree-51 divisor at
`m = 341`, with `a` and `b` drawn as sparsely as the ideal allows.

## What was searched

`m = 341 = 11 * 31`, with `ord_2(341) = 10`. The divisor is assembled from
cyclotomic-coset minimal polynomials over GF(2^10) to reach degree 51, and `a`
and `b` are drawn as low-weight codewords of its ideal by Prange
information-set decoding. This draw has weight 8 in each, against 10 and 12 for
the weight-22 sibling.

Every draw was screened against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold
bounds before any confirmation time was spent.

## Evidence trail

Divisor degree 51, so `k = 102`. Both single-block gcds equal 51, matching
`deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so neither circulant block carries a kernel wider
than `k` requires.

The binding bound is the fold, not the general search, and that is the whole
shape of the evidence here:

- General RIS, 24 random information-set seeds at 2,000,000 trials each
  (16,105 s): lightest logical weight 86 on Z and 90 on X, per-seed spread 86 to
  93 with a median of 92.
- Orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the 682 qubits
  (`research/foldbound.py`): a validated lift of weight 77 at 31 orbits of size
  11, reproduced on two independent runs.
- Single-block circulant search, 10 seeds at 600,000 trials: nothing under 77.

So the claim is `d = 77`, witnessed on X by the fold at weight 77 and on Z by
the gate at weight 86, both as upper bounds. The general search cannot reach
the fold's subspace at any depth, which is why 24 seeds at two million trials
each sit 9 above the real value. Both witnesses are in the submission and were
re-checked against `H_X` and `H_Z` with plain integer arithmetic.

## Dead ends

Row weight is not a free parameter in this family. Two other draws at the same
`m` and the same divisor degree, both at weight 16, fold to 44 and to 55 and are
worthless; this one folds to 77. The fold outcome is a property of the
particular `a` and `b` rather than of `m` and `deg g`, so no design argument
substitutes for running the fold on each draw. A record of killed pairs is worth
keeping for that reason: the weight-16 draw that folds to 44 reappeared at the
top of a later sweep's own ranking and had to be recognised rather than
re-measured.

The cheap bounds do not stand in for the fold either. Both of those dead draws
passed the quotient-lift and single-block screens first.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 under Claude Code. `gf2_fast` from `verify/` for the accelerated
RIS and single-block searches, `verify/gf2.py` for GF(2) rank, nullspace and
logical bases, `research/foldbound.py` for the orbit fold, and
`verify/validate_candidate.py` for the verdict. Divisor assembly and the
information-set draw are described above rather than committed. The 24-seed
confirmation ran 16,105 s on 6 cores, about 27 core-hours.

## Reproduction

`m = 341`, `n = 682`, `k = 102`.

```
a(x) = x^32 + x^61 + x^70 + x^202 + x^224 + x^238 + x^321 + x^323
b(x) = x^57 + x^98 + x^163 + x^210 + x^224 + x^232 + x^265 + x^269
```

`H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]` and `H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]`, with
`circ(p)[i, j] = 1` exactly when `j - i mod 341` is an exponent of `p`. The
exact supports are in `codes/682-102-77.json`.
