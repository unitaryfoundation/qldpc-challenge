## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Here m is prime, so the quotient fold has no proper divisor to act on and is vacuous; only the single-block ceiling binds, and degree 17 sits below where it bites.

## What was searched

`m = 257` with `ord_2(257) = 16`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^257 - 1` over GF(2) number 17 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 2 cosets of total degree 17, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

The divisor carries the root-1 coset, so both polynomial weights are even by construction and `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd by design rather than by accident. Here `a` has weight 6 and `b` has weight 6.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^257 - 1) = 17`, `deg gcd(b, x^257 - 1) = 17` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^257 - 1) = 17`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 64, above the claim: see below. The per-seed spread: 1 seed found 64, 1 seed found 66, 3 seeds found 67, 5 seeds found 68, 5 seeds found 69, 8 seeds found 70, 1 seed found 71. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 17 whose zeros are the 240 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 63, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 64 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 72. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim.
