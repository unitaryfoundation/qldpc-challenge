# [[674,168,74]]

A prime generalized-bicycle code on Z_337: `H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]`,
`H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]`, with `a` and `b` of weight 15, so the check
weight is 30.

## Why this ring

For a generalized-bicycle code on a cyclic ring, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`,
so the logical dimension costs one Euclid rather than a rank computation. For
`m = 337`, which is prime with `ord_337(2) = 21`, `x^m - 1` splits as `(x+1)`
times sixteen irreducibles of degree 21. Every divisor therefore has degree
`21j` or `21j + 1`, and `k` lands on the ladder `2(21j)` or `2(21j + 1)`. This
entry sits at `j = 4`: `deg gcd = 84`, so `k = 168` by construction and before
any matrix is built.

The rate is 168/674 = 0.249. The board's other entries at this blocklength sit
at comparable rates, which is what makes the regime worth searching: `k` scales
with `n` while the check weight stays bounded.

## The distance, and how far it was pushed

The search that produced the candidate reported 78 at 20,000,000 trials. That
was wrong, and the ladder below is the correction:

| trials | lightest logical found |
|---|---|
| 200,000 | 84 |
| 2,000,000 | 80 |
| 8,000,000 | 78 |
| 20,000,000 | 74 |
| 50,000,000 | 74 |
| 150,000,000 | 74 |

Three consecutive rungs at 74 across a factor of seven in budget. The claim here
is 74 and it is an upper bound: the witness proves `d <= 74`, and nothing in the
ladder proves the converse.

Worth recording that the 20,000,000-trial reading in the originating search was
four heavier than the truth, so a single deep reading is not a converged one.
The ladder is the evidence, not its last rung.

## Witness

The reported witness is validated independently of the search that found it:
it lies in the kernel of the opposite check matrix, and it raises that matrix's
rank, so it is a nontrivial logical rather than a stabiliser.
