# [[662,62,93]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 331

At row weight 20 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 662` and `k >= 62` is 89, at `[[662,62,89]]` and row weight 18. This entry reaches 93.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 31 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 331` with `ord_2(331) = 30`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^331 - 1` over GF(2) number 12 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 2 cosets of total degree 31, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

The divisor carries the root-1 coset, so both polynomial weights are even by construction and `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd by design rather than by accident. Here `a` has weight 10 and `b` has weight 10.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^331 - 1) = 31`, `deg gcd(b, x^331 - 1) = 31` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^331 - 1) = 31`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 93. The per-seed spread: 2 seeds found 93, 1 seed found 95, 3 seeds found 96, 5 seeds found 97, 5 seeds found 98, 7 seeds found 99, 1 seed found 101. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 31 whose zeros are the 300 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 66, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 67 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 107. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier itself reaches, reads 98.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; the X witness weighs 95 and the Z witness weighs 93, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

Two draws in the neighbouring [[662,60]] cell at row weight 20 were gated alongside this one and both read 94, so the degree-55 and degree-56 design points at m=331 land within a few points of each other.

The reading before this gate was 107, against a gated 93.

## Tools

## Reproduction

`m = 331`, `n = 2m = 662`. With `a = {49, 50, 85, 115, 181, 182, 248, 278, 313, 314}` and `b = {14, 34, 55, 80, 89, 103, 151, 161, 162, 281}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/662-62-93.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
