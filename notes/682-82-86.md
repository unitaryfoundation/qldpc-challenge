# [[682,82,86]] — weight-12 Frobenius-paired cyclic generalized bicycle on Z_341

## Direction & hypothesis

Target: the weight ≤ 12 headline, the best kd²/n among codes with check
weight at most 12. The leader was `codes/674-44-95.json` ([[674,44,95]],
w = 12, kd²/n = 589.17): a cyclic generalized bicycle over Z_337 whose two
weight-6 polynomials share a degree-22 divisor of x^337 − 1. Its note lists
the moduli it swept (127, 251, 257, 331, 337); 341 was not among them.

Z_341 is the best-factored modulus near the n ≤ 700 cap: ord_341(2) = 10, so
x^341 − 1 splits into x + 1, six quintics (order 31) and 31 decics (30 of
full order 341, one of order 11). The construction behind
`codes/682-172-78.json` (w = 28) carries over to weight 6 + 6. Take a word a
invariant under j → 32 j (mod 341). Its Fourier values a(ζ^s) then lie in
GF(32) instead of GF(1024), so a full-order decic divides a with probability
2⁻⁵ instead of 2⁻¹⁰, and the Frobenius images a(x⁴), a(x⁸) have the same
zero set. So (a, a(x⁸)) shares the whole divisor gcd(a, x^341 − 1) for free
and k = 2 deg gcd. The hypothesis was that weight-6 symmetric words with a
large zero set exist, and that the distance of the w = 28 family survives at
w = 12.

## What was searched

All of it, not a sample. Weight-6 words invariant under j → 32 j are a fixed
point 0 (fixed points are the multiples of 11) plus either three more fixed
points and one pair-orbit {j, 32j}, or one fixed point and two pair-orbits.
Words with no fixed point are divisible by x^31 − 1 and degenerate. That is
987,350 words with one fixed point pinned at 0 (rotation by 11·Z_341
preserves the family).

1. Zero sets, exactly, by evaluating a at one root per cyclotomic coset in
   GF(1024). Kept: degree ≥ 21, with every vanishing factor either x + 1 or a
   full-order decic. Words vanishing on a quintic or on the order-11 decic
   have periodic low-weight logicals and were dropped. Left: 193,950 words —
   degree 21 (k = 42) 148,050; degree 31 (k = 62) 36,600; degree 41 (k = 82)
   8,700; degree 51 (k = 102) 600.
2. Equivalence: a multiplier j → μj (μ a unit) and re-pinning another fixed
   point at 0 give permutation-equivalent codes. Canonical forms leave 433
   classes: 321 / 86 / 24 / 2 by degree.
3. Screen: both Frobenius partners b = a(x⁴), a(x⁸) for both degree-51
   classes, all 24 degree-41 classes and the first part of the degree-31
   list, at 4,000 RIS trials per code (gf2_fast accelerator, pair depth 8).
   The screen was stopped there to spend the budget on the ladder. 92 codes
   cleared the distance needed to beat 589.17 (d ≥ 71 at k = 82, d ≥ 63 at
   k = 102, d ≥ 81 at k = 62); 23 of the 24 degree-41 classes passed.
4. Ladder: the top five by screen bound at 1,000,000 trials each, then the
   two best at 10,000,000+ trials.

## Dead ends

- Weight 4 + 4 (w = 8) and 3 + 3 (w = 6) with the same symmetric pairing:
  exhaustive (9 classes at weight 4, one at weight 3), every code has d ≤ 17
  at k ≥ 20. Far below what the w ≤ 8 (123.14) and w ≤ 6 (30.48) headlines
  need, so this family does not reach those cells.
- Unrestricted (non-symmetric) weight-4 words on Z_341, all 6.5 million
  enumerated: the words with zero-set degree ≥ 16 are the multiples of
  11·Z_341 and pairs of them give d ≤ 11.
- Weight-4 multiples of a degree-22 divisor on Z_337: none exist
  (exhaustive), so the leader's construction has no w = 8 version there.
- Random sampling of the same family before the enumeration (one seed,
  30 min) surfaced only a k = 42 word, [[682,42,≤101]] (628): the
  high-degree classes are too rare to hit by sampling and were found only by
  enumerating.

## Reproduction

Let C(S) be the 341 × 341 binary circulant whose row i has ones at columns
(i + s) mod 341 for s ∈ S. With a and b above, H_X = [C(a) | C(b)] and
H_Z = [C(b)ᵀ | C(a)ᵀ]. Equivalently b = {8j mod 341 : j ∈ a}. The
enumeration is: all 6-subsets of Z_341 containing 0 that are unions of
multiples of 11 and orbits {j, 32j}, zero sets by evaluation at one
representative per cyclotomic coset of 2 in GF(2^10).
