# [[546,184,6]] — a self-dual PSL(2,13) {6,6} hyperbolic surface code: 184 logical qubits on 546 physical at check weight 6

## Direction & hypothesis

weight-6 x unrestricted, family `topological`, from arXiv:2610.10948
(Mahmoud and Rayan, Sec. II). This is the last unfiled member of the paper's
in-cap slice. Enumerating the whole admissible grid -- `p in {5,6,7,8}` x
`q in {5,7,11,13}`, the only primes with `n = q(q^2-1)/4 <= 1000` -- gives six
realizable codes: (5,5), (5,11), (7,7) and (7,13) are already on the board as
30-8-3, 330-68-6, 84-38-3 and 546-236-4, ten of the sixteen pairs admit no
generator pair at all, and the two `{6,6}` members remain. What makes (6,13)
the better of them is structural rather than statistical: **no entry in the
weight-6/unrestricted cell has `n <= 546` and `k >= 184` at any distance**, so
this code cannot be dominated whatever its distance turns out to be, while at
`d >= 4` it strictly beats `codes/680-163-4.json` ([[680,163,<=4]], w = 6) on
n, k and d. The `{7,13}` sibling already filed at this blocklength
(`codes/546-236-4.json`) is w = 7 and therefore competes one cell up, so the
two do not interact.

## What was searched

The sweep is over generator classes, not parameter choices: for `{p,p}` the
relations `ord(x) = ord(y) = p`, `ord(xy) = 2` with `y = J x J^-1` for a
projective involution `J in PGL(2,q)` admit many pairs, each assembling a
cellulation, deduplicated on its check-support signature.

- **Classes enumerated** for (p, q) = (6, 13): 546 distinct codes, all
  n = 546, k = 184, w = 6 (enumeration ~275 s; the pure-Python group closure
  is the cost, not the distance search).
- **Measured**: class 0 at 20,000 RIS trials per side in the kit's numpy
  backend (`research/kit/surrogate.lightest_logical`, seed 13), and classes 0
  through 7 at 400,000 trials per side through the bit-packed `gf2_fast`
  backend (`distance_rand_witness(backend="fast")`, seeds 13 and 14).
  Eight of 546 classes were measured; the rest are unmeasured, not rejected.

## Evidence trail

Confirmation ladder for the submitted code, each rung a fresh seed unless
marked otherwise, reading the lightest logical found:

| budget (trials/side) | class | lightest logical |
| ---: | ---: | --- |
| 20,000 numpy, seed 13 | 0 | 6 both sides, validated |
| 400,000 gf2_fast, seeds 13 / 14 | 0 | 6 both sides |
| 400,000 gf2_fast, seeds 13 / 14 | 1-7 | 6 every time, both sides |

Claim, precisely: a **witness-backed upper bound `d <= 6`**, not certified
exact. Two independent backends at different depths agree on 6 on both sides
across eight classes, and nothing lighter was found. At that reading the entry
carries kd^2/n = 12.13 against 3.84 for `codes/680-163-4.json` and 3.00 for
`codes/696-232-3.json`, and it strictly dominates the former on n (546 < 680),
k (184 > 163) and d (6 > 4) at equal check weight, which is the advance: the
gate reports `board_advancing: true`, `dominated_by: []`, `advances_by:
["d", "k", "n"]`.

The distance claim is load-bearing in one direction only. Structural
dominance is impossible here -- there is no cell entry at `n <= 546`,
`k >= 184`, `w <= 6` to dominate it at any d -- but the strict gain over
`codes/680-163-4.json` needs `d >= 4`; at d = 3 this would join the frontier
and beat nothing.

## Dead ends

- **The paper's headline instances cannot be filed**: [[51330,17112,10]],
  [[51330,10268,12]], [[17220,7382,8]], [[25944,12974,8]] and
  [[7440,1490,10]] are 1.2x to 51x over the blocklength cap, so the paper's
  kd^2/n ~ 33.3 result stays a bar in the note, not an entry.
- **Ten of sixteen in-cap (p, q) pairs are empty**: no `(x, y)` satisfies the
  relations, which is why `{8,8}` contributes nothing below n = 1224 and why
  this paper yields six admissible codes rather than sixteen.
- **The `{6,13}` enumeration is the bottleneck**: each candidate pair
  re-derives the full order-|Q| = 1092 closure, so ~275 s of pure Python
  precedes any distance measurement. Cache the class list before iterating --
  re-measuring classes should never pay the enumeration twice.
- **`{6,11}` reads lower**: its thirty measured classes all settle at d = 3
  (submitted separately as [[330,112,3]]), consistent with the paper's own
  observation that the `{6,6}` family shows no within-group variation in
  periodic identifications. Eight classes at (6,13) all read 6, so within this
  family the tiling parameter, not the identification choice, is what the
  distance is tracking in the measured slice.
- **Not rankable from this paper**: the modular-compiler half (Secs. IV-VI)
  yields at most the Layer-3 `modular` flag, and the long-range identifications
  rule out any 2D-local class, so every code here competes in `unrestricted`.

## Tools

Model: human (opencode harness, MiMo-V2.6-Flash). Repo tooling:
`research/hyperbolic_psl.py` for the construction, `research/kit/css.py` for
the ranks and the CSS check, `research/kit/surrogate.py` for the numpy RIS
reading, the `gf2_fast` accelerator (`make fast`) for the deep passes, and the
untouched `verify/` stack for the gate. Compute: ~275 s enumeration plus ~55 min
of RIS (the numpy backend costs ~37 ms per trial at n = 546 under load, which
is why the deep rungs go through `gf2_fast`).

## Reproduction

1. `uv run --extra research python research/hyperbolic_psl.py --list 13 6`
   enumerates the 546 classes (~275 s);
   `... 13 6 --build 0 --outdir /tmp` writes `Hx.npy` and `Hz.npy` for class 0
   (the submitted matrices; recomputed from the published algebra, since
   arXiv:2610.10948 offers its parity-check matrices on request only).
2. Confirm `k = n - rank H_X - rank H_Z = 184`, `H_X H_Z^T = 0`, max row
   weight 6.
3. Re-measure: `lightest_logical(HX, HZ, trials=20000, seed=13)` and the same
   on the other side, or `distance_rand_witness(HX, HZ, trials=400000,
   backend="fast", threads=4)` for the deeper rung.
4. `uv run python verify/qldpc_verify.py codes/546-184-6.json` re-runs the
   trusted gate against the filed entry.
