# [[994,142,40]] — pair-partition CPM code on a 6 x 14 pair-closed template, weight-8 checks, rate 1/7, lift N = 71

## Direction & hypothesis

Cell: CSS, unrestricted, weight 8, extended tier (n <= 1000 with w <= 8 and
d <= 40). The pair-partition (PP) construction of Okada and Kasai
(arXiv:2607.14091) gives CSS orthogonality from paired-difference equations
on CPM exponents; they apply it to complete J x L templates. Lee, Okada,
Maskara, Kasai and Zhou (arXiv:2609.30069) count the design rate as
R = 1 - J_tot/L_stab and name quantum-compatible irregular templates as a
natural direction. Our weight-8 entries so far use an irregular 5 x 12
template at rate 1/6 (codes/996-166-32.json, codes/996-166-34.json), and
codes/996-166-36.json by @jacksongquera uses the same template.

Hypothesis: at weight 8 and n near 1000 a lower rate with more degree-4
columns can buy distance faster than it loses rate. A typical-distance
estimate for the LDPC ensemble with the template's column-degree mix
(the weight-distribution estimate of Gallager, Low-Density Parity-Check
Codes, MIT Press 1963) puts a 6 x 14 pair-closed template (rate 1/7,
3 of 7 column pairs of degree 4) above the distance a rate-1/7 code needs to
pass k d^2 / n = 216 at n = 994. This is an estimate, not a bound. This code:
142 * 40^2 / 994 = 228.6 (upper bound, from d <= 40); no lower bound is
claimed.

**Frontier:** Among entries with max check weight <= 8 (and n <= 1000), the
highest k d^2 / n on the board today is 216.0 (codes/996-166-36.json by
@jacksongquera). This entry's is 228.6 as an upper bound (d <= 40). No lower
bound is claimed, so the true score may be lower.

**Technique (not ours):** the pair-partition rule, its pairing arrays and the
CPM lift are Okada and Kasai's (arXiv:2607.14091); the design-rate count and
the permanent-vector upper bound (App. A of arXiv:2609.30069) are from Lee,
Okada, Maskara, Kasai and Zhou (arXiv:2609.30069); quasi-cyclic CSS
LDPC codes from circulant permutation matrices go back to Hagiwara and Imai
(arXiv:quant-ph/0701020), and CSS pairs of two irregular LDPC codes were
analysed by Hagiwara and Imai (arXiv:0801.1361).

**Board precedents:** the same commutation rule at weight 8:
codes/996-166-36.json and codes/912-234-22.json by @jacksongquera,
codes/904-230-22.json by @vprusso, our codes/996-166-32.json and
codes/996-166-34.json. Literature novelty is not claimed.

## What was searched

Template (the same on the X and the Z side). The 14 block columns form 7
column pairs {0,1}, ..., {12,13}; each of the 6 block rows is the union of 4
of the 7 pairs, so every check has weight 8 and every X-row / Z-row overlap
is a union of whole pairs. Rows by pairs: [0,1,2,5], [0,2,3,5], [0,3,4,6],
[1,3,5,6], [1,2,4,6], [0,1,2,4]: pairs {0,1}, {2,3}, {4,5} have qubit degree
4 and the other four pairs degree 3. Design rate 1 - 12/14 = 1/7. We
enumerated the X = Z pair-closed 6 x 14 templates (33 classes with a
permanent-vector ceiling, App. A of arXiv:2609.30069, of 48 or more) and searched each for pairings with
our couple solver (a depth-first search for pairings whose exponent
equations keep free dimensions), one cyclic symmetry of the template at a time, at least 3
free exponent dimensions and no forced parallel-column forms. Two classes
had solutions; this template is the class whose solutions showed no built-in
light logicals in a short screen. This pairing is one of the
solutions with 3 free exponent dimensions found under an order-2 symmetry.

Lifts and triage. Random points of the solution space mod 71, 4-cycle free,
k = 142 (= design) checked by rank, permanent-vector upper bound 52 for this
draw (56 for the template). A short Stern screen (8 s per side) removed draws with a logical of
weight 30 or less; the survivors entered a funnel that kills a code at its
first logical of weight 38 or less (142 * 38^2 / 994 = 206.3 <= 216.0).
Of the 40 lifts of this template class that entered the funnel, 29 showed
a logical of weight 38 or less (28 of them within 0.4 to 2,045 s of search,
one after 25,563 s), 3 passed 6 CPU-hours per side without one (this lift, whose search
continued to the totals below, and two others still under check), and 8 were stopped undecided.

recover it; the filed X witness is the lightest saved one (41). The claim
d <= 40 rests on the filed Z witness. Every filed witness
was re-checked in a separate script: weight as reported, zero syndrome
against the opposite checks, outside the row space of its own checks; the
board's verify/qldpc_verify.py passes on the file.

Programs: our isd_fast (Stern, p = 2, collision windows 16 and 14) and our
isd_trace (a second Stern implementation). Runs of the two programs with
the same seed visit the same information sets, so they are counted once in
the CPU figure (15.4 independent CPU-hours; 21.4 counting every run).

Claim: d <= 40, a witness-backed upper bound (confidence upper_bound). No
lower bound is claimed; the search covers odd and even weights (odd-weight
logicals occur in this template: 35, 37 and 39 were seen on other lifts),
so a lighter logical may exist.

## Dead ends

- Template class 21 of the same shape: built-in light logicals (one qubit
  on each column of 4 pairs, weight 8) in every lift.
- 6 x 14 with X != Z, and 9 x 21 / 12 x 28 templates with a built-in cyclic
  symmetry: no couple solutions with 3 or more free dimensions.
- Our 5 x 12 template at n = 996: about 130 further lifts on 2026-10-28/29, all with
  a logical of weight 36 or less.
