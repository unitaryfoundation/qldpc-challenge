# [[630,28,60]]: distance revision of the board's [[630,28,90]] entry

## Revision history

[[630,28,60]] supersedes the board's [[630,28,90]] entry. The code, its checks and its
original provenance are unchanged; only the distance claim is corrected. Both
generator polynomials, reduced modulo x^21 - 1, give a generalized-bicycle quotient
code on Z_21 (42 qubits, 16 logical qubits) with a weight-4 logical on each side.
Multiplying that logical by the norm word N_15(x) = 1 + x^21 + ... + x^294 (weight 15)
lifts it to a weight-60 logical of the full code, on the X side and on the Z side, so
the previous witness-backed bound d <= 90 was overstated and the honest parameter set
is [[630,28,60]]. The headline falls from kd^2/n = 360.0 to 160.0. Each lifted
operator is carried in `codes/630-28-60.json` with the budget it was found at. The
distance remains an upper bound, not an exact claim.

## Evidence

The lift is exact algebra applied to a sampled quotient search. Let m = 315 = q r and
let A_q, B_q be the circulants obtained by summing the columns of the first q rows of
circ(a), circ(b) modulo q. The quotient code is H_X = [A_q | B_q], H_Z = [B_q^T | A_q^T].
A logical u of it lifts to the vector that repeats every coordinate of each block r
times (position p goes to p, p + q, ..., p + (r - 1) q), which is multiplication by
the norm word N_r. It has weight r |u|, lies in the kernel of the opposite checks
because N_r (x^q - 1) = x^m - 1 = 0, and is not a stabilizer because N_r = 1 modulo
x^q - 1. The mechanism and its first use are written up in
`fieldnotes/2026-09-22-frontier-truth-gpu-audit.md` and `codes/682-182-66.json`.

Randomized information-set search (`lightest_logical` in `research/kit/surrogate.py`,
pure NumPy) found the weight-4 quotient logicals within 300 trials at seed 0.
Quotient supports, numbering the two blocks 0..20 and 21..41:

| side | quotient support (Z_21, 42 qubits) | lifted weight |
|---|---|---|
| X | 7, 15, 22, 33 | 60 |
| Z | 7, 18, 25, 33 | 60 |

Each lifted support was re-validated against the committed check matrices with the
repository's GF(2) routines (in the kernel of the opposite side's checks, outside the
row space of its own side), and `verify/qldpc_verify.py` accepts `codes/630-28-60.json`
with both witnesses at weight 60. A second, independent quotient agrees: reducing
modulo x^63 - 1 gives a 126-qubit quotient with 28 logical qubits and a weight-12
logical on each side, which lifts through N_5 to weight 60 again.

A rerun at 1,500 trials on each of seeds 0, 1 and 2 covered every proper divisor q of
315 whose quotient has logical qubits. Lifted weights, X / Z:

Nothing lifted below 60. The q = 7 lift is exactly 90, the value the entry

## Reproduction

{3, 7, 9, 15, 21, 35, 45, 63, 105}, find the lightest logical of each quotient with
`lightest_logical` on both sides, repeat each coordinate r = 315 / q times per block,
and check the result against the full H_X, H_Z.

## Original note

The text below is the note filed with the entry as [[630,28,90]]. Its distance and
score claims are superseded by the revision above and are kept as the record of the
original filing.

## [[630,28,90]] weight-22 cyclic generalized-bicycle

### Hypothesis

Cyclic generalized-bicycle codes at check weight 22 cover a wide blocklength
range, and the board's coverage of that range is uneven: there are stretches of
n where no entry sits near the (k, d) the family can reach. A broad randomized
sweep over sparse polynomial pairs, screened against the live board before any
expensive work, finds the gaps.

### Construction

    ring   x^315 - 1
    a(x) = x^16 + x^120 + x^151 + x^157 + x^158 + x^174 + x^208 + x^228 + x^259
    b(x) = x^27 + x^37 + x^41 + x^49 + x^73 + x^80 + x^86 + x^89 + x^95 + x^190 + x^237 + x^240 + x^262
    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]
    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

n = 2m, and k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1), computed by Euclid before any matrix
work so that most draws are discarded for free.

### What was swept

One loop draws a ring size and a weight split, samples the two sparse
polynomials, computes k by Euclid, and runs a cheap 60k-trial distance screen.
A draw survives only if the resulting (n, k, d, w) is undominated on the live
board snapshot. Surviving draws go through an escalating ladder of 400k, 2M and
8M random information-set trials, with the board comparison repeated at each
rung so a candidate that collapses is dropped as soon as it stops being
interesting.

### The single-block check

General RIS over all 2m columns is close to blind to a logical supported
entirely on one block. Such a vector u satisfies a(x) u(x) = 0 mod x^m - 1,
which puts u in the ideal generated by h = (x^m - 1) / gcd(a, x^m - 1), so it
can be searched directly in a space of dimension deg h rather than 2m. This
matters: a previous submission of mine survived 150M general RIS trials at
weight 74 and was then refuted by a weight-64 logical sitting entirely in one
block, which the ideal search finds in seconds. Every candidate here is checked
that way before the distance is believed, and any candidate whose single-block
bound comes in below the ladder is dropped rather than submitted.

### Distance ladder

Witness search runs on each side separately, so a run that happens to find one
side cannot leave the other without a witness. Both witnesses are re-checked
directly against H: in the kernel of the opposite matrix, and outside the row
space of its own.

### What it advances

Score kd^2/n = 360.0 at check weight 22. Undominated in its cell against
the board at submission time.

### Boundary

The distance is an upper bound from witness search, not a proof. Literature
novelty is unverified: cyclic GB codes are a classical family and these
parameters may appear in the 2BGA literature.
