# [[674,168,76]] — generalized bicycle with independent polynomials

## Direction & hypothesis

The aim was to search beyond the one-polynomial Frobenius restriction while retaining sparse circulant checks. This is the known generalized-bicycle (GB) family, as in [Panteleev–Kalachev, arXiv:1904.02703v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02703v3). It has two independently chosen sparse polynomials of weights 16 and 15, maximum check weight 31, and witnessed bound d <= 78. Its score is 168*78^2/674 = 1516.486647, 4.10398% above the posted [[682,172,76]] score 1456.703812. It uses weight-31 checks versus that incumbent's weight 28; the comparison is the witnessed-bound leaderboard metric, not a hardware result.

An explicit weight-31 stabilizer separates this code from the ordinary Frobenius UB subclass: all stabilizers of those codes have even weight. The [fieldnote](../fieldnotes/2026-09-24-mixed-projection-bicycle.md) gives the self-contained proof excluding a tensor product of arbitrary single-qubit Clifford gates followed by any qubit permutation. This uses a known parity invariant and the UB definition in [arXiv:2605.14173v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14173v1); it does not claim a new family or literature-wide novelty. There is no exact-distance or hardware-performance claim.

## What was searched

For three degree-84 cyclic ideals, the constructor intersected each ideal with the coefficient-fixed subspace under i -> 2^7*i modulo 337. This permutation has one fixed coordinate and 112 orbits of size three. Native GF(2) kernel calculation gives an 85-dimensional basis on 113 orbit coordinates. Randomized RREF on these condensed coordinates used 100,000 proposals per ideal, seeds 924960036, 924960049, and 924960067; expanded weights used orbit sizes 1 or 3.

Among these three ideals, only ideal 049 yielded a new sparse-word orbit in this constructor search: weight 16, first found at proposal 13, RREF row 34, seed 924960049. Pairing it with the known weight-15 word produced seven relative alignments with k=168 and weight 31. Two further cases paired the even word with its own Frobenius images. These 300,000 classical constructor proposals are not quantum-distance trials.

## Dead ends

The two even/even Frobenius cases fell to 66 and 65 in coefficient-fixed tests. Of the two mixed pairs given 100k comparisons, this pair returned 81 and the other returned 78. Earlier, a weight-30 UB candidate fell to 76 after 8M, and a different k=170 candidate fell to 71 after 2M+6M. Those separate audits are not included in this code's totals.

## Reproduction

Work over F_2[x]/(x^337+1), with

```text
A = [0, 1, 11, 34, 46, 48, 77, 80, 135, 201, 206, 226, 255, 266, 315, 333]
B = [0, 4, 15, 88, 118, 145, 150, 205, 210, 232, 251, 266, 303, 312, 323]
```

Let a and b be the binary polynomials with these supports and C(a)[r,c]=a[(c-r) mod 337]. Set HX=[C(a)|C(b)], HZ=[C(b)^T|C(a)^T]. Both have 337 rows of weight 31 and rank 253, so k=674−253−253=168. The X left/right column weights are 16/15; Z reverses them.

Integer bit i encodes the coefficient of x^i:

```text
g = gcd(a,b,x^337+1) = gcd(b,x^337+1) = 32650130462612855861996279
degree-21 factors of g = [2332367, 2420557, 2508455, 3612295]
gcd(a,x^337+1) = (x+1)*g = 54412013000919294326554393
```

The X full/left/right ranks are 253/252/253, and Z ranks 253/253/252. This block-projection inequality does not imply unequal X/Z distances: the block-swap/reversal involution exchanges the entire X and Z stabilizer spaces and logical sectors.

First filed as [[674,168,78]]. The verifier's RIS-fast refutation pass
(8,000,000 trials, seed 1504866454) found a weight-76 logical, below the

(verify/ris_gpu.py, recover mode, pair depth 8, 50,000,000 trials per side per

The lightest CPU-validated logical per side is a weight-77 X logical from the
GPU search and a weight-76 Z logical from the CI gate, so the entry is filed at
