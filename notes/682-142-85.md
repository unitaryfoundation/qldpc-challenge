# [[682,142,85]] — sparse cyclic-ideal generalized bicycle

This submission reports a witness-backed upper bound `d <= 85`. Its score is
1504.325513, 3.269% above the posted unrestricted headline score 1456.703812
from `codes/682-172-76.json`. The maximum check weight is 28. No exact-distance
certificate or literature novelty is claimed.

## Direction & hypothesis

The search targeted the unrestricted headline near the challenge's standard
blocklength limit. It changed one irreducible factor of an existing cyclic ideal and mined
sparse generators from the result. This preserves a high number of encoded qubits while changing the structure
that determines short logical operators.

Start with the degree-70 common polynomial divisor from
`codes/682-140-82.json`. Replace the irreducible factor with binary coefficient
bitmask 1199 by factor 1727. The resulting generator has bitmask
`2206035533874970615479`. The ideal was checked against all invertible
multipliers modulo 341 and is outside the source ideal's multiplier orbit.
This is an algebraic construction check, not a claim of inequivalence under
all qubit permutations.

## What was searched

The ideal-mutation sweep visited 37 distinct degree-70/80 ideals and retained
91 sparse polynomial words. Words with extra factors beyond the chosen
generator and an optional `x+1` were rejected. Sparse words were obtained by
randomly permuting columns of a classical cyclic-ideal basis, computing its
GF(2) RREF, and retaining rows of weight at most 16. Cyclic shifts and
Frobenius-equivalent words were deduplicated.

The selected ideal was the first in the sweep. Seed 9245001 produced the two
weight-14 supports at iterations 4240 and 6895. Both have individual gcd
`g(x)*(x+1)` with `x^341-1`, so their common gcd has degree 71 and `k=142`.

The wider search also explored relative Frobenius changes, new sparse pairs
within an existing ideal, primitive-factor ideals at cyclic orders 331 and
333, and degree-65/66 ideals with lower component ranks. Every recovered
logical was retained; distance claims only decreased.

## Evidence trail

The submission contains X and Z weight-85 logical operators.
The native search found a weight-85 operator, and the generalized-bicycle
block-swap/reversal symmetry produced the opposite-side witness. Both were
checked on the CPU for commutation and non-membership in the corresponding stabilizer rowspace.

Python seed: 92521240. The Python search took 244.1 seconds; its API reports

seeds: 923642001 and 923642002. Both GPU searches completed all eight million
trials, with no early stop. Together they took 219.3 seconds on an A40.
The subsequent native pass used five 100,000-trial slices with seeds 925100001 through 925100005,
returning 89, 87, 87, 88, and 85. It took 395.5 seconds with four threads.
The final submitted bound is therefore 85, retaining every earlier downgrade.

Targeted searches additionally imposed each of the six nontrivial degree-5
character constraints for the order-31 shift subgroup. Each restricted
problem received 20,000 trials using the trusted native DEM search with the
full original logical basis. All six returned weight at least 96. The 15
pairs of character components were then searched at the same depth, again
without improving the bound. Every proposal was checked against the original
682-qubit matrices. These searches expose some structure missed by ordinary
RIS but do not certify a lower bound.

The different algorithms and budgets are reported separately. None makes the
distance exact; a later search may still find a lighter logical.

## Dead ends

An earlier `[[682,140,d<=88]]` candidate passed a million native trials per
side and the candidate validator, then fell to 82 in a ten-million-trial GPU
screen. Another relative-Frobenius `[[682,142,d<=86]]` candidate survived a
million native trials, ten million GPU sketch trials, and a million full-basis
GPU trials before a restricted character-component search found weight 80.
That explicit witness ruled it out and motivated the targeted checks here.

The order-333 primitive-factor sweep covered its three multiplier-equivalence
classes; pure-block witnesses of weights 72, 72, and 64 ruled them out. The
order-331 sweep found four candidate constructions with final bounds
60, 60, 16, and 16. None was presented as a record.

Construction and packaging used the challenge research kit. All distance

## Reproduction

Over `GF(2)[x]/(x^341-1)`, let the circulant `C(S)` have row `i` supported on
`{(s+i) mod 341 : s in S}`. Use

```
A = [9,26,40,71,95,101,172,197,249,260,274,287,290,326]
B = [46,74,82,96,107,173,184,201,208,239,270,295,326,330]
H_X = [C(A) | C(B)]
H_Z = [C(B)^T | C(A)^T]
```

Both matrices have rank 270, commute over GF(2), and have row weight 28.
The complete matrices and the two logical operators are included in
`codes/682-142-85.json`.
