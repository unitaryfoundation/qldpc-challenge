# [[430,58,48]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code at m = 215, row weight 20

At row weight 20 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 430` and `k >= 58` is 31, at `[[390,82,31]]` and row weight 16. This entry reaches 48. Allowing any row weight the best in the same range is 38, at `[[390,82,38]]` and row weight 32.

## Direction & hypothesis

`m = 215` had never been swept for this construction, and `n = 430` held a single board entry, `[[430,22,5]]` at row weight 4.

For a cyclic generalized bicycle on `x^m - 1` the dimension is `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so it can be set in advance: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree out of cyclotomic coset minimal polynomials and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Degree 29 at `m = 215` is `deg g / m` of 0.135, mid-band for this family, whose candidates all sat between 0.117 and 0.152; degrees above roughly 0.17 return distance 4 or 5 at any modulus.

## What was searched

`m = 215 = 5 x 43` with `ord_2(215) = 28`, so `x^215 - 1` factors over GF(2) into 11 cyclotomic cosets: one of size 1, one of size 4, three of size 14 and six of size 28. Reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of those sizes, which is a coarse set at this modulus. The divisor here has degree 29, fixing `k = 58`.

Degrees were filtered before cores were spent: the degree must be a sum of coset sizes or no divisor exists, the chosen cosets must include the size-1 coset at the origin or almost every draw is lost to a parity rejection, and the chosen exponents must not share a factor with `m` or the divisor sits inside `x^q - 1` for a proper divisor `q` and the codes fall below distance 4.

The sweep ran at 20,000 information-set trials per draw. This draw screened at 60 and a probe at 600,000 trials over 4 seeds read 52. Both polynomial weights are 10, so `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd; since `x + 1` is `x^1 - 1`, the only block logical that forces has weight 215, far above the claim.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, b, x^215 - 1) = 29`, and the same degree on each side alone, so the leak between the two single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 48, with per-seed readings from 48 to 54 and one of the 24 seeds reaching the minimum. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier's own refutation pass reaches, read 53, above the gate, so the gate is the binding instrument and the claim is 48. The thin minimum is the reason to read 48 as an upper bound that may fall further; the margin over the weight-20 bar is wide enough that a correction would adjust the claim rather than remove the entry.

The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 29 whose zeros are the 186 residues the divisor omits. The longest run of consecutive residues among them is 38, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 39 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 52. The quotient fold acts at `t = 5` and `t = 43`; a fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than 65, so neither fold binds.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. The Z witness of weight 48 is the operator the gate found; the X witness is that operator carried across by the construction's own symmetry, since `H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]` and `H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]` means swapping the blocks and negating the index inside each maps one side's logicals onto the other's. It was validated against the opposite check matrix and the logical basis before being written, and both witnesses commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space.

## Dead ends

A row weight 24 draw at this exact dimension screened 61, probed to 54 and then fell to 46 under its 8,000,000-trial run. That is dominated by this entry outright, on equal `n` and `k`, lighter weight and higher distance, so it is not submitted. The ordering of those two was not knowable from the probes alone: at probe depth the weight-24 draw read 54 against this one's 52, and only the deep runs reversed it.

A row weight 14 draw at the neighbouring `[[430,56]]` screened 54 and probed to 30. At row weight 14 the board's bar for that `(n, k)` falls to 26 from 31, but the distance fell much further than the bar did, so it prices at +4. Lighter draws in this family pay only where a strong entry already occupies the heavier weight and the bar collapses underneath it, which is not the case there.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings, witness validation and the single-block searches come from this repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension. Divisor assembly, coset selection, the information-set draws, the orbit fold and the witness mirror are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite. Roughly four core-hours for the gate, one for the 8,000,000-trial run, and a few minutes each for the screen and the fold.

## Reproduction

`m = 215`, `n = 2m = 430`. With `a = {31, 50, 73, 125, 136, 145, 162, 169, 176, 179}` and `b = {3, 11, 16, 18, 33, 41, 64, 124, 139, 160}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The divisor is recovered as `gcd(a, b, x^215 - 1)`, of degree 29. The submitted `codes/430-58-48.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
