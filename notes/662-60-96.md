# [[662,60,96]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 331

At row weight 22 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 662` and `k >= 60` is 64, at `[[514,64,64]]` and row weight 20. This entry reaches 96. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 84, at `[[662,122,84]]` and row weight 30.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 30 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 331` with `ord_2(331) = 30`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^331 - 1` over GF(2) number 12 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 1 coset of total degree 30, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

Draws with two even weights are rejected: `(x + 1)` divides a binary polynomial exactly when its weight is even, so two even weights would add the root-1 coset to the common gcd and land the draw one degree above the target. Here `a` has weight 11 and `b` has weight 11.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^331 - 1) = 30`, `deg gcd(b, x^331 - 1) = 30` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^331 - 1) = 30`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 96. The per-seed spread: 5 seeds found 96, 10 seeds found 98, 9 seeds found 100. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 30 whose zeros are the 301 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 66, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 67 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 106. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; each weighs 96, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends

- Nothing else from this front survived its gate.
- Degrees 60 and 61 at this modulus were swept first and are capped by the single block, not by the search: a circulant search at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials found a weight-94 logical in the best degree-60 candidate and a weight-91 one in the best degree-61 candidate. Their longest unpicked runs are 49 and 35, which permits exactly that. Degrees 30 and 31, used here, have runs of 67.
- The degree-61 point yielded more undominated rows at triage than any other point tried, and is the structurally weakest of the four. Row yield is the wrong way to choose a design point; the run of the run-maximising coset pick is computable before any search and orders them correctly.
- A candidate at [[662,120,92]] and row weight 28 was gated and withdrawn: it read 84 against the 81 it needed after four seeds of 2,000,000 trials, having probed at 92. Erosion from a 60,000-trial probe to a full gate runs near 15 percent here, so a margin thinner than that is not worth the hours.

The triage reading for this pair was 106, against a gated 96. Every member of this front moved the same way, which is why all of them were gated rather than filed off the sweep.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings and single-block searches come from the repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension; divisor assembly, coset selection and the information-set draws are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite.

## Reproduction

`m = 331`, `n = 2m = 662`. With `a = {124, 128, 132, 149, 178, 193, 236, 272, 281, 286, 288}` and `b = {40, 67, 73, 87, 103, 105, 132, 150, 185, 200, 269}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/662-60-96.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
