# [[674,44,91]] — weight-12 cyclic generalized bicycle at prime length 337, no imposed symmetry

## Direction & hypothesis

Target: the kd²/n headline at check weight ≤ 12. The leader is
`codes/674-44-89.json` ([[674,44,89]], w = 12, 517.1), the BCH-designed
cyclic generalized bicycle at L = 337 whose claim came down from 95 in the
October corrections. Its divisor has structural cap 129 (measured here with
`circulant_gb_witness`, two seeds), so its distance is set by two-block
logicals, not by the constituent code — which means other weight-6 pairs
from the same family can land higher.

The construction is the one behind `codes/674-86-93.json` and
`codes/662-122-84.json`, at the lightest possible weight: a **prime** block
length (no rotation subgroups) and a polynomial pair with **no multiplier
relation**, so the orbit-fold search of issue #2899 has nothing to act on
(the verifier's `orbit_fold_min_logical` reports 0 partitions).

## What was searched

Weight-6 multiples of a degree-22 divisor of x^337 − 1 ((x + 1) · one
degree-21 factor; 16 such ideals, k = 44) exist in quantity — a
meet-in-the-middle enumeration over the divisor behind `codes/674-44-89.json`
counts 19,404 of them. A separate exhaustive check showed that at L = 331
no degree-31 divisor has any weight-6 multiple at all, so L = 337 is the
only prime length under the cap with a w = 12 row in this family.

Campaign (seed 511): ideals in random order; per ideal, weight-6 words by
the kit's Prange search (20,000 trials, 120 kept), the structural cap once
(`circulant_gb_witness`, 60k then 300k trials), and per pair k = 44,
connectivity, the verifier's orbit-fold search, then an RIS ladder
2k → 50k → 500k → 2M, discarding below the target. Ideal 1: cap 129; its
second pair survived the whole ladder and is this entry.

## Reproduction

Let C(S) be the 337 × 337 binary circulant whose row i has ones at columns
(i + s) mod 337 for s ∈ S. H_X = [C(a) | C(b)], H_Z = [C(b)ᵀ | C(a)ᵀ] with a
and b above. The divisor is gcd(a, x^337 − 1) = gcd(b, x^337 − 1), degree 22.
