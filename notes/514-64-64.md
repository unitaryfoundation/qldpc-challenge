# [[514,64,64]] — cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal

No board entry at `n <= 514` reaches `k >= 64` and `d >= 64` together. The best
entry at this blocklength is `[[514,128,52]]` at `kd^2/n = 673.4`, against
510.0 here, so this adds a distinct corner rather than a better score: twice
the distance at half the dimension and two thirds the row weight.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`,
`k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd
and can be chosen. `m = 257` is prime, so there is no quotient fold at all: a
proper divisor `t` of `m` would give a ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, and a prime
modulus has none. That leaves the two-orbit block swap and the single-block
bound as the only structural ceilings, and 257 is the only prime in range with
`ord_2(m) <= 20`, the limit the primitive-polynomial table imposes.

Degree 32 was the target, giving `k = 64`, where the single-block BCH bound is
86 — the highest of any reachable degree at this modulus.

## What was searched

The cyclotomic cosets of `x^257 - 1` over GF(2) are one of size 1 and sixteen of
size 16, so reachable divisor degrees are multiples of 16 plus an optional 1.
The divisor is assembled from coset minimal polynomials over GF(2^16), and `a`
and `b` are drawn as low-weight codewords of its ideal by Prange
information-set decoding.

Draws where both polynomial weights are even are rejected. `(x+1)` divides a
polynomial exactly when its weight is even, so it divides `gcd(a, b)` exactly
when both are, which silently adds the root-1 coset to the gcd and lands the
sweep two degrees above its target. That is the worst place to land here: the
BCH bound reads 90, 86 and 74 at `k` = 32, 64 and 96, and 64, 52 and 44 at
`k` = 34, 66 and 98.

Triage read 76 at 20,000 trials; the confirmation settled it at 64.

## Evidence trail

Divisor degree 32, so `k = 64`. Both single-block gcds equal 32, matching
`deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so neither circulant block carries a kernel wider
than `k` requires.

- General RIS, 24 random information-set seeds at 2,000,000 trials each:
  lightest logical weight 64, per-seed spread 64 to 70.
- Orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the 514 qubits
  (`research/foldbound.py`): lightest validated lift weight 162. A prime
  modulus leaves only the two-orbit block swap, and it is not binding.
- Single-block circulant search, 12 seeds at 600,000 trials: nothing under 76.

The claim is `d = 64`, a witness-backed upper bound, with both witnesses in the
submission and re-checked against `H_X` and `H_Z` by plain integer arithmetic.

## Dead ends

A partial gate is not a result, and this family demonstrated it twice in one
session. At `k = 128` the gate read 54 across its first eighteen seeds, which
would have superseded the `[[514,128,52]]` on the board, and seeds nineteen
through twenty-four turned up a single 52 that made the candidate worthless.
The final distribution there was one seed at 52, five at 54, sixteen at 56 and
two at 58, so the minimum rode on one rare seed rather than on the mode. This
code's own gate was quoted as "stable at 66 after eighteen seeds" until all
twenty-four had run.

Degrees 80, 96 and 112 (`k` = 160, 192 and 224) produced nothing undominated
across 300 draws each on eight processes, despite domination thresholds of only
`d > 30` at `k = 160` and `d > 13` at `k = 192`. A low threshold is not on its
own a reason to search a cell; the general distance never reached even those.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 under Claude Code. `gf2_fast` from `verify/` for the accelerated
RIS and single-block searches, `verify/gf2.py` for GF(2) rank, nullspace and
logical bases, `research/foldbound.py` for the orbit fold, and
`verify/validate_candidate.py` for the verdict. Divisor assembly and the
information-set draw are described above rather than committed.

## Reproduction

`m = 257`, `n = 514`, `k = 64`.

```
a(x) = x^28 + x^70 + x^83 + x^127 + x^177 + x^185 + x^210 + x^211 + x^243
b(x) = x^63 + x^69 + x^71 + x^114 + x^133 + x^143 + x^185 + x^205 + x^232
     + x^236 + x^238
```

`H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]` and `H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]`, with
`circ(p)[i, j] = 1` exactly when `j - i mod 257` is an exponent of `p`. The
exact supports are in `codes/514-64-64.json`.
