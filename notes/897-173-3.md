# [[897,173,3]] contracted affine checkerboard core, exact d = 3

## Direction & hypothesis

Issue #2939 recorded a contraction sweep of the affine mod-5 checkerboard
cores that was killed at 11 of 36 seeds while still descending, and named
`[[897,173,3]]` from base 32x31, phases (1,4), order 3, seed 0 as the first
candidate: it beats `codes/899-173-3.json` (#2938) on n at equal k and d in
the `weight-8 x local-2d-bilayer` cell. This entry is that chain, reproduced,
and the completed sweep around it.

Construction and tooling are #2938's (`research/geometric_grafts/campaign.py`,
@MathysRennela): rectangle completion of the affine mod-5 checkerboard grammar
of `notes/961-169-3.md`, projection onto the encoding core, then pivot moves
of degree at most 3 (XOR an own-side row into every other own-side row
covering a qubit, delete the row, delete the qubit) accepted only when k is
unchanged, d >= 3 holds exactly, and the stabilizer group stays one connected
block. Our part is running the sweep to completion and filing what it found.

## What was searched

All 36 planned chains, one process per (base, seed), on a laptop and on a
Perlmutter CPU node (`genesis` campaign tooling; every chain also saves its
artifact, so a kill loses nothing). The sixth base of #2939's list, 34x29 at
order 0, is incomplete in the constructor and yields no chain, as campaign.py
itself reports; the other five bases ran six seeds each.

Four of six seeds on this base end at exactly 897, and none below, so the
base has converged rather than been sampled lightly. A chain costs 745 to
950 s on 8 threads (85 accepted moves here; 8 to 11 s per accepted move,
dominated by the two RREFs each candidate move needs).

The chain: base 32x31, phases (1,4), order 3 gives the completion; projected
onto its encoding core, n = 982, k = 173; 85 accepted moves, seed 0,
deterministic, n = 897. Pivot moves leave a few empty or repeated check rows
behind (four on the X side here); those are dropped when the matrices are
written out, which changes neither the stabilizer group nor the code.

## Evidence trail

- Lower bound, d >= 3: the campaign's exact test (no weight-1 or weight-2
  logical, decided by column enumeration against the opposite checks plus
  rowspace membership), re-run on the final matrices.
- Upper bound, d <= 3: weight-3 witnesses on both sides in the JSON, found
  by the submit pipeline's RIS and re-checked by the gate.
- Certificate: `certs/897-173-3.json`, level `proof_log`. `verify/sat_certify.py`
  encodes "a nontrivial logical of weight <= 2 exists" per side; kissat 4.0.4
  reports UNSAT on both and drat-trim verifies both refutations; the XOR path
  (pycryptosat) agrees. The entry claims `confidence: exact` on both sides,
  which the verifier accepts as an upper bound until the certificate is read.
- Gate (`verify/validate_candidate.py` against main at 9de3faad): passed, no
  exact or WL duplicate, refuter found nothing lighter, `board_advancing`
  true, `advances_by: ["n"]`, cell `weight-8 x local-2d-bilayer`.

## Where it sits

Max check weight 8 and max check diameter sqrt(20) = 4.47 after contraction,
so the cell is `weight-8 x local-2d-bilayer`, as for #2938. There, k = 173 at
d = 3 was held by `[[899,173,3]]`; this entry reaches it at n = 897 and
dominates that one on n alone. The weight-4 records `[[923,173,3]]` and
`[[925,173,3]]` are untouched: contraction cannot hold w <= 4 (#2939, "What is
dead").

## Dead ends

- The weight-4 x local-2d-single cell stays out of reach by this move set:
  the capped variant (`constrained.py`) stalls near n = 950 at k = 173 whether
  or not degree-2 and degree-3 moves are allowed, exactly as #2939 measured.
- More seeds on this base do not help: four of six already end at 897.
