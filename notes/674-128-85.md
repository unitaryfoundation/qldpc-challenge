# [[674,128,85]] — cyclic generalized bicycle at prime length 337, no imposed symmetry

## Direction & hypothesis

Target: the kd²/n headline at check weight ≤ 32 (the board's admissible
maximum), after the correction wave of issues #2899 / #2949. Every top entry
at n = 674–690 — this author's `codes/682-172-52-b.json`, `codes/682-130-56.json`
and `codes/682-82-35.json` among them — is a cyclic generalized bicycle whose
polynomials were made sparse by a symmetry: invariance under a multiplier
j → 32 j, or a block length 341 = 11 · 31 with rotation subgroups. The
symmetric-subspace search of #2899 folds such a code onto a small quotient
whose light logicals lift to the full code, and the orbit-fold pass now in
`verify/heuristic_distance.py` finds them in seconds.

This entry is the opposite construction: a **prime** block length, so Z_337
has no rotation subgroups, and a polynomial pair with **no multiplier
relation**, so the only automorphisms are the cyclic shifts and the fold
search has nothing to act on. The price is that sparse multiples of a
divisor are rarer without a symmetry; at degree 64 they exist at weights
12–16 only with a 40,000-trial Prange search, and the check weight lands
exactly on the cap, 16 + 16 = 32.

## What was searched

x^337 − 1 = (x + 1) · sixteen irreducible factors of degree 21. Divisors of
degree 64 = 1 + 3 · 21 (k = 128): 560 ideals, visited in random order (seed
322). Per ideal:

1. Sparse codewords of the ideal by the kit's Prange search
   (`research/cyclic_gb.py`, `prange_sparse_words`, 40,000 trials, weights
   10–16; 120 words kept).
2. The structural cap once per ideal: single-block logicals are words of the
   [337, 64] code generated by (x^337 − 1)/g and do not depend on the pair,
   so `gf2_fast.circulant_gb_witness` (60k, then 300k trials) decides whether
   any pair from the ideal can reach the target. Ideal 1: cap 85, above the
   82 needed for kd²/n > 1250.
3. Per pair (|a| + |b| ≤ 32): k = 2 deg g checked, Tanner-graph and
   stabilizer-group connectivity checked, then the verifier's own
   `orbit_fold_min_logical` (300 fold trials, 90 s cap) — **no symmetry to
   fold** (0 partitions) — then an RIS ladder of 2k → 50k → 500k → 2M trials
   per pair, discarding as soon as the bound fell below the target.

The first pair of ideal 1 survived the whole ladder at 89 — but the
structural search of step 2 had already exhibited a weight-85 single-block
logical, which is a logical of this code too (it depends only on the
divisor), and the 2M-trial ladder never reached it. The entry therefore
claims d ≤ 85. Random search did get there eventually: the 10M-trial
fresh-seed deep run found a weight-85 two-block logical on the Z side in
its ninth chunk, so the claim rests on two independent witnesses, one per
side. The ladder's miss is the same lesson as #2899 in miniature: a
structured family of logicals that shallow RIS rarely samples must be
searched by its structure, and the gate's `_structural_refute` would have
found it.

Score at the bound: 128 · 85² / 674 = 1372.1. The single-block family
bottoms out at 85 under the structural search (three seeds agree), and the
two-block logicals random search reached also bottom out at 85 (10M
trials, per-chunk minima 85–89); nothing lighter was found by either.

Sibling survivors of the same campaign family, all prime-length, all with

## Reproduction

Let C(S) be the 337 × 337 binary circulant whose row i has ones at columns
(i + s) mod 337 for s ∈ S. H_X = [C(a) | C(b)], H_Z = [C(b)ᵀ | C(a)ᵀ] with a
and b above. The divisor is gcd(a, x^337 − 1) = gcd(b, x^337 − 1), degree 64.
