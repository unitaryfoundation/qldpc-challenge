The distance is a witness-backed upper bound d <= 10, not an exact certificate.

## Direction & hypothesis

hackathon issue 1155. Against board commit 6995fc4f72a33fcbea27a244da230fcfc370e108,
the trusted candidate gate labels this candidate board-advancing. Eligibility
dimensions are n=84, w=8 and claimed d=10. No layout is supplied, so this targets
the weight <= 8 prize, not the weight <= 6 or locality prizes.

## Reproduction

Use G=SmallGroup(42,3) in GAP and e=Elements(G), with 1-based indexing. Set
a=1+e[8]+e[27] and b=1+e[3]+e[16]+e[39]+e[42]. Export the multiplication table
in that exact element order; convert indices to zero-based for the research kit.
Build H_X=[L(a)|R(b)] and H_Z=[R(b)^T|L(a)^T]. Package with confidence
upper_bound. The construction is completely specified by the pinned source and
these supports.

## Circuit tier (contributed 2026-10-22, @willzeng)

The entry now ships the generator's interleaved syndrome-extraction memory
circuits with d_circ = 9 in both bases (code distance 10), with witnesses
constructed by hand rather than found by search. The entry had no circuit tier
before.

The circuits. For this code the two-block decomposition of
`research/circuit_autogen.py` succeeds (3 + 5 commuting permutation terms), so
`research/circuit_backfill.py` builds the Bravyi et al. style interleaved
schedule: joint reset and measurement layers, 9 CX layers per round, 168
qubits, 47,754 (Z) and 47,796 (X) mechanisms per memory at rounds = 10. These
are the committed files, unchanged from the generator's first candidate
(seed 0).

Why the recorded value is 9 and not the 10 the generator prints. The
generator's deep search ran 12 RIS trials per basis at this mechanism count
and found nothing, so it recorded d_circ = 10/10 through the readout-flip
fallback along the code's own distance witness; a 10-minute RIS search per
basis with the C++ accelerator also finds nothing below weight 37, so the
search says nothing about this circuit. The interleaving does blunt hooks (a
hook's data error is partly seen by the Z checks coupled later in the same
round, so the sequential hook-cost picture of
`fieldnotes/2026-09-18-hook-cost-and-flag-schedules.md` does not transfer
directly), but a time-aware construction gives real witnesses: for every check
of the hook's type and every CNOT position, take the ancilla hook, the cheapest
completion of its remaining-support error to a logical of the opposite type
(exact meet-in-the-middle oracle up to weight 8), place the completion at the
start or the end of the last round, and add one measurement flip per
detecting check that still sees the partial error. The cheapest such set in
each memory has 9 faults: Z memory, X check 0, hook after the 5th CNOT, 7 data
errors at the end of the round, 1 measurement flip; X memory, Z check 0, hook
after the 2nd CNOT, 8 data errors at the start of the round, no measurement
flip. Both are checked in GF(2) against the committed detector error models
and are the committed `circuit.d_circ` witnesses.

What 9 means here. It is an upper bound from single-hook mechanisms only;
pairs of hooks on different ancillas of this interleaved circuit were not
analysed, so a stronger search may lower it. For comparison, a flag-protected
sequential schedule of this code (two flags per check, CNOT orders chosen by
SAT under an exhaustive hook-combination analysis, as in the circuit tier of
`notes/64-18-8.md`) also reaches exactly 9, with 10 statically infeasible,
at 336 qubits and 26 CX layers per round; the interleaved circuit was kept
because it reaches the same bound with half the qubits and a third of
the depth.

The analysis scripts (exact hook-cost oracle, time-aware witness
construction, the flagged alternative) are local staging output of this
contribution and are not committed here; the descriptions above are meant to
be enough to rewrite them.
