# [[514,98,57]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 257

At row weight 24 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 514` and `k >= 98` is 54, at `[[514,98,54]]` and row weight 20. This entry reaches 57. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 54, at `[[514,98,54]]` and row weight 20.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 49 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 257` with `ord_2(257) = 16`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^257 - 1` over GF(2) number 17 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 4 cosets of total degree 49, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

The divisor carries the root-1 coset, so both polynomial weights are even by construction and `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd by design rather than by accident. Here `a` has weight 12 and `b` has weight 12.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^257 - 1) = 49`, `deg gcd(b, x^257 - 1) = 49` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^257 - 1) = 49`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 57. The per-seed spread: 2 seeds found 57, 1 seed found 58, 2 seeds found 59, 2 seeds found 60, 7 seeds found 61, 9 seeds found 62, 1 seed found 63. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 49 whose zeros are the 208 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 25, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 26 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 57. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; each weighs 57, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends

- Nothing else from this front survived its gate.
- Another code at this exact design point, m = 257 degree 49 at row weight 20, was filed earlier at distance 58 and refuted by the verifier with a weight-54 logical inside one circulant block. The degree is structurally weak: its run of unpicked residues is 25, so the block admits logicals as light as 26, far below the 55 this cell needs. The present code was therefore screened harder than the rest, at 24 seeds of 1,000,000 trials confined to one block, and found nothing below 57.
- Three sibling candidates from the same older batch were built and refused before filing, all by the verifier own refutation search: [[560,32,78]] at m=280 fell to a weight-40 logical and two codes at m=210 fell to weight-42 logicals. Each refuting witness is an arithmetic progression, step 14 at m=280 and step 10 at m=210, identifying them as quotient-fold lifts; both moduli are composite and neither candidate had been screened against the fold. At a prime modulus such as 257 the quotient fold is vacuous.

The triage reading for this pair was 69, against a gated 57. Every member of this front moved the same way, which is why all of them were gated rather than filed off the sweep.

## Tools

## Reproduction

`m = 257`, `n = 2m = 514`. With `a = {16, 26, 62, 64, 79, 90, 105, 107, 143, 153, 195, 231}` and `b = {42, 53, 63, 82, 93, 141, 151, 156, 183, 186, 227, 240}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/514-98-57.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
