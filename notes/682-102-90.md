# [[682,102,90]] — cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal

No board entry at any blocklength reaches `k >= 102` and `d >= 90` together. At
`n <= 682` with `d >= 90` and row weight at most 22 there are six entries, and
the largest dimension among them is 42. The nearest high-dimension neighbours
sit at `[[682,142,82]]` with weight 32 and `[[662,122,84]]` with weight 30, so
this code trades dimension for 8 more distance at two thirds the row weight.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`,
so the dimension is a property of a polynomial gcd and can be chosen instead of
waited for. Drawing `a` and `b` at random and keeping whatever `k` appears
reaches `k >= 4` only by luck; building a divisor `g` of known degree and
drawing `a, b` from its ideal fixes `k = 2 deg g` on demand. The opening was the
high-`k`, high-`d` corner at `n` near 700, where the board's entries all carry
row weight 30 or more.

## What was searched

`m = 341 = 11 * 31`, with `ord_2(341) = 10`. The cyclotomic cosets of
`x^341 - 1` over GF(2) number 38: one of size 1, thirty-one of size 10 and six
of size 5, so the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of that
multiset. The divisor is assembled from coset minimal polynomials
over GF(2^10) to hit a target degree, and `a` and `b` are drawn as low-weight
codewords of its ideal by Prange information-set decoding. A sweep over target
degrees from `0.04m` to `0.30m` produced 29 undominated triage rows at 20,000
RIS trials each; the six best were taken to a 24-seed confirmation at 2,000,000
trials per seed.

Four of those six are unsubmittable on row weight alone: `schema/code.schema.json`
caps a check's support list at 32 entries, and they carry 44 to 58. Screening on
that cap before spending confirmation time is the cheaper order.

## Evidence trail

Divisor degree 51, so `k = 102`. Both single-block gcds, `deg gcd(a, x^m - 1)`
and `deg gcd(b, x^m - 1)`, equal 51 as well, so neither circulant block carries
a kernel wider than `k` requires.

Confirmation, three independent mechanisms:

- General RIS, 24 random information-set seeds at 2,000,000 trials each
  (8,451 s): lightest logical weight 90 on X and 92 on Z, spread 90 to 96 across
  seeds, median 94.
- Single-block circulant search, 8 seeds at 600,000 trials: nothing under 101.
  This matters because a logical supported on one block of
  `H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]` is constrained only by that block, so it lives in
  one 341-column kernel and the general search essentially never samples it.
- Orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the 682 qubits, with the folded
  code handed to a distance search (`research/foldbound.py`): lightest validated
  lift weight 110, at 31 orbits of size 11.

The claim is `d = 90`, a witness-backed upper bound. Both witnesses are in the
submission and were re-checked against `H_X` and `H_Z` with plain integer
arithmetic: each has the stated weight, commutes with the opposite checks, and
lies outside the rowspace of its own.

## Dead ends

A sparse multiplier is the wrong way to use the divisor. Setting `a = g u` with
`u` of two to four terms gives the target `k` immediately, but `a` is then a few
shifts of `g` and inherits its degeneracy: across 32,000 such draws every code
came out at distance 4 to 7. Searching for a sparse `a` inside the ideal of `g`
instead, with the multiplier left dense, is what produces distance.

At `m = 257` a draw can also pick up cyclotomic cosets beyond `g` without
changing `k`, since only the common gcd sets the dimension. Each extra coset
widens a single-block kernel, and the resulting codes are the ones a structural
search refutes; rejecting them costs two polynomial gcds and 17% of draws. A
zero leak is not a guarantee, though. `[[682,132,88]]` from the same sweep has
all three gcds at degree 66 and still carries a validated weight-80 single-block
logical, which is why the search runs on every candidate rather than only the
leaky ones.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 under Claude Code. `gf2_fast` from `verify/` for the accelerated
RIS and single-block searches, `verify/gf2.py` for GF(2) rank, nullspace and
logical bases, `research/foldbound.py` for the orbit fold, and
`verify/validate_candidate.py` for the verdict. Divisor assembly and the
information-set draw are described above rather than committed. The 24-seed
confirmation for this code ran 8,451 s on 12 cores, so about 28 core-hours; the
single-block and fold searches are minutes.

## Reproduction

`m = 341`, `n = 682`, `k = 102`.

```
a(x) = x^46 + x^55 + x^78 + x^83 + x^108 + x^109 + x^213 + x^269 + x^308
     + x^337
b(x) = x^9 + x^21 + x^56 + x^89 + x^190 + x^192 + x^196 + x^261 + x^270
     + x^289 + x^295 + x^310
```

`H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]` and `H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]`, with
`circ(p)[i, j] = 1` exactly when `j - i mod 341` is an exponent of `p`. The
exact supports are in `codes/682-102-90.json`.
