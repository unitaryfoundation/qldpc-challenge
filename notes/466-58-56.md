# [[466,58,56]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 233

At row weight 18 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 466` and `k >= 58` is 31, at `[[390,82,31]]` and row weight 16. This entry reaches 56. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 46, at `[[466,118,46]]` and row weight 30.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 29 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 233` with `ord_2(233) = 29`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^233 - 1` over GF(2) number 9 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 1 coset of total degree 29, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

Draws with two even weights are rejected: `(x + 1)` divides a binary polynomial exactly when its weight is even, so two even weights would add the root-1 coset to the common gcd and land the draw one degree above the target. Here `a` has weight 11 and `b` has weight 7.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^233 - 1) = 29`, `deg gcd(b, x^233 - 1) = 29` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^233 - 1) = 29`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 56. The per-seed spread: 3 seeds found 56, 9 seeds found 58, 11 seeds found 60, 1 seed found 62. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 29 whose zeros are the 204 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 43, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 44 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 66. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier itself reaches, reads 58.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; each weighs 56, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends

- [[466,58,54]] from the same front stops at 54 at the heavier row weight 20, so this entry beats it on both distance and weight.
- [[466,58,52]] from the same front is lighter at row weight 16 and stops at 52, so neither settles the other.
- The whole weight ladder at this design point was gated rather than just the draw with the highest triage: row weight 16 settled at 52, row weight 18 at 56, and two row-weight-20 draws at 54 and 58. The distance is set by the modulus and the divisor degree, not by the weight, so a point like this yields one distance across a range of weights and the comparison between them is what decides which are worth entries.

The reading before this gate was 66, against a gated 56. Every member of this front moved the same way, which is why all of them were gated rather than filed off the sweep.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings and single-block searches come from the repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension; divisor assembly, coset selection and the information-set draws are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite.

## Reproduction

`m = 233`, `n = 2m = 466`. With `a = {9, 11, 23, 35, 53, 69, 94, 160, 200, 208, 215}` and `b = {29, 41, 55, 72, 97, 133, 150}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/466-58-56.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
