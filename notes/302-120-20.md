# [[302,120,20]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 151

At row weight 30 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 302` and `k >= 120` is 10, at `[[256,120,10]]` and row weight 16. This entry reaches 20.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Here m is prime, so the quotient fold has no proper divisor to act on and is vacuous; only the single-block ceiling binds, and degree 60 sits below where it bites.

## What was searched

`m = 151` with `ord_2(151) = 15`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^151 - 1` over GF(2) number 11 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 4 cosets of total degree 60, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

Draws with two even weights are rejected: `(x + 1)` divides a binary polynomial exactly when its weight is even, so two even weights would add the root-1 coset to the common gcd and land the draw one degree above the target. Here `a` has weight 15 and `b` has weight 15.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^151 - 1) = 60`, `deg gcd(b, x^151 - 1) = 60` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^151 - 1) = 60`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 20. The per-seed spread: 24 seeds found 20. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 60 whose zeros are the 91 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 21, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 22 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 20. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier itself reaches, reads 20.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; each weighs 20, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings and single-block searches come from the repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension; divisor assembly, coset selection and the information-set draws are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite.

## Reproduction

`m = 151`, `n = 2m = 302`. With `a = {1, 14, 19, 21, 38, 42, 48, 55, 57, 61, 65, 79, 85, 121, 150}` and `b = {4, 7, 12, 50, 66, 75, 82, 83, 90, 103, 118, 121, 128, 142, 145}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/302-120-20.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
