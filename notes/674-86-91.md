# [[674,86,91]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 337

At row weight 22 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 674` and `k >= 86` is 58, at `[[514,96,58]]` and row weight 22. This entry reaches 91. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 93, at `[[674,86,93]]` and row weight 26.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 43 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 337` with `ord_2(337) = 21`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^337 - 1` over GF(2) number 17 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 3 cosets of total degree 43, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

The divisor carries the root-1 coset, so both polynomial weights are even by construction and `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd by design rather than by accident. Here `a` has weight 12 and `b` has weight 10.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^337 - 1) = 43`, `deg gcd(b, x^337 - 1) = 43` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^337 - 1) = 43`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 91. The per-seed spread: 1 seed found 91, 3 seeds found 92, 2 seeds found 93, 4 seeds found 94, 6 seeds found 95, 4 seeds found 96, 4 seeds found 97, 1 seed found 98. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 43 whose zeros are the 294 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 56, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 57 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 105. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; the X witness weighs 91 and the Z witness weighs 92, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends

- Nothing else from this front survived its gate.
- Degree 43 at this modulus has a longest unpicked run of 57 against a cell threshold of 59, so the single-block mechanism alone could put a candidate here under its own domination threshold. A circulant search at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials found nothing below 105 in the best degree-43 draw: the run bounds what the block can take away, not what it does.
- The claim started at 92, the minimum of a 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 trials per seed that three seeds reached. A single run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the refutation gate depth, read 95 and agreed. A single run of 32,000,000 found a weight-91 logical, and that witness is the one carried here. A sibling at degree 42 and the same row weight was filed at 94 and refuted by the gate at 92 after an identical 24-seed spread, which is why the deeper runs are made before filing rather than after.
- Design points at this modulus were ranked by the run of the run-maximising coset pick rather than by row yield: 120 at degree 21, 113 at 22, 70 at 42, 58 at 63, 51 at 64 and 41 at 85.

The triage reading for this pair was 101, against a gated 91. Every member of this front moved the same way, which is why all of them were gated rather than filed off the sweep.

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/674-86-91.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
