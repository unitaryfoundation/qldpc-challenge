# [[662,62,89]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 331

At row weight 18 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 662` and `k >= 62` is 31, at `[[390,82,31]]` and row weight 16. This entry reaches 89. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 84, at `[[662,122,84]]` and row weight 30.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 31 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 331` with `ord_2(331) = 30`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^331 - 1` over GF(2) number 12 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 2 cosets of total degree 31, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

The divisor carries the root-1 coset, so both polynomial weights are even by construction and `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd by design rather than by accident. Here `a` has weight 10 and `b` has weight 8.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^331 - 1) = 31`, `deg gcd(b, x^331 - 1) = 31` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^331 - 1) = 31`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 89. The per-seed spread: 1 seed found 89, 1 seed found 94, 1 seed found 95, 6 seeds found 96, 2 seeds found 97, 6 seeds found 98, 6 seeds found 99, 1 seed found 100. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 31 whose zeros are the 300 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 66, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 67 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 104. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; the X witness weighs 89 and the Z witness weighs 94, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends

- Nothing else from this front survived its gate.
- Degrees 60 and 61 at this modulus were swept first and are capped by the single block, not by the search: a circulant search at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials found a weight-94 logical in the best degree-60 candidate and a weight-91 one in the best degree-61 candidate. Their longest unpicked runs are 49 and 35, which permits exactly that. Degrees 30 and 31, used here, have runs of 67.
- The degree-61 point yielded more undominated rows at triage than any other point tried, and is the structurally weakest of the four. Row yield is the wrong way to choose a design point; the run of the run-maximising coset pick is computable before any search and orders them correctly.
- A candidate at [[662,120,92]] and row weight 28 was gated and withdrawn: it read 84 against the 81 it needed after four seeds of 2,000,000 trials, having probed at 92. Erosion from a 60,000-trial probe to a full gate runs near 15 percent here, so a margin thinner than that is not worth the hours.

The triage reading for this pair was 104, against a gated 89. Every member of this front moved the same way, which is why all of them were gated rather than filed off the sweep.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings and single-block searches come from the repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension; divisor assembly, coset selection and the information-set draws are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite.

## Reproduction

`m = 331`, `n = 2m = 662`. With `a = {57, 70, 148, 153, 162, 165, 225, 285, 295, 300}` and `b = {8, 124, 141, 195, 196, 267, 268, 322}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/662-62-89.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
