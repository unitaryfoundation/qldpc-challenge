# [[898,18,31]] — code-calculus peel of `899-18-31`

## Direction & hypothesis

Target cell: `local-2d-bilayer` / weight ≤ 8, at k ≈ 18, d ≈ 31. The board entry
`899-18-31` (`codes/899-18-31.json`) is the frontier point there; the hypothesis
was that peeling one qubit whose interaction radius and check support allow the
calculus's E-PEEL move would drop `n` by one while preserving `k` and `d`,
landing a new point (`n = 898`) just inside the frontier.

## What was searched

A single E-PEEL move was applied to every qubit of `899-18-31` that is touched
by exactly one displayed check in a sector (the move's precondition), for both
the X and Z sectors: 898 qubits × 2 sectors. Each candidate was exported by
`unitaryfoundation/code-calculus` @ b8a7465, `machine/priced_calculus.py`
(`export_challenge_entry`) and pre-screened with `research/kit/prescreen.py`.
Survivors were gated with `research/kit/coordination.py gate`. The winning
qubit was q = 123 in the X sector.

## Evidence trail

The candidate `[[898,18,31]]` passes `verify/validate_candidate.py` with
`passed: true` (gate run recorded beside the staged file). Its distance is a
witness-backed upper bound: `d_X ≤ 31`, `d_Z ≤ 31`, both reusing the parent's
validated logical witnesses, which the verifier re-checked against the child's
matrices. The accelerator pass (100,000 trials/side) did not find a lighter
logical, so the claim stands at `d ≤ 31`.

## Dead ends

Peeling other qubits of `899-18-31` either failed the calculus's
`NoDerivation` preconditions (non-unique touch, zero-check guard) or, among
the 50+ survivors, collapsed to existing frontier entries on refutation (the
gate found a lighter logical). Puncture of the same parent raised `k` but
dropped `d` to 2 and was dominated. Deletion on smaller parents (`24-4-5`,
`30-10-5`, `36-2-8`) likewise collapsed to d ≈ 1–2.
