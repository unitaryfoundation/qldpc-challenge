# [[514,130,51]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 257

At row weight 24 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 514` and `k >= 130` is 38, at `[[514,130,38]]` and row weight 24. This entry reaches 51. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 38, at `[[514,130,38]]` and row weight 24.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 65 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 257` with `ord_2(257) = 16`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^257 - 1` over GF(2) number 17 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 5 cosets of total degree 65, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

The divisor carries the root-1 coset, so both polynomial weights are even by construction and `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd by design rather than by accident. Here `a` has weight 12 and `b` has weight 12.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^257 - 1) = 65`, `deg gcd(b, x^257 - 1) = 65` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^257 - 1) = 65`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 51. The per-seed spread: 1 seed found 51, 3 seeds found 52, 1 seed found 53, 7 seeds found 54, 7 seeds found 55, 4 seeds found 56, 1 seed found 57. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 65 whose zeros are the 192 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 12, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 13 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 51. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; the X witness weighs 52 and the Z witness weighs 51, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends

- Nothing else from this front survived its gate.
- Three sibling candidates from the same older batch were built and refused before filing, all three by the verifier own refutation search: [[560,32,78]] at m=280 fell to a weight-40 logical, and two codes at m=210 fell to weight-42 logicals. Each refuting witness is an arithmetic progression, step 14 at m=280 and step 10 at m=210, which identifies them as quotient-fold lifts; both moduli are composite with many proper divisors and neither candidate had been screened against the fold.
- At a prime modulus the quotient fold is vacuous, which is why the later work in this family moved to prime m. The present code sits at m=257, prime.

The triage reading for this pair was 60, against a gated 51. Every member of this front moved the same way, which is why all of them were gated rather than filed off the sweep.

## Reproduction

`m = 257`, `n = 2m = 514`. With `a = {8, 43, 82, 89, 95, 100, 143, 165, 208, 213, 219, 226}` and `b = {9, 21, 57, 77, 97, 103, 132, 155, 184, 190, 210, 230}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/514-130-51.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
