# [[390,50,42]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code at m = 195, row weight 24

At row weight 24 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 390` and `k >= 50` is 31, at `[[390,82,31]]` and row weight 16. This entry reaches 42. Allowing any row weight the best in the same range is 38, at `[[390,82,38]]` and row weight 32.

## Direction & hypothesis

`m = 195` had never been swept for this construction, and `n = 390` holds 19 board entries, none at this dimension and row weight.

For a cyclic generalized bicycle on `x^m - 1` the dimension is `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so it can be set in advance: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree out of cyclotomic coset minimal polynomials and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Degree 25 at `m = 195` is `deg g / m` of 0.128, mid-band for this family, whose candidates all sat between 0.117 and 0.152.

## What was searched

`m = 195 = 3 x 5 x 13` with `ord_2(195) = 12`, so `x^195 - 1` factors over GF(2) into 20 cyclotomic cosets: one of size 1, one of size 2, three of size 4 and fifteen of size 12. Reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of those sizes, and the divisor here has degree 25, fixing `k = 50`.

Degrees were filtered before cores were spent: the degree must be a sum of coset sizes or the divisor does not exist, the chosen cosets must include the size-1 coset at the origin or almost every draw is lost to a parity rejection, and the chosen exponents must not share a factor with `m` or the divisor sits inside `x^q - 1` for a proper divisor `q` and the codes fall below distance 4.

The sweep ran at 20,000 information-set trials per draw. This draw screened at 53. Both polynomial weights are 12, so `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd; since `x + 1` is `x^1 - 1`, the only block logical that forces has weight 195, far above the claim.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, b, x^195 - 1) = 25`, and the same degree on each side alone, so the leak between the two single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

The instruments disagreed here and the claim is the minimum of them. A probe at 600,000 trials over 4 seeds read 47. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier's own refutation pass reaches, fell to 46. A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 trials per seed then settled at 42, four below the deep run, so the gate is the binding instrument. That ordering is worth recording: on other candidates from this same construction the deep run was the binding one and the gate read higher, so neither instrument dominates and only the minimum over both is defensible.

The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 25 whose zeros are the 170 residues the divisor omits. The longest run of consecutive residues among them is 46, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 47 or above, which is above the claim, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 47. The claim is therefore a general logical rather than a block one. The quotient fold acts at `t = 3`, `5`, `13`, `15`, `39` and `65`; a fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than 54, so none of the six folds binds.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. The Z witness of weight 42 is the operator the gate found; the X witness is that operator carried across by the construction's own symmetry, since `H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]` and `H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]` means swapping the blocks and negating the index inside each maps one side's logicals onto the other's. It was validated against the opposite check matrix and the logical basis before being written, and both witnesses commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space.

## Dead ends

A row weight 18 draw at the neighbouring `[[390,54]]` reached 41 under both its gate and its deep run. Neither entry dominates the other: that one is lighter but no further, and this one is further but heavier.

A lighter draw at `[[390,54]]`, row weight 12, screened 40 and probed to 24. At row weight 12 the board's bar for that `(n, k)` falls to 16 from 31, but the distance fell further than the bar did, so it prices at +8. The threshold gap between row weight 18 and row weight 14 at these `m = 195` cells is only 5, which is why lighter draws have no room here; they pay only where a strong entry already occupies the heavier weight and the bar collapses underneath it.

At `[[390,58]]` row weight 22 the recorded distance of 39 came from a structural bound rather than a search, and re-running the screen's own search returns 52, so no witness that light exists and the cell is finished.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings, witness validation and the single-block searches come from this repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension. Divisor assembly, coset selection, the information-set draws, the orbit fold and the witness mirror are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite. Roughly three core-hours for the gate, one for the 8,000,000-trial run, and a few minutes each for the screen and the fold.

## Reproduction

`m = 195`, `n = 2m = 390`. With `a = {10, 29, 45, 48, 86, 138, 141, 144, 161, 179, 183, 192}` and `b = {3, 19, 32, 33, 40, 87, 103, 104, 119, 133, 162, 190}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The divisor is recovered as `gcd(a, b, x^195 - 1)`, of degree 25. The submitted `codes/390-50-42.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
