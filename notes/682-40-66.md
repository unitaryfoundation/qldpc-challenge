# [[682,40,66]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 341

At row weight 10 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 682` and `k >= 40` is 20, at `[[630,126,20]]` and row weight 9. This entry reaches 66. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 100, at `[[682,62,100]]` and row weight 20.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 20 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 341` with `ord_2(341) = 10`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^341 - 1` over GF(2) number 38 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 3 cosets of total degree 20, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

Draws with two even weights are rejected: `(x + 1)` divides a binary polynomial exactly when its weight is even, so two even weights would add the root-1 coset to the common gcd and land the draw one degree above the target. Here `a` has weight 5 and `b` has weight 5.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^341 - 1) = 20`, `deg gcd(b, x^341 - 1) = 20` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^341 - 1) = 20`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 80, above the claim: see below. The per-seed spread: 1 seed found 80, 2 seeds found 84, 7 seeds found 86, 4 seeds found 88, 6 seeds found 90, 4 seeds found 92. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 20 whose zeros are the 321 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 131, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 132 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 100. The claim is the orbit folds own witness: folding onto the x^t-invariant subspaces produced a weight-{fold_w} logical, which is what caps this code and why the searches above read higher. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier itself reaches, reads 88.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; each weighs 66, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends


- The sweep row for this pair printed 100 and the orbit fold cut it to 66, so the row was wrong by 34. Two neighbouring rows from the same generator at m=315 were cut much further, [[630,44,90]] to 30 and [[630,48,90]] to 54, both below their cell thresholds and dropped. A composite modulus at low dimension is where the quotient fold bites, and m=341 = 11 x 31 with k=40 is squarely in that regime.
- The randomised searches here do not confirm the claim and are not expected to: the 24-seed gate bottomed at 80 and a single 8,000,000-trial run read 88, both well above 66, because a logical living in an x^t-invariant subspace is not something a general information-set search samples. The claim rests on the fold witness, which is in the submitted file and validates against both check matrices.

The reading before this gate was 66, against a gated 66.

## Reproduction

`m = 341`, `n = 2m = 682`. With `a = {2, 197, 231, 232, 271}` and `b = {49, 215, 253, 290, 292}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/682-40-66.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
