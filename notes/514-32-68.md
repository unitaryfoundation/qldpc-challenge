# [[514,32,68]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 257

At row weight 14 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 514` and `k >= 32` is 30, at `[[390,32,30]]` and row weight 10. This entry reaches 68. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 64, at `[[514,64,64]]` and row weight 20.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 16 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 257` with `ord_2(257) = 16`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^257 - 1` over GF(2) number 17 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 1 coset of total degree 16, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

Draws with two even weights are rejected: `(x + 1)` divides a binary polynomial exactly when its weight is even, so two even weights would add the root-1 coset to the common gcd and land the draw one degree above the target. Here `a` has weight 7 and `b` has weight 7.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^257 - 1) = 16`, `deg gcd(b, x^257 - 1) = 16` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^257 - 1) = 16`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 68. The per-seed spread: 2 seeds found 68, 4 seeds found 70, 9 seeds found 72, 9 seeds found 74. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 16 whose zeros are the 241 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 89, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 90 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 82. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, weigh 68, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space.

## Dead ends

- [[514,32,70]] from the same front reaches 70, 2 more, but at row weight 16 against 14 here, so neither settles the other.
- [[514,32,62]] from the same front is lighter at row weight 12 and stops at 62, so neither settles the other.
- [[514,32,46]] from the same front is lighter at row weight 10 and stops at 46, so neither settles the other.
- Target degree 17, the 16j+1 point at this modulus, has its longest unpicked run fall from 89 to 64 because the root-1 coset splits the long run. That is the degree an even-weight draw lands on by accident, which is why such draws are rejected at degrees that do not carry the coset by design.
- The front was ranked by a 20,000-trial screen, which ordered it backwards: the weight-16 member read 84 at triage, the highest of the four, and gated to 70 while this one read 82 and gated to 68.
- A second run of the gate on another machine returned a byte-identical spread. The seed sequence is derived from the candidate, so it was a duplicate rather than an independent confirmation, and the single gate below is the whole of the evidence.

The triage reading for this pair was 82, against a gated 68. Every member of this front moved the same way, which is why all of them were gated rather than filed off the sweep.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings and single-block searches come from the repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension; divisor assembly, coset selection and the information-set draws are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite.

## Reproduction

`m = 257`, `n = 2m = 514`. With `a = {1, 21, 70, 88, 137, 224, 230}` and `b = {4, 14, 23, 26, 30, 124, 241}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/514-32-68.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
