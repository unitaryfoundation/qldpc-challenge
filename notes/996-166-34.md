# [[996,166,34]] — irregular pair-partition CPM code with weight-8 checks, rate 1/6, lift P = 83

## Direction & hypothesis

Cell: CSS, unrestricted, weight 8, extended tier (n <= 1000 with w <= 8 and
d <= 40). The pair-partition (PP) construction of Okada and Kasai
(arXiv:2607.14091) gives CSS orthogonality from paired-difference equations
on CPM exponents; they apply it to complete J x L templates. Lee, Okada,
Maskara, Kasai and Zhou (arXiv:2609.30069) count the design rate as
R = 1 - J_tot/L_stab and name quantum-compatible irregular templates as a
natural direction. This entry uses the same irregular 5 x 12 template as our
codes/996-166-32.json. Hypothesis: that template's lifts at P = 83 vary in
distance from draw to draw, so a wider overnight sweep of its couple
solutions and exponent draws can find lifts whose lightest logical is above
32. This code:
166 * 34^2 / 996 = 192.7 (upper bound, from d <= 34); no lower bound is
claimed.

**Frontier:** Among entries with max check weight <= 8, the highest kd^2/n on
the board today is 170.7 (our codes/996-166-32.json, the same template).
This entry's is 192.7 as an upper bound (d <= 34). Distance: d <= 34 from
Stern witnesses (8 runs per side, 300 s to 1 h, nothing lighter found; X
34, and Z 34 as the image of the X witness under the code's X<->Z symmetry); the board heuristic returned 'inconclusive' (lightest 80 (X) / 102 (Z)). No lower bound is claimed, so the true score may be
lower.

**Related:** a different exponent draw of the same template as our
codes/996-166-32.json (PR #4045): the pairing differs in 3 of the 25
X-row / Z-row cells and all 80 exponents are a new draw, so the check
matrices differ; its lightest logical found is 34 instead of 32.

**Technique (not ours):** the pair-partition rule, its pairing arrays and the
CPM lift are Okada and Kasai's (arXiv:2607.14091); the design-rate count and
the permanent-vector upper bound (App. A of arXiv:2609.30069) are from Lee,
Okada, Maskara, Kasai and Zhou (arXiv:2609.30069); quasi-cyclic CSS LDPC
codes from circulant permutation matrices go back to Hagiwara and Imai
(arXiv:quant-ph/0701020), and CSS pairs of two irregular LDPC codes were
analysed by Hagiwara and Imai (arXiv:0801.1361).

**Board precedents:** the same commutation rule at weight 8 on the complete
(3,8) template, e.g. codes/912-234-22.json by @jacksongquera,
codes/904-230-22.json by @vprusso (which also cites the lifted-product
construction of Panteleev and Kalachev, arXiv:2111.03654) and the published
codes/584-150-18.json (Okada and Kasai). Our codes/990-198-17.json uses the
rule on a different irregular template with weight-7 checks, our
codes/1000-200-29.json on the 4 x 10 "pair-complement" template (all pairs
but one) at rate 1/5, and our codes/996-166-32.json on this template.

**Prior-art check:** the one made for codes/990-198-17.json (2026-10-22:
arXiv:2607.14091, arXiv:2609.30069, the Okada-Kasai project page, web
searches) found no pair-partition code on an irregular template. Literature
novelty is not claimed (`novelty: unknown`).

Our contribution: the irregular "pair-complement-2" template below, the
census of that family (ranked by our extension of the permanent-vector
bound to sparse templates), the symmetric couple solver and its overnight
extension that found this pairing, the lift P = 83 and this exponent draw,
and the distance screening below.

## What was searched

Template (as in codes/996-166-32.json). The 12 block columns form 6 column
pairs {0,1}, ..., {10,11}; each of the 5 block rows contains all pairs but
two, so every check has weight 8. The rows leave out {0,1}+{2,3},
{0,1}+{4,5}, {2,3}+{4,5}, {6,7}+{8,9}, {6,7}+{10,11}: a triangle and a path
on the 6 pairs, so {8,9} and {10,11} have qubit degree 4, the rest degree 3.
The X and Z sides use the same template; every X-row / Z-row overlap has 4,
6 or 8 columns. This is class 0 of our census (259 X/Z classes).

Couple solutions. Overnight, 33 class-0 jobs restarted our symmetric
depth-first solver (pairings invariant under one template automorphism) and
grew the pool with an exact large-neighbourhood search (free 6 cells of a
known solution, complete them with the same depth-first solver); the pool
reached 13665 4-cycle-free pairings. This pairing is pool solution 115.

Lifts and triage. 132 solutions, 2 random 4-cycle-free exponent draws each
at P = 83 (some drawn twice by two workers: 328 lifts), every one with
k = 166. Stern 3 s per side, then 60 s for the 69 lifts at 25 or more; 40
had a 60 s upper bound above 124.2 and were queued for 3 x 300 s per side;
five went on to 1200 s runs and three to 1 h runs. This lift (solution 115,
draw 1) is the one submitted.

The board heuristic's verdict is 'inconclusive': at this size its search
did not get down to the claimed 34 (lightest 80 and 102), and it found
nothing lighter, so it gives no evidence either way at weight 34.
X<->Z symmetry. The code has a qubit permutation (found with nauty: an
automorphism of the Tanner graph with one hub joined to all X checks and one
to all Z checks that swaps the hubs) carrying the X checks onto the Z checks
and back, so d_X = d_Z. The filed Z witness is the image of the weight-34 X
witness; Stern on the Z side alone had found 36 at lightest.

Every witness of the 16 recorded Stern runs, the mapped Z witness and the
heuristic's were re-checked in our build script (numpy and the board's verify/gf2.py):
weight as reported, zero syndrome against the opposite checks, outside the
row space of its own checks.

Matched depth. codes/996-166-32.json had the same Stern ladder (3 x 1200 s
and 2 x 1 h per side): 8 of its 10 deep runs reached 32. This code's 10 deep
runs stayed at 34 or above (X) and 36 or above (Z). This is a comparison of
two upper bounds at one search depth, not a proof that the codes differ in
distance.

Dedup. The board's trusted gate flags a shared WL signature with
codes/996-166-32.json (same template and lift; colour refinement does not
separate them). The board's research/audits/permutation_audit.py decider
returns 'undecided' for the pair: equal ranks (415, 415) and X/Z row-space
intersection dimension (5), and the row spaces are too large to enumerate.
The cheaper row-space invariants also agree (hull dimension 5 on each side,
hull weights {0:1, 332:15, 664:15, 996:1}). The two codes are nonetheless not
equivalent under any qubit permutation, with or without an X/Z swap:

1. All low-weight stabilizers. For each of the four row spaces (X and Z of
   both codes, dimension 415 each) we listed every vector of weight <= 9 by
   Brouwer-Zimmermann enumeration (K.-H. Zimmermann, Tech. Rep. 3-96, TU
   Hamburg-Harburg, 1996, after an idea of A. E. Brouwer) over two disjoint
   information sets: all sums of <= 4 rows of the systematic generator matrix
   for each set (about 1.23e9 sums per set, 2.46e9 per row space). The list
   is complete, because a vector of weight <= 9 has at most 4 ones on one of
   the two disjoint sets. In each row space
   the vectors of weight <= 9 are exactly the 415 given weight-8 checks
   (pairwise distinct).
   A second, separately written enumerator (its own elimination on a random
   column order) and a rerun from a randomly scrambled basis give the same
   lists.
2. Leon's argument (J. S. Leon, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 28 (1982) 496-511,
   doi:10.1109/TIT.1982.1056498). A permutation carrying one code's X and Z
   row spaces onto the other's (or onto its Z and X) preserves weights, so it
   maps the weight-8 vectors of one onto those of the other. By (1) these are
   the check rows, so it would be an isomorphism of the coloured Tanner
   graphs.
3. The canonical forms (nauty: B. D. McKay and A. Piperno, J. Symbolic
   Comput. 60 (2014) 94-112, arXiv:1301.1493) of the coloured Tanner
   graphs differ, with the X and Z checks in separate cells and with the
   cells swapped. A relabelled, row-shuffled copy of each code gives its
   original canonical form, as a control.

Reproducing this needs a Brouwer-Zimmermann low-weight enumerator and nauty;
it takes about 15 CPU-seconds per row space, about a CPU-minute in all.

Claim: d <= 34 (34 on both sides), a witness-backed upper bound
(confidence upper_bound). No lower bound is claimed; per-run results ranged
from 34 to 52, and the 34 came from one 1200 s run, so a lighter logical
may exist. The search covers odd and even weights.

## Reproduction

P = 83, n = 996. H_X[i*P + r, c*P + ((r + S_X[i][c]) mod P)] = 1 for every
block column c in X block row i (r = 0..82), and the same for H_Z with S_Z
('-' = empty cell):

S_X = [[-,-,-,-,33,82,69,60,51,18,29,68], [-,-,26,42,-,-,50,62,40,14,27,52], [34,53,-,-,-,-,55,55,51,21,17,50], [51,35,51,24,43,63,-,-,-,-,6,74], [3,69,65,49,66,76,-,-,53,59,-,-]]

S_Z = [[-,-,-,-,74,40,27,18,17,67,66,22], [-,-,61,77,-,-,2,14,81,55,59,1], [71,7,-,-,-,-,9,9,74,44,61,11], [77,61,67,40,58,78,-,-,-,-,25,10], [0,66,82,66,2,12,-,-,64,70,-,-]]

provenance.construction; every pair (a, b) satisfies

rank H_Z = 415, so k = 996 - 830 = 166. Tanner girth 6 on both sides.
