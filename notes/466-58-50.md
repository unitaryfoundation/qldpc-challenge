# [[466,58,50]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code at m = 233, row weight 14

At row weight 14 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 466` and `k >= 58` is 26, at `[[390,68,26]]` and row weight 13. This entry reaches 50. Allowing any row weight the best in the same range is 57, at `[[466,60,57]]` and row weight 20, which is six weight steps heavier.

## Direction & hypothesis

This cell was reached by looking for a threshold cliff rather than by sweeping a new modulus. `m = 233` had already been swept at the default information-set weight target, which produced the entries now on the board at `[[466,58]]` row weight 16 and 18 and `[[466,60]]` row weight 18 and 20. Domination requires a dominator's row weight to be lighter than or equal to the candidate's, so those entries defend nothing below row weight 16, and the board's bar at `[[466,58]]` falls from 52 at row weight 16 to 26 at row weight 14. A lighter draw there can lose a great deal of distance and still advance.

The lever is the information-set weight target, since the reachable row weight is `2(target + 1)`: the default target of 12 produces the row weights 16 to 20 already filed, and a target of 7 asks for 14. Searching the board for cells whose bar drops by 15 or more across four weight steps found nine, all of them occupied by entries from this same construction, and this is one of two that proved reachable and in the usable degree band.

## What was searched

`m = 233` is prime with `ord_2(233) = 29`, so `x^233 - 1` factors over GF(2) into nine cyclotomic cosets: one of size 1 and eight of size 29. Reachable divisor degrees are therefore `29j` and `29j + 1`, a very coarse set. The divisor here has degree 29, fixing `k = 58`, and `deg g / m` of 0.124 sits mid-band for this family, whose candidates all fall between 0.117 and 0.152.

The sweep ran at 20,000 information-set trials per draw at weight target 7. This draw came back with weight 7 in both blocks, so row weight 14, and screened at 66. The polynomial weights are both odd, so `(x + 1)` does not divide the common gcd.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, b, x^233 - 1) = 29`, and the same degree on each side alone, so the leak between the two single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

The three instruments disagreed and the claim is the minimum. A probe at 600,000 trials over 4 seeds read 58. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier's own refutation pass reaches, fell to 52. A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 trials per seed then settled at 50, two below the deep run, with per-seed readings from 50 to 58 and one of the 24 seeds reaching the minimum. The thin minimum is the reason to read 50 as an upper bound that may fall further; the margin over the weight-14 bar is wide enough that a correction would adjust the claim rather than remove the entry.

The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 29 whose zeros are the 204 residues the divisor omits. The longest run of consecutive residues among them is 26, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 27 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 66. `m` is prime, so the quotient fold has no proper divisor to act on; an orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than 176.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. The X witness of weight 50 is the operator the gate found; the Z witness is that operator carried across by the construction's own symmetry, since `H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]` and `H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]` means swapping the blocks and negating the index inside each maps one side's logicals onto the other's. It was validated against the opposite check matrix and the logical basis before being written, and both witnesses commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space.

## Dead ends

A cliff is necessary but not sufficient, and `m = 205` shows the failure mode. At `[[410,48]]` the bar falls from 30 at row weight 12 to 16 at row weight 10, a clean cliff, and degree 24 is reachable and in band. Swept at low targets it produced row weight 10 draws screening only 22 and 24, against the 30 the row weight 12 entry already holds. The distance lost nearly cancels the bar gained, leaving nothing after a typical fall, so that cell was dropped without a probe. The lighter draw has to hold its distance, which here it did: 66 at screen against merged entries at 52 to 57.

Lower targets at design points without a cliff were also measured and failed. At `m = 195` `[[390,54]]` a row weight 12 draw screened 40 and probed to 24 against a bar of 16, pricing at +8 where the row weight 18 draw prices at +10. At `m = 215` `[[430,56]]` a row weight 14 draw screened 54 and probed to 30 against a bar of 26, pricing at +4 where the row weight 16 draw prices at +15. At `m = 171` `[[342,40]]` and `[[342,42]]` the bar is 24 at every weight from 20 down to 16, so a lighter draw there gains nothing at all.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings, witness validation and the single-block searches come from this repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension. Divisor assembly, coset selection, the information-set draws, the orbit fold and the witness mirror are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite. Roughly five core-hours for the gate, two for the 8,000,000-trial run, and a few minutes each for the screen and the fold.

## Reproduction

`m = 233`, `n = 2m = 466`. With `a = {7, 20, 86, 139, 153, 195, 198}` and `b = {19, 87, 98, 148, 166, 187, 196}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The divisor is recovered as `gcd(a, b, x^233 - 1)`, of degree 29. The submitted `codes/466-58-50.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
