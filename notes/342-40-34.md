# [[342,40,34]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code at m = 171, row weight 16

At row weight 16 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 342` and `k >= 40` is 24, at `[[254,44,24]]` and row weight 16. This entry reaches 34, and since that same entry is also the best at any row weight in the range, it clears the unrestricted comparison too.

## Direction & hypothesis

`m = 171` had never been swept for this construction, and `n = 342` held three board entries, at `k = 6`, `k = 46` and `k = 76`, with distances 39, 3 and 4. Nothing sat at this dimension.

For a cyclic generalized bicycle on `x^m - 1` the dimension is `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so it can be set in advance: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree out of cyclotomic coset minimal polynomials and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Degree 20 at `m = 171` is `deg g / m` of 0.117, at the bottom of the band this family produces anything in; degrees above roughly 0.17 return distance 4 or 5 at any modulus.

## What was searched

`m = 171 = 3^2 x 19` with `ord_2(171) = 18`, so `x^171 - 1` factors over GF(2) into 12 cyclotomic cosets: one of size 1, one of size 2, one of size 6 and nine of size 18. Reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of those sizes, and the divisor here has degree 20, fixing `k = 40`.

Degrees were filtered before cores were spent: the degree must be a sum of coset sizes or no divisor exists, and the chosen exponents must not share a factor with `m` or the divisor sits inside `x^q - 1` for a proper divisor `q` and the codes fall below distance 4.

The sweep ran at 20,000 information-set trials per draw. This design point produced draws across row weights 14 through 24. Those are separate board cells rather than competitors, because domination needs the dominator to be no heavier and at least as far, so a lighter draw at a lower distance does not displace a heavier one at a higher distance. The polynomial weights here are 7 and 9, so `(x + 1)` does not divide the common gcd.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, b, x^171 - 1) = 20`, and the same degree on each side alone, so the leak between the two single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 34. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier's own refutation pass reaches, read 36, above the gate, so the gate is the binding instrument and the claim is 34.

The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 20 whose zeros are the 151 residues the divisor omits. The longest run of consecutive residues among them is 25, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 26 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 38. The quotient fold acts at `t = 3`, `9`, `19` and `57`; a fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than 48, so none of the four folds binds.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. The Z witness of weight 34 is the operator the gate found; the X witness is that operator carried across by the construction's own symmetry, since `H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)]` and `H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]` means swapping the blocks and negating the index inside each maps one side's logicals onto the other's. It was validated against the opposite check matrix and the logical basis before being written, and both witnesses commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space.

## Dead ends

A row weight 20 draw at this dimension reached 36 under both its gate and a 24-seed spread, and a row weight 16 draw at the neighbouring `[[342,42]]` reached the same 36. Neither dominates this entry and this entry dominates neither of them, since each is lighter or heavier without being at least as far.

The board's bar at `[[342,40]]` is 24 at row weight 20, 24 at weight 16 and 22 at weight 14, so dropping weight steps buys almost nothing here while the distance falls 36 to 34 to 32. At `[[342,42]]` the bar is 24 at every weight from 20 down to 16, so a lighter draw there is pure loss. Lighter is not automatically better in this family; it pays only where a strong entry already occupies the heavier weight and the bar collapses underneath it.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings, witness validation and the single-block searches come from this repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension. Divisor assembly, coset selection, the information-set draws, the orbit fold and the witness mirror are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite. Roughly two core-hours for the gate, five for the 8,000,000-trial run on a slower host, and a few minutes each for the screen and the fold.

## Reproduction

`m = 171`, `n = 2m = 342`. With `a = {81, 87, 99, 107, 110, 133, 140}` and `b = {16, 49, 55, 91, 98, 143, 146, 151, 162}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The divisor is recovered as `gcd(a, b, x^171 - 1)`, of degree 20. The submitted `codes/342-40-34.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
