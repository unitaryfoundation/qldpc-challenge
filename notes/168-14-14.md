# [[168,14,14]] weight-seven bivariate bicycle code

Submitted by Bryan Hooi (@bhooi).

## Direction and hypothesis

We searched for a binary CSS code on at most 168 data qubits encoding at least 14 logical qubits with maximum check weight seven. The challenge lists a weight-seven [[168,14,12]] code. Two commuting blocks of order 84 allow us to enforce the qubit and check-weight budgets before testing rank or distance.

## What was searched

The generating program sampled supports for two binary polynomials on products of cyclic groups of order 84. It rejected instances with fewer than 14 logical qubits and screened the survivors for light logical errors. The submitted instance came from the four-term and three-term split on the group of orders 14 and 6. In one 60-second sweep, 435 of 385,895 proposals passed the dimension filter. Three scored 14 and one scored 13. The reversed split produced two hits at 14 among 324 dimension-qualified proposals out of 288,573 samples. Two plain baseline runs also reached distance 14. The package contains one representative.

## Evidence trail

Both binary check matrices have rank 77. All 168 checks have weight seven, every qubit occurs in seven checks, and every X check commutes with every Z check. An independent exhaustive search ruled out X logicals of weight at most 13. It branches on an unsatisfied Z check, trying each available qubit in that check, and tests row-space membership when the syndrome vanishes. Translations preserve the check sets and act transitively in each block, so two fixed starting qubits suffice for a minimum logical. Group inversion combined with a block swap exchanges the X and Z check sets. This permutation was verified on the matrices, giving the same lower bound for Z. One zero-indexed X witness is {0,8,16,24,25,27,43,52,54,71,114,115,120,121}. Together these give exact distance 14 locally.

The challenge peer audit used the same random-information-set search on this candidate and the listed [[168,14,12]] code. With 2,000,000 trials, seed 51 and pair depth 10, the lightest witnesses found had weights 14 and 12, respectively. Both witnesses passed the audit. [Comparison entry](https://qecchallenge.com/codes/168-14-12.html)

The WL screen also flags the listed [[168,14,12]]. Each X and Z Tanner graph has 1,176 six-cycles here and 756 in that entry. A qubit and check permutation preserves those counts. More generally, the peer has a verified weight-12 logical, whereas our exhaustive search excludes every logical of weight at most 13. The counts can be reproduced by enumerating unordered triples of same-type checks that intersect pairwise in three distinct qubits. Within each side, any two different rows intersect in at most one qubit for both entries.

## Dead ends

In a 60-second four-plus-three sweep on the group of orders 12 and 7, 342 of 523,926 samples passed the dimension filter. All scored at most 12. Even in the successful four-plus-three sweep on the group of orders 14 and 6, 431 of 435 dimension-qualified proposals scored at most 12.

## Tools

DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017, with reasoning effort 50, proposed and ran the search program in our swarm math harness. The successful worker reported the result at minute 86 of a 120-minute shared run.

## Reproduction

Work over the binary group algebra of the product of the cyclic groups of orders 14 and 6. Number a group element (i,j) by 6i+j. Use these exponent pairs for the two supports:

- A = {(13,2), (2,4), (1,4), (3,5)}.
- B = {(2,4), (1,2), (4,3)}.

For each group element g, create one X row from g+A in the first block and g+B in the second. Create one Z row from g−B in the first block and g−A in the second. Reduce coordinates modulo 14 and 6 and add 84 to second-block indices. These supports reproduce the submitted matrices. Multiple search hits shared an output filename; the supports above were checked against the archived matrix.
