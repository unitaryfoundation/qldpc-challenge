# [[976,250,22]] — pair-partition CPM (3,8) code at the even lift P = 122, with k = 2P + 6

## Direction & hypothesis

Cell: CSS, unrestricted, weight-8, extended tier (n <= 1000 with w <= 8 and
d <= 40). In the (3,8) pair-partition CPM family of Okada and Kasai
(arXiv:2607.14091) n = 8P and, generically, k = 2P + 4, so at d = 22 the
score kd^2/n = d^2 (1/4 + 1/(2P)) falls below the cell's leader,
codes/904-230-22.json by @vprusso (P = 113, 123.14), for every larger lift.
Hypothesis: at even P the rank of H_X, H_Z can drop by a few more, giving
k > 2P + 4; at P = 122 two extra logical qubits (k = 250) with d = 22 give
250 * 22^2 / 976 = 123.98.

**Inspired by:** codes/904-230-22.json and codes/712-182-20.json by @vprusso
(found with Claude Fable 5.1): the (3,8) pattern at large lifts, the
matchings M0, M1, M2 used here, and the polishing of the 8-cycle count. Lifts
beyond the paper's P = 73 start with codes/632-162-18.json by @vprusso; the
girth-8 hard filter follows codes/808-206-20.json by @pandey-tushar. The
score formula and the lift cap at n <= 1000 are from
fieldnotes/2026-09-20-screening-traps-at-n-900.md by @e-eight.

**Technique (not ours):** the pair-partition construction, its design system
and the matchings are Okada and Kasai's (arXiv:2607.14091); quasi-cyclic CSS
LDPC codes from circulant permutation matrices go back to Hagiwara and Imai
(arXiv:quant-ph/0701020); @vprusso's entries also cite the lifted-product
construction of Panteleev and Kalachev (arXiv:2111.03654). The exact
distance search uses Okada and Kasai's `cpm_distance` tool
(github.com/kasaikenta/pair-partition-cpm-css-codes, MIT licence).

**Board precedents:** other (3,8) weight-8 entries of this family:
codes/776-198-20.json and codes/664-170-18.json by @vprusso,
codes/824-210-20.json, codes/792-202-20.json, codes/840-214-20.json,
codes/856-218-20.json, codes/872-222-18.json and codes/888-226-18.json by
@MathysRennela, codes/568-146-18.json by @nihoniumqc, and the published
codes/584-150-18.json (Okada and Kasai). Even lifts are already on the board:
P = 32 and P = 72 in codes/256-68-12.json and codes/576-148-18.json by
@MathysRennela (arrays printed in Maskara et al., arXiv:2610.06749), both at
k = 2P + 4. We found no (3,8) pair-partition CPM entry with k > 2P + 4 in the board's codes,
notes and fieldnotes; the wider literature was not searched. The rank of a
quasi-cyclic matrix is set by its common factors with x^P - 1, which has
repeated factors over GF(2) when P is even (x^122 - 1 = (x^61 - 1)^2), so the
extra logical qubits are not claimed as a new effect.

Our contribution: the observation, in our draws, that on Z_122 k depends on
the exponents mod 2; the choice of a parity class with k = 2P + 6; this draw;
and the distance screening below.

## What was searched

Rate probe: 2000 random girth-8 draws of the design system per lift
P = 104..125. Draws with k > 2P + 4 appeared on about 6 to 10% of draws at
even P, about 2% at P divisible by 3, and on none at prime P (nor at 115, 119, 121; 2 of 2000 at 125). Re-draws
that keep the free exponents' residues (mod 2 here) keep the extra k, so the
search fixed a residue class and annealed inside it (20,000 moves, fewer
8-cycles, girth 8 and a connected Tanner graph kept). 71 annealed codes with
extra k at P = 114..124; a fold filter of ours (quotient codes over every
proper subgroup, keep lightest fold >= 22) removed 32; a Stern screen,
20 s per side, on the other 39 read 16 (x12), 18 (x5), 20 (x20), 22 (x2).
Both 22s are at P = 122, in the same parity class; this entry is one of them.
The second was also put through the exact search below with the same
outcome and is not filed.

## Evidence trail

Lightest non-trivial logical found for the submitted code. Every witness
was checked in numpy: zero syndrome against the opposite checks, outside the
row space of its own checks.

| search, per side | method | lightest found |
|---|---|---|
| 20 s, then 240 s | Stern ISD | 22 |
| 2000000 trials (4 chunks, seeds 1117, 2001, 2002, 2003) | board gf2_fast RIS, pair depth 8 | 22 |
| 6 runs of 590 s, fresh seeds (about 70000 to 78000 iterations each) | Stern ISD | 22 |
| complete, 29 pieces | exact search, patched `cpm_distance`, weight <= 20 | none |

Exact search: `cpm_distance` enumerates connected supports with zero
syndrome, prunes with a packing lower bound and tests closed words against
the stabilizer span. Our copy has two small patches: (1) the quasi-cyclic root
reduction, which the original enables only for prime P, is enabled for
composite P (any lifted word can be cyclically shifted so that its smallest
index lies in the copy g = 0 of its base column; primality is not needed;
the original's max_weight < P guard is also dropped, and here 20 < 122);
(2) a split option divides the top-level branches of one root into
disjoint pieces. Roots 0 to 6 ran in 4 pieces each and root 7 whole; all 29
pieces per side finished with nothing found (3005 CPU-s X side, 4698 CPU-s
Z side). Every logical of this family has even weight (noted in
codes/904-230-22.json), so no logical of weight 21 exists either, and with
the weight-22 witnesses d = 22 if the tool and our patch are correct. This
is not a board certificate: confidence stays upper_bound. Known-answer
tests of the patched copy: the published d = 14 of the Okada-Kasai
instances qc_472_122_14 and qc_488_126_14 (X side, complete through 12,
weight 14 found); two of our own P = 122 codes with witnesses of weight 18
and 20 (complete through 16 and 18, words found at 18 and 20); on a
composite lift the reduced and the full-root searches agree; split pieces
add up to the unsplit run. Piece logs are
available on request.

Fold check: the quotient code over Z_61 (subgroup {0, 61}) has no logical of
weight <= 11 (same tool, complete) and its weight-12 words lift to
weight-24 logicals of this code, so fold words through that subgroup weigh
at least 24.

## Dead ends

The other 37 screened codes with extra k fell to 20 or below in the 20 s
Stern screen. A wider scan of residue classes at P = 100..124 (334 annealed
codes, k up to 2P + 20) found no further code reading 22: the classes with
larger k carry light structured words (weight 8 to 12) or light folds.

## Reproduction

P = 122. H_X[i*P + r, j*P + ((r - E_x[i][j]) mod P)] = 1 and the same for
H_Z with E_z (i in 0..2, j in 0..7, r in 0..121):

E_x = [[0, 73, 60, 121, 0, 121, 60, 73], [0, 10, 10, 94, 94, 104, 104, 0], [0, 53, 0, 103, 46, 53, 46, 103]]

E_z = [[0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0], [0, 113, 60, 29, 94, 39, 32, 103], [0, 23, 10, 45, 46, 117, 56, 73]]

The arrays satisfy the 36 pair equations mod 122 with the matchings of
arXiv:2607.14091; both Tanner graphs have girth 8; rank H_X = rank H_Z = 363,
so k = 976 - 726 = 250.
