# [[96,23,6]] — one additional logical qubit by check deletion

## Direction & hypothesis

Target: CSS, unrestricted connectivity, maximum check weight 8. Starting from
an existing sparse stabilizer code, remove one independent X generator to
increase k by one, then search both logical sectors for the resulting distance
loss. The parent is the board's [[96,22,6]] 2BGA code on SmallGroup(48,11),
reconstructed there from the Lin–Pryadko database. This submission concerns the
derivative; it makes no claim to the parent's construction or literature novelty.

## What was searched

144 single-row-deletion trials across 36 parent entries. The first 48 trials
used 12 parents with n=20–30; the next 96 used 24 parents with n=50–120.
Parents were selected from the stored-claim Pareto frontier with check weight
at most 8 and claimed distance 4–6. Each side was first reduced to an
independent subset of its original rows. Each parent received two X-row and
two Z-row deletions, with k recomputed exactly. These are trial counts, not a
claim that every resulting stabilizer group is inequivalent.

Screening used the repository submission builder with 200 randomized trials
per side. All trial documents and their witnesses were saved, and all 144
passed the trusted structural/witness verifier with refutation disabled.
Only three cases with a bound at least 3 remained provisionally nondominated;
these received 8,000 fresh trials per side and the full candidate gate.
One [[54,21,3]] case was an exact duplicate of the existing entry; a second
matched its parameters. The selected [[96,23,6]] derivative improves k over
its parent and has a previously unoccupied parameter triple in this checkout.

## Evidence trail

For this candidate, the 200-trial screen used seed 20261035 for X and
20261036 for Z and found weight-6 logicals on both sides. The 8,000-trial
confirmation used seeds 20361035 and 20361036 and again found 6 on both sides.
The lowest stored witness from each side was retained across these runs.
A further independent 100,000 trials per side at seeds 20461035 (X) and
20461036 (Z) again returned weight 6 on both sides, taking about 231 seconds.
The submitted witnesses remain the saved weight-6 confirmation witnesses.

The trusted candidate gate passed at seed 111883778: structure and witnesses
accepted; no lighter logical in 6,340 RIS trials; no exact fingerprint or WL
signature duplicate; board advancement in unrestricted weight-8, with gains
on k and n relative to dominated peers and no distance-only gain flag.
This comparison is relative to checkout 0ad745c011ce7393c921a39cf35fe760090222c4, not the entire literature.

Both d_X <= 6 and d_Z <= 6 are upper bounds. No lower-bound proof or exact
certificate is claimed for this code. Circuit-level distance and logical
error rates have not been measured.

## Dead ends

The first 48 trials yielded bounds of 1 (19 trials), 2 (23), or 3 (6).
The larger 96-trial screen yielded 1 (32), 2 (38), 3 (16), 4 (9), or 6 (1).
Deleting an independent stabilizer raises k but usually creates short logicals;
retaining the parent's claimed distance was rare in this pilot. The result
does not establish that any other deletion or construction family is exhausted.

## Reproduction

Use the parent from github.com/unitaryfoundation/qldpc-challenge at
0ad745c011ce7393c921a39cf35fe760090222c4, `codes/96-22-6.json`.
Its construction and literature attribution are documented in `notes/96-22-6.md`.
Convert each support list to a binary matrix with 96 columns. For each
matrix H, compute the pivot-column list p from the repository's GF(2) RREF of
H transpose, and retain H[p,:] in that order. This selects original sparse
rows; it does not replace the checks by potentially dense RREF rows.
Remove row 17 (zero-based) from the resulting X basis and keep the Z basis.
The resulting ranks are 36 and 37, hence k=96-36-37=23. CSS commutation is
preserved by taking subsets; all retained rows have weight at most 8.
Package with the repository submission builder at the seeds and trial counts
above, keeping the lower-weight saved witness on each side. The submitted
JSON contains both full support matrices and the final witnesses, so the
code can also be verified directly without rerunning the search.
